## ISSUES FOUND

**Issue 1:** Unattributed quantified statistic
**Location:** Script 10, body, paragraph 1: *"prefrontal activity decreases significantly"* and caption hook: *"Poor sleep cuts prefrontal cortex activity by up to 30%"*
**Problem:** The "up to 30%" figure appears in the caption hook and is implied in the script body. This is a specific quantified claim. If it derives from a real study (e.g., a specific neuroimaging paper), it needs to be attributed in the production notes so an editor can verify it. If it cannot be attributed, it must be reframed as approximate or directional ("significantly," "measurably," or with a qualifier like "research consistently shows reductions in the range of 20–30%"). As written, it is a precise unverifiable statistic. For NeuroForge, this is a credibility violation regardless of whether the finding is directionally true — the brand cannot publish specific percentages that cannot be sourced.
**Required fix:** Either (a) add the source citation to the production notes for this script, or (b) remove the percentage from the caption hook and replace with a non-quantified formulation that matches the script body language ("significantly," "measurably," etc.). Do not leave a specific percentage in the caption without a sourced equivalent in the notes.
**Severity:** Critical

## PASSED ELEMENTS

1. **Script 7's closing sequence** — *"You're not getting energy from caffeine. You're borrowing against tomorrow's sleep pressure. And interest accrues."* This is the best writing in the batch. It is precise, structurally satisfying (three short sentences with escalating weight), and genuinely memorable. The financial metaphor earns its place because it maps exactly onto the mechanism, not just the feeling.

2. **Script 12's precision on melatonin dosage** — The specific "0.5 milligrams" figure, delivered with instruction on timing and application, is exactly the kind of specificity that separates this content from generic sleep advice. It is also accurate, non-fabricated, and clinically defensible. This is the right way to use specificity as a credibility signal.

3. **Script 8's mechanism framing** — *"During REM, the amygdala is active, but norepinephrine is almost entirely suppressed. This creates a unique window where the brain can process emotionally loaded experiences without the physiological stress response."* This is a clean, accurate, and vivid explanation of a counterintuitive mechanism. It would hold up in a longer piece and works exceptionally well compressed to this length.

4. **Script 13's close** — *"The night you drink, you sleep. You just don't restore. That distinction costs more than most people realise."* Short, honest, and lands with weight. The contrast between the two sentences does the work without underlining it.

5. **Consistent absence of motivational language across all 14 scripts** — Not a single instance of "you've got this," "start your journey," "unlock your potential," or any equivalent. The content respects the audience's intelligence throughout. This is harder to maintain across a 14-script batch than it looks, and it was maintained.
