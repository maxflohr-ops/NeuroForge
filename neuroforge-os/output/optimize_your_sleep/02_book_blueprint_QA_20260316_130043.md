**Issue 2:** Three chapter titles label rather than pull
**Location:** Chapter 1: "What Is Actually Happening While You Sleep"; Chapter 12: "Building Your Sleep System — The Complete Protocol"; Chapter 13: "What Your Sleep Tracker Is Telling You (And What It's Getting Wrong)"
**Problem:** These titles describe the chapter's contents without making a claim or generating curiosity. They function as table-of-contents entries, not hooks. The blueprint's own checklist criterion is that "chapter titles could sell the book on their own." Compare these to Chapter 7 ("What Alcohol Actually Does to Your Brain While You Sleep") and Chapter 8 ("Why You Wake Up at 3am (And What to Do About It)") — both of which work because they name a specific, slightly uncomfortable truth. The three flagged titles do not.
**Required fix:**
- Chapter 1: Reframe to make a claim or surface a counterintuition. A direction: the revelation that sleep is an active, structured biological process — not rest — should be in the title. Something like *Sleep Is Not Rest* or *The Architecture Your Brain Runs Every Night* would signal the mechanism-first approach more sharply.
- Chapter 12: The word "complete" reads as a length promise rather than a value promise. The title should communicate what the system *does*, not that it is comprehensive. Direction: something that names the output the reader will have.
- Chapter 13: Functional but not compelling. The parenthetical is doing most of the work. Consider foregrounding the "orthosomnia" concept or the tracking paradox more directly — the most interesting idea in this chapter is that the tool you're using to fix your sleep may be making it worse.
**Severity:** Major

**Location:** Chapter 11 title: "The Single Most Powerful Thing You Can Do (It's Not What You Think)"
**Problem:** This construction — "the most powerful thing," with a parenthetical tease — reads as clickbait framing. If the Manuscript Agent executes the chapter without resolving this, the published chapter will either contain a misattributed citation or a fabricated one. This is a Red Flag category risk — not yet a Red Flag because the blueprint itself flags the issue, but it is one agent-execution step away from becoming one.

**Issue 8:** Chapter 9 exercise maps sleep architecture deficits to cognitive domains but the mapping has one imprecision
**Location:** Chapter 9 exercise: "This audit becomes the reader's 'why this matters to me' anchor" — specifically the parenthetical: "(REM loss: emotional regulation and memory; slow-wave loss: physical restoration and growth hormone; N2 loss: procedural memory and fine motor performance)"
**Problem:** Attributing procedural memory primarily to N2 is an oversimplification. Procedural memory consolidation has been linked to both N2 (sleep spindles) and slow-wave sleep, with some evidence that motor sequence learning specifically involves slow-wave sleep. The blueprint's assignment of "procedural memory and fine motor performance" exclusively to N2 loss could produce a chapter-level inaccuracy that a careful reader (exactly Dr. Hale's audience) would notice. Memory consolidation across sleep stages is an area where the research is nuanced, and the blueprint's voice profile specifies that Dr. Hale "earns credibility by acknowledging what the science does not yet know."
**Required fix:** Revise the parenthetical to reflect the distributed nature of procedural memory consolidation across N2 and slow-wave, or simplify to "N2 loss: fine motor sequencing and certain forms of procedural memory (evidence mixed with slow-wave)." The Manuscript Agent should be instructed to treat this with appropriate hedging.
**Severity:** Minor

### PASSED ELEMENTS

1. **The Two-System Diagnostic (Chapter 2) is an exceptionally well-designed chapter exercise.** The two-column format maps directly onto the chapter's core structure, produces a personalised output (which system is the primary problem), and creates a meaningful through-line to subsequent chapters. It does what blueprint exercises should do: generate data the reader uses later.

2. **Chapter transitions are consistently functional and mechanistically grounded.** Each transition names the specific mechanism that will be introduced next and explains *why* it is the logical next question. The transition from Chapter 4 to Chapter 5 ("Light controls *when* the circadian clock wants you to sleep. But there is a second environmental input that directly controls *whether* you can fall asleep at all — and it operates through a completely different mechanism") is a model for how to build chapter-to-chapter momentum without resorting to cliffhangers or hype.

3. **The recommended title rationale (Section 3) is the strongest section of the blueprint.** It correctly identifies what *Why You're Still Tired* does that competing titles don't, names the specific reader it is targeting (someone who has already read Walker and listened to Huberman), and explains why the word "precise" in the subtitle does specific credibility work. This level of marketing reasoning is rare in a blueprint document and directly useful to a human editor or publisher.

4. **The alcohol chapter (Chapter 7) handles a sensitive topic exactly as Dr. Hale's voice profile requires.** The explicit note — "Dr. Hale does not tell the reader not to drink. He gives them the mechanism and lets them decide" — is precisely the distinction between clinical precision and moral prescription that defines this faculty member's credibility. The GABA-A mechanism, the cortisol rebound timing, and the "deferred adenosine bill" framing in Chapter 2 are examples of analogy used correctly: to make a mechanism land, not to decorate.

5. **The transformation arc (Section 5) is behavioural and specific on both sides.** The "Before" states are not generic pain points — they name specific behaviours (weekend lie-ins, 3am waking, caffeine as primary performance input) and misattributions (assuming exhaustion is a fixed trait). The "After" states are not vague improvements — they name specific competencies the reader will have (can identify which sleep lever to apply, can read tracker data with calibrated scepticism, has implemented a specific anchor protocol). This is what a transformation arc should look like.

### PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** Unresolved research delegation — the blueprint contains multiple instances of "Manuscript Agent should verify/find/cite" instructions that embed citation risk directly into the writing agent's task. This is a systemic boundary failure between the Research Agent and the Book Architect Agent.

**Agent responsible:** Book Architect Agent (blueprint production) and Research Agent (source verification).

**Root cause:** The blueprint is being produced before all citations are fully verified. The Book Architect Agent is correctly identifying where citations are needed and which papers are likely candidates, but is passing unverified placeholders forward rather than holding the blueprint pending Research Agent confirmation.

**Recommended prompt change for Book Architect Agent:** Add the following constraint to the blueprint production instructions: *"Do not include any citation marked 'verify,' 'if applicable,' or 'Manuscript Agent should find' in the final blueprint. Every citation included in a blueprint must either (a) be a verified reference with full author, year, journal, volume, and page details confirmed by the Research Agent, or (b) be replaced with a bracketed instruction that says [CITATION REQUIRED — Research Agent to supply before Manuscript Agent execution] so the gap is explicit and trackable. Unverified citations are more dangerous than missing citations: a missing citation produces a gap; an unverified citation produces a fabrication risk."*

**Recommended prompt change for Research Agent:** Add the following output requirement: *"When delivering sources to the Book Architect Agent, mark each source as VERIFIED (full details confirmed) or CANDIDATE (likely relevant, details not confirmed). The Book Architect Agent will not include CANDIDATE sources in the final blueprint — they will be held for Research Agent verification before blueprint finalisation."*

This change closes the fabrication risk at the blueprint stage rather than relying on the Manuscript Agent to catch it downstream — which it will not reliably do.
