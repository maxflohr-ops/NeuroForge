### ISSUES FOUND

**Issue 1:** Unverified specific citation — potential fabricated reference
**Location:** Email 3, paragraph 4: *"A study out of Brookhaven National Laboratory (Volkow et al., 2012) measured dopamine receptor availability in the striatum and prefrontal cortex of participants after a night of total sleep deprivation. The finding was precise and significant: D2 and D3 receptor availability dropped measurably."*
**Problem:** Nora Volkow has published extensively on dopamine and addiction at Brookhaven, and research on dopamine receptor availability and sleep deprivation exists in the literature — but the specific combination of author, institution, year, and methodology as written here requires verification. NeuroForge's credibility standard is zero tolerance for imprecise or fabricated attribution. If this citation is inaccurate in any detail — wrong year, wrong institution, methodology mischaracterised — it is a Red Flag violation. The copy is also making a precise quantitative implication ("dropped measurably") without stating the actual finding, which is a form of overclaiming even if the citation is genuine.
**Required fix:** Verify this citation against the original paper before this email is cleared. Confirm author, year, institution, study design, and finding. If the study cannot be verified exactly as described, either (a) replace with the correct citation and finding, or (b) reframe the claim without a specific attribution: *"Research measuring dopamine receptor availability in the striatum and prefrontal cortex after sleep deprivation has found..."* — less precise, but credible. Do not publish this email with an unverified specific citation.
**Severity:** Critical

**Issue 2:** Van Dongen study — citation detail requires verification
**Location:** Email 4, paragraph 5: *"The Van Dongen study I cite in Chapter 9 of the book maps his situation with uncomfortable precision. Participants restricted to six hours per night for fourteen days showed cognitive performance equivalent to 24 hours of total sleep deprivation by day ten."*
**Problem:** The Van Dongen et al. (2003) sleep restriction study is real and well-documented, and the general finding described here is consistent with the published literature. However, the specific claim — "equivalent to 24 hours of total sleep deprivation by day ten" — is a precise quantitative statement that should be verified against the original paper before publication. The study's findings on psychomotor vigilance task performance are robust, but the exact equivalence threshold and the specific day-ten framing should be confirmed. A misquoted finding from a real study is still a credibility violation.
**Required fix:** Verify the specific "day ten / equivalent to 24 hours" framing against Van Dongen et al. (2003) or whichever study is being referenced. Adjust the stated figure if the original paper uses different parameters. If confirmed accurate, this is cleared with no further change needed.
**Severity:** Major

**Issue 3:** Google Ads — headline character counts unverified
**Location:** Section 4, all three Search Ads, Headline fields
**Problem:** Google Ads headlines have a 30-character limit. The following headlines are at risk of truncation and need to be counted precisely:
- "Fix Your Sleep Architecture" — 26 characters, within limit.
- "Not Habits — Mechanisms" — 22 characters, within limit.
- "Free Field Guide, No Opt-In Tricks" — 34 characters. **Over the 30-character limit by 4 characters.** This headline will be rejected or truncated by Google Ads and cannot run as written.
- "Still Tired After 8 Hours?" — 26 characters, within limit.

### PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** One critical and one major credibility issue, both involving specific citations with precise quantitative claims. The copy handles citations correctly at the conceptual level — it names researchers, institutions, findings — but has not been passed through a citation verification step before delivery.

**Producing agent:** Funnel Agent (and by extension, any agent producing manuscript or email content that includes research citations).

**Recommended prompt change:** Add a mandatory citation verification flag to the Funnel Agent and Manuscript Agent prompts. Specifically: *"Any citation that includes a named researcher, institution, year, and specific quantitative finding must be flagged with [VERIFY] before delivery. Do not present specific citations as confirmed unless they have been verified against a primary source. If a study supports a general point but the exact parameters cannot be confirmed, reframe as a general literature claim without specific attribution."* This prevents the failure mode where a real body of research is cited with invented specificity — the most credible-sounding and therefore most dangerous form of citation error.
