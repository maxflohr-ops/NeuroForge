### ISSUES FOUND

**Issue 1:** "Clinically grounded" in Subtitle Option 1 creates a credibility obligation the blueprint does not yet discharge
**Location:** Section 2, Subtitle Option 1 — *"A Clinically Grounded Guide to Breaking the Anxiety Loop and Reclaiming Your Mental Quiet"*
**Problem:** "Clinically grounded" is a specific credibility claim that will be read by readers and retailers as a signal that the content is evidenced-based and vetted. At blueprint stage, no credentials for Dr. Nova Vale are referenced anywhere in the document, and no clinical methodology is named. If this subtitle survives to publication and the book's claims cannot withstand scrutiny, it creates a reputational and potentially legal exposure for NeuroForge. This is especially sensitive given that the book touches on anxiety, rumination, and sleep — areas where overclaiming has caused harm to similar brands.
**Required fix:** Either (a) add a Faculty Credentials section to the blueprint confirming Dr. Nova Vale's qualifications that justify the "clinically grounded" claim, or (b) flag to the human editor that this subtitle should only be selected if credentials are on record. If credentials cannot be confirmed, remove "clinically grounded" from the subtitle shortlist. Do not pass this subtitle to the Manuscript Agent without resolution.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 2:** Kristin Neff named as researcher without characterisation guardrails for the Manuscript Agent
**Location:** Section 6, Chapter 11 — *"The research on self-compassion (Kristin Neff's framework, accessible without over-relying on citation)"*
**Problem:** Kristin Neff is a real researcher and her framework is well-documented, so this is not a fabrication risk. The risk is different: the parenthetical instruction to use her work "without over-relying on citation" is ambiguous. It could be interpreted by the Manuscript Agent as licence to paraphrase her framework loosely or attribute ideas to her without specific grounding — which, depending on execution, could result in misrepresentation of her work or a claim that reads as fabricated. The blueprint gives no instruction on which specific aspects of her framework to include or how to characterise it accurately.
**Required fix:** Replace the parenthetical with a specific instruction: name which element of Neff's framework is to be referenced (e.g., the three components of self-compassion — self-kindness, common humanity, mindfulness), confirm it is to be paraphrased accurately and attributed to her by name, and specify that no statistics or study findings are to be cited unless they can be independently verified. This protects against the Manuscript Agent making up supporting data.
**Severity:** Major

**Issue 3:** The Differentiation Note names a competing title without a sourced characterisation
**Location:** Section 10, Differentiation Note — *"Nick Trenton's Stop Overthinking... Trenton is list-heavy and tactical but thin on mechanism"*
**Problem:** Nick Trenton's *Stop Overthinking* is a real published book. Characterising it as "list-heavy and tactical but thin on mechanism" is a comparative claim that could be accurate or could be an AI-generated assumption. If this characterisation is inaccurate and the language migrates into any published copy (author note, media kit, pitch document), it is a reputational risk. The blueprint should not make confident claims about competitor content it may not have verified.
**Required fix:** Add a note that the Differentiation Note characterisations are hypotheses for the human editor to verify, not confirmed analysis. Flag that competitor characterisations must be reviewed against the actual texts before use in any external-facing document. This is a blueprint-level flag, not a failure condition.
**Severity:** Minor

The total target is 25,000–30,000 words. Without an Introduction word count, the Manuscript Agent cannot accurately scope the full manuscript or ensure the chapter targets are calibrated to the overall target. This is a production gap, not a content problem, but it will cause scoping friction at the next stage.
**Required fix:** Add a target word count range for the Introduction (suggested: 800–1,200 words, consistent with the stated intent and scene-setting function). Confirm the Conclusion target of under 500 words in the chapter outline table or a dedicated note. Add both to the Workbook/Exercise Map if appropriate — the Introduction does not have an exercise, which is correct and should be noted explicitly to avoid the Manuscript Agent feeling something is missing.
**Severity:** Minor

**Issue 5:** Chapter 7's "and then what?" technique references ACT without the same sourcing guidance applied to Chapter 6
**Location:** Section 6, Chapter 7 — *"The 'and then what?' technique: following a 'what if' scenario all the way through to its actual conclusion"*; compared with Chapter 6, which explicitly names Acceptance and Commitment Therapy as the source of defusion techniques
**Problem:** Chapter 6 correctly names ACT as the source of its defusion techniques and instructs the Manuscript Agent to explain them "without clinical jargon." Chapter 7 uses what appears to be a related CBT/ACT technique (commonly associated with "worry decatastrophising" in CBT literature) but does not attribute it or give the Manuscript Agent equivalent sourcing guardrails. If the Manuscript Agent names or implies a clinical source for this technique that turns out to be incorrect or misattributed, it creates a credibility problem. Inconsistency in sourcing instruction across chapters is a Manuscript Agent error risk.
**Required fix:** Add a note to Chapter 7's key points confirming the source of the "and then what?" technique (CBT decatastrophising or a specific ACT variant), with the same instruction applied in Chapter 6: explain the technique in plain terms, name the framework it comes from, do not fabricate supporting statistics.
**Severity:** Minor

**Issue 6:** "Postponement as a practical tool" appears in both Chapter 7 and is implied in Chapter 9 without a cross-reference instruction
**Location:** Section 6, Chapter 7 — *"Postponement as a practical tool: scheduling a specific 'worry window'"*; Chapter 9 — *"The scheduled worry period as a daytime intervention that reduces night-time processing"*
**Problem:** This is not a duplication failure — the two applications are meaningfully distinct (daytime postponement as acute management vs. daytime worry period as a night-time prevention strategy). However, without an explicit cross-reference instruction, the Manuscript Agent may either repeat the explanation fully in Chapter 9 (redundant) or assume the reader remembers Chapter 7 and under-explain it (confusing for readers who follow the non-linear reading path recommended in the Introduction). The blueprint's quality check confirms no chapter covers the same terrain, but this technique spans two chapters and needs handling guidance.
**Required fix:** Add a note to Chapter 9's key points: "The scheduled worry period was introduced in Chapter 7 as an acute management tool. In this chapter, it is reframed specifically as an upstream night-time prevention strategy. Do not re-explain the full mechanism — refer back briefly and then extend the application. Approximately one paragraph of re-orientation, not a full re-introduction."
**Severity:** Minor

### PASSED ELEMENTS

1. **The Transformation Arc (Section 5) is the strongest in any blueprint reviewed to date.** Every "before" and "after" statement describes a specific, observable behaviour — not an emotional state. "Stops reassurance-seeking in the acute moments that previously triggered it, using a concrete script" is the level of specificity the brand requires. No vague "feels calmer" language anywhere in the arc.

2. **The Chapter 9 neurology framing is precise and earns its credibility.** The explanation of why night-time overthinking is neurologically distinct — reduced prefrontal activity, elevated default mode network, threat detector remaining online — is accurate at a lay level, specific enough to be useful, and stops short of making clinical claims. This is exactly the "warm but clinical" register Dr. Nova Vale requires.

3. **The Workbook/Exercise Map (Section 9) is complete and correctly scoped.** Every exercise produces a named, tangible output. None requires more than the stated time. The progression from recognition tools (Ch. 1–2) to interrupt tools (Ch. 3–7) to integration (Ch. 8–12) is visible in the map and mirrors the transformation arc cleanly.

4. **The Introduction and Conclusion design is structurally sophisticated.** Opening with a scene in second person, closing by returning to that scene with a changed relationship to it — not a resolved one — is exactly right for this audience and this faculty member's voice. The explicit calibration of what the book will *not* deliver is a credibility decision that will earn more trust than it costs.

5. **The Differentiation Note (Section 10) makes a defensible and specific case.** Rather than generic "this book is different because it's better" language, it identifies two specific gaps in the competitive set (the physiological layer and the identity layer) and maps them precisely to chapters. This is usable for a pitch deck, a media kit, or an Amazon description with minimal editing.

### PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure observed:** The blueprint performs well on structure, voice, and transformation arc, but applies sourcing and attribution guardrails inconsistently across chapters. Chapter 6 correctly instructs the Manuscript Agent on how to handle ACT citations; Chapters 7 and 11 reference clinical techniques and named researchers without equivalent instruction. This is a systemic gap in how the Book Architect Agent handles sourcing — it flags clinical frameworks when they are the chapter's primary focus but does not apply the same discipline when frameworks appear as supporting points.

**Producing agent:** Book Architect Agent

**Recommended prompt change:** Add the following standing instruction to the Book Architect Agent prompt, under the Key Points section of the chapter outline format: *"If any key point references a named researcher, clinical framework, or specific psychological technique, the blueprint must include: (a) the name of the source framework or researcher, (b) confirmation that this attribution is accurate to the best of the agent's knowledge, and (c) a Manuscript Agent instruction specifying how to handle it — paraphrase only, attribution required, no statistics without verification, etc. This instruction applies equally whether the framework is the chapter's primary subject or a supporting reference."* This single addition would have prevented Issues 2 and 5 in this report.
