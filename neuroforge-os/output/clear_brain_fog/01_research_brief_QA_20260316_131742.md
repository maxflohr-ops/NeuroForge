### ISSUES FOUND

---

**Issue 1:** Unanchored quantitative claim — dehydration figure
**Location:** Section 5, Angle 9 ("Hydration and cognitive performance"); also repeated in Section 7, Hook 4 ("Mild dehydration — losing just 1% of your body weight in fluid...")
**Problem:** The claim that 1–2% body weight fluid loss measurably impairs working memory, attention, and processing speed is specific enough to read as a cited finding. This research does exist (Ganio et al., Armstrong et al.), but the brief provides no sourcing note for manuscript agents who will use this as a seed document. If a Manuscript Agent picks this up and cannot verify it, there is a real risk it gets either quietly fabricated-cited or stripped out entirely — both bad outcomes. The hook version repeats the "1%" figure specifically, compounding the risk.
**Required fix:** Add a sourcing note in parentheses after the claim in Angle 9, e.g. "(Source: verify against Ganio et al. or equivalent — do not use figure without confirmation)." Add the same note in the hook bank so the Manuscript Agent knows this is a number that requires verification, not a settled fact to drop in freely.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 2:** Unanchored quantitative claim — sedentary behaviour and cerebral blood flow
**Location:** Section 5, Angle 10 ("Sedentary behaviour and brain blood flow"); also Section 7, Hook 6
**Problem:** The claim that prolonged sitting reduces cerebral blood flow to the prefrontal cortex is referenced as a mechanism with the confidence of an established finding. Research in this space exists but is more contested and less settled than the brief implies — particularly the specificity of the prefrontal cortex claim. Hook 6 states this as flat fact: "You're not unfocused. You're just not moving enough." That is a strong, specific causal claim with no sourcing note. If this seeds a manuscript chapter and the Manuscript Agent treats it as confirmed science, it becomes a credibility liability.
**Required fix:** Add a sourcing note to Angle 10 flagging that the PFC-specificity claim requires verification before use in manuscript. Soften the certainty level in the hook bank entry, or add a note to the hook that it requires a sourced mechanism in the script or chapter body before it can be published.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 3:** Near-duplicate chapter angles — Angles 1 and 4
**Location:** Section 5, Angle 1 ("What brain fog actually is at the neurological level — neuroinflammation, impaired glymphatic clearance, dysregulated neurotransmitter signalling") and Angle 4 ("Neuroinflammation as a driver of fog — chronic low-grade inflammation")
**Problem:** Angle 1 already names neuroinflammation as one of the three core mechanisms of brain fog. Angle 4 then dedicates an entire chapter angle to neuroinflammation specifically. If both are used in a book blueprint, the result will either be a chapter that repeats content from the overview chapter, or an overview chapter that cannibalises what should be Angle 4's standalone value. The Book Architect Agent needs to receive a clean brief where each angle is genuinely distinct.
**Required fix:** Either (a) remove neuroinflammation from Angle 1's mechanism list so it remains truly introductory, and let Angle 4 own the mechanism in full depth; or (b) reframe Angle 4 so it is clearly about the lifestyle inputs that drive neuroinflammation — the dietary, behavioural, and environmental levers — rather than the mechanism itself, which Angle 1 already introduces. Option (b) is stronger.
**Severity:** Minor (but must be resolved before passing to Book Architect)

**Issue 4:** Motivational drift in two hooks — voice consistency risk
**Location:** Section 7, Hook 14 ("You're not lazy. You're not burned out. You're not getting dumber. Your brain is responding rationally to the exact conditions you're giving it. Change the conditions.") and Hook 15 ("That's your default. Brain fog is the deviation. Here's what's causing it.")
**Problem:** Hook 14 in particular reads like therapeutic reassurance, which is a warmer register than Dr. Orion Hale consistently occupies. "You're not lazy. You're not burned out." is the kind of affirmation-adjacent copy that belongs to a wellness brand, not a mechanism-first neuroscience educator. Hale's voice is precise and clinical — he names mechanisms, not feelings. Hook 15 is borderline; "that's your default" is fine, but the framing is closer to motivational content than neuroscience framing. These are not automatic fails, but they introduce the risk of tonal drift in downstream scripts or copy that draws from these hooks.
**Required fix:** Revise Hook 14 to reframe the reassurance as a mechanistic observation rather than an emotional reassurance — e.g., shifting from "You're not lazy" (emotional absolution) to something that names the biological state creating the experience. Hook 15 is acceptable with minor tightening. Flag both in the hook bank as requiring voice review before use in short-form scripts.
**Severity:** Minor

**Issue 5:** Monetisation section — vague deliverable in mid-ticket offer

**Severity:** Minor

### PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** Two unanchored quantitative claims in the chapter angles and hook bank. The brief correctly avoided fabricated citations, but did not flag specific numerical claims as requiring source verification before downstream use. The self-check in Section 10 confirms "no fake citations" — but the risk is not fabrication at the brief stage; it is that specific numbers with no sourcing note get treated as verified facts by the Manuscript Agent.

**Producing agent:** Research Agent

**Recommended prompt change:** Add the following instruction to the Research Brief prompt under Section 5 (Chapter Angles) and Section 7 (Hooks):

*"If any angle or hook includes a specific quantitative claim (percentages, measurements, named thresholds), append a sourcing note in brackets: either the study name/author to verify against, or the flag '[VERIFY BEFORE USE — unconfirmed figure]'. Do not present specific numbers as established facts without sourcing guidance. The manuscript agent will treat unseeded numbers as verified; the brief must not create that false confidence."*

This single addition would prevent the Issue 1 and Issue 2 failures in this brief and in any future brief that includes mechanism-level numerical claims.
