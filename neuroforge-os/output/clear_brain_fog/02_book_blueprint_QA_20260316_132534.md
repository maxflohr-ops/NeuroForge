**Issue 2:** Potentially Inaccurate Citation Instruction — Chapter 9
**Location:** Chapter 9, first Key Point bullet, source note — *"Wheeler et al., 2020, Journal of Applied Physiology"*
**Problem:** The blueprint's own source note flags uncertainty here: *"Manuscript Agent must locate specifically"* — which is appropriate. However, the note also names several other citations (Tari et al., 2022; Hiden et al.; Hamer & Sharma, 2021) that appear uncertain, with one name possibly misspelled ("Hiden" does not correspond to a known author on this topic; the relevant researcher in cerebrovascular blood flow and sedentary behaviour literature may be Hieda or a different author entirely). Passing a citation instruction with a likely misspelled author name to the Manuscript Agent creates a high-probability fabricated or misattributed citation — a Red Flag category risk.
**Required fix:** The Book Architect Agent must verify or remove the "Hiden et al." reference before passing this blueprint. Replace with a confirmed citation or replace the source note with: *"Manuscript Agent: locate primary literature on prolonged sitting and middle cerebral artery blood flow velocity — do not cite without verification."* Do not pass a potentially misspelled author name to the Manuscript Agent as a citation instruction.
**Severity:** Critical

**Issue 3:** Overstatement Risk in Chapter 8 — Dehydration-Sleep Comparison Claim
**Location:** Chapter 8, second Key Point bullet — *"with impairments comparable in magnitude to a night of poor sleep"*
**Problem:** The comparison of mild dehydration impairment to a night of poor sleep is a strong claim that requires careful qualification. The cited studies (Ganio et al., 2011; Armstrong et al., 2012) demonstrate measurable cognitive impairment at 1–2% dehydration, but effect sizes vary substantially by task type, individual, and hydration method used in the study. "Comparable in magnitude to a night of poor sleep" is a rhetorical compression that risks overclaiming — which is a direct violation of Dr. Hale's voice profile ("fake precision" is explicitly listed under what he avoids). If the Manuscript Agent reproduces this framing without qualification, it becomes a credibility issue in published content.
**Required fix:** Revise the instruction to: *"Multiple controlled studies demonstrate that 1–2% dehydration produces measurable cognitive impairment in healthy adults — on some task types, effect sizes are substantial. Avoid the direct comparison to sleep deprivation unless a specific source supports this comparison explicitly; present the finding on its own terms."* The point is strong enough without the overreach.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 4:** Chapter 9 Source Note Instructs Citation of a Secondary Source
**Location:** Chapter 9, BDNF bullet, source note — *"Cotman & Berchtold, 2002, Trends in Neurosciences"*
**Problem:** This is not a flaw in the citation itself — Cotman & Berchtold (2002) is a legitimate and frequently cited review. The issue is that it is a review article, and the Manuscript Agent should be instructed to treat it as a secondary source pointing to primary literature rather than as the primary evidence. Given Dr. Hale's voice profile (no wellness blog; credentialled claims), citing a 2002 review as the sole support for the BDNF-exercise connection is thin when substantially more recent primary literature exists (e.g., Erickson et al., 2011, PNAS on hippocampal volume and exercise). This is a minor credibility risk but consistent with Dr. Hale's standard.
**Required fix:** Add to the source note: *"Cotman & Berchtold, 2002 is an appropriate anchor citation; Manuscript Agent should supplement with more recent primary literature on acute BDNF response to exercise — Erickson et al., 2011, PNAS is a well-supported option but must be verified before use."*
**Severity:** Minor

**Issue 5:** Chapter 10 — Dopamine Citation Epistemic Risk
**Location:** Chapter 10, dopaminergic reward loop bullet, source note — *"Schultz et al., 1997, Science — Manuscript Agent must verify relevance of this citation to phone behaviour specifically; frame with appropriate epistemic care"*
**Problem:** The blueprint correctly flags this, but the flag may not be strong enough to prevent misuse. Schultz et al., 1997 established the neural basis of prediction error and dopamine response in reward learning — in primates. The leap from this to "social media depletes the dopamine system's ability to sustain engagement with deep work" is a mechanistic extrapolation that the research does not directly support, and stating it in a neuroscience book under Dr. Hale's name without qualification would be a credibility violation. The mechanism is plausible, but the specific Schultz citation does not support the specific claim as written.
**Required fix:** Revise the claim framing in the Key Points instruction to: *"Explain that habitual variable-reward checking behaviour has been associated with attentional fragmentation and reduced tolerance for low-stimulation tasks — frame this as a behavioural pattern with a plausible dopaminergic mechanism rather than an established causal finding. Do not cite Schultz et al. as support for phone behaviour specifically; use with appropriate epistemic hedging or locate more directly relevant behavioural research (e.g., research on smartphone use and attentional capacity, or Alter, 2017, Irresistible as a secondary reference to direct primary research)."*
**Severity:** Major

**Issue 6:** Chapter 11 — Medical Claim Boundary Requires Explicit Guard
**Location:** Chapter 11, Key Points — blood test guidance and the Deficiency Risk Assessment tool
**Problem:** The blueprint handles this carefully — "The tool explicitly does not diagnose — it generates the right questions to bring to a clinical conversation" — and the instruction to "recommend supplementation only when deficiency is indicated by testing, not prophylactically" is correct. However, the Key Points instruct the Manuscript Agent to "provide a brief guide to interpreting results relative to optimal cognitive function ranges (which often differ from clinical deficiency thresholds)." This instruction, without an explicit guard, risks the Manuscript Agent producing content that tells readers their lab result means something specific — which is clinical advice. The blueprint does not instruct the agent to position this guidance carefully enough.
**Required fix:** Add explicit instruction to the Key Points: *"Manuscript Agent must not present specific numerical ranges as prescriptive clinical targets — present the distinction between clinical deficiency thresholds and ranges associated with optimal cognitive function in the research literature as a topic to raise with a clinician, not as self-diagnostic criteria. Language must make clear this is context for a conversation with a doctor, not a basis for self-diagnosis or self-treatment."*
**Severity:** Major

These transitions should be passed to the Manuscript Agent as models.

It is specific, mechanistically grounded, and counterintuitive in exactly the right way for Dr. Hale's voice.

The tone anchor sentence is well chosen and correctly identifies the three-beat rhythm that should govern every chapter.

5. **Epistemic self-regulation is consistently demonstrated throughout.** The blueprint repeatedly instructs the Manuscript Agent to verify citations, flag where evidence comes from animal models, note where supplement evidence is limited to deficient populations, and distinguish association from causation. This is a genuine strength — the blueprint is actively protecting Dr. Hale's credibility rather than assuming the Manuscript Agent will do so unprompted.

### PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure observed:** The blueprint contains three instances of citation instructions that are either unverifiable as written ("Hiden et al."), insufficiently guarded against misapplication (Schultz et al. to phone behaviour), or compressed into overstatements the cited sources do not fully support (dehydration-sleep comparison). In each case, the blueprint's own source notes flag uncertainty — but the flag language is not always strong enough to prevent the Manuscript Agent from using the citation anyway with the problematic framing intact.

**Producing agent:** Book Architect Agent

**Recommended prompt change:** Add the following rule to the Book Architect Agent's citation instruction protocol:

*"When you are uncertain whether a citation supports the specific claim being made, do not include the citation with a 'verify this' flag — instead, write the source note as: 'Manuscript Agent: DO NOT CITE until verified. The claim being made is [X]. Locate a primary source that directly supports this specific claim. If no such source exists, revise the claim to reflect what the evidence actually shows.' A 'verify this' flag is insufficient protection — it permits the Manuscript Agent to cite speculatively. A 'do not cite until verified' instruction prevents it."*

This single change would have caught Issues 2, 3, and 5 before they reached QA.
