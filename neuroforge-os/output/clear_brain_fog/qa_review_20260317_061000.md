**Issue 2:** Script 15 — Clinical proximity risk on brain fog / depression overlap
**Location:** Script 15, paragraphs 2–3: *"Elevated inflammatory markers have been found in people with both major depression and in people experiencing chronic cognitive fog without a mood disorder diagnosis"* and *"it reflects measurable similarities in the inflammatory and neurotransmitter profiles of both states."*
**Problem:** This script is operating in close proximity to clinical diagnosis territory. The claim that brain fog and depression share "measurable similarities in neurotransmitter profiles" is stated as established fact. The neurotransmitter overlap claim in particular is less settled than the neuroinflammation literature and risks overstating the science. The disclaimer in paragraph 4 ("This does not mean brain fog is depression, or that lifestyle changes are a substitute for clinical treatment") is present and correct in instinct, but it comes after the clinical-adjacent claims rather than structuring them. If a viewer shares this script without the disclaimer, or the clip is edited, the framing could be read as diagnostic. NeuroForge's credibility standard and safety policy both require this to be handled with more precision.
**Required fix:** (1) Soften the neurotransmitter overlap claim to reflect its actual evidential status — "some research suggests overlapping inflammatory profiles" rather than stating shared neurotransmitter profiles as established fact. (2) Move the clinical caveat earlier in the script — before the mechanistic claims, not after them. (3) Remove or substantially qualify "regardless of which label you're using" — this phrasing implies interchangeability between brain fog and depression that the science does not support and that could discourage someone from seeking clinical help.
**Severity:** Critical

**Issue 3:** Script 10 — Credibility of citation language
**Location:** Script 10, paragraph 3: *"This is not a wellness talking point. It is a documented, replicable effect across multiple independent studies."*
**Problem:** The phrase "multiple independent studies" is invoked to establish authority but no study, researcher, or institution is named or gestured toward. On its own in a script this is acceptable. The problem is the framing *"This is not a wellness talking point"* actively positions the claim against scepticism and therefore raises the credibility bar for itself. Having raised that bar, the vague citation doesn't clear it. A knowledgeable viewer who knows the hydration-cognition literature is actually mixed in terms of effect size will notice. The 1% dehydration threshold and specific cognitive effects (working memory, attention, processing speed) are also stated with more precision than the literature uniformly supports.
**Required fix:** Remove or replace *"It is a documented, replicable effect across multiple independent studies"* with a more precise qualifier such as *"Research in this area consistently shows measurable effects on working memory and attention"* — this maintains credibility without overclaiming. If the production team can identify the specific research (Ganio et al. 2011 is the most-cited study on mild dehydration and cognition), a brief on-screen citation reference would strengthen the script considerably.
**Severity:** Major

**Issue 4:** Repeated engine analogy across scripts
**Location:** Script 1, paragraph 4: *"Think of a high-performance engine running on degraded fuel at the wrong temperature."* Script 14, final paragraph: *"Layering a compound on top of an unfixed system is like adding high-quality oil to an engine running at the wrong temperature."*
**Problem:** The same central metaphor appears in two different scripts in the same batch. For a viewer who watches both — which is the intended outcome of a content series — this reads as repetitive and slightly undermines the sense that each script is a complete, distinct piece. It also weakens Dr. Hale's voice, which should be characterised by precision and freshness rather than recycled metaphor.
**Required fix:** Replace the engine analogy in either Script 1 or Script 14. Script 14 is the better candidate for replacement since the engine analogy already appears in Script 1 and the "wrong temperature" image is already established there. The Manuscript Agent should identify a fresh analogy for Script 14 that preserves the upstream/downstream logic.
**Severity:** Minor

**Issue 5:** Scripts 5 and 13 — Overlap in core argument
**Location:** Script 5 topic: "Sleep duration vs. sleep architecture — why hours are the wrong metric." Script 13 topic: "Sleep architecture as the real determinant of next-day cognition."
**Problem:** Both scripts lead with the same reframe (duration is the wrong metric; architecture is what matters) and both name the same disruptors (alcohol, inconsistent sleep timing, elevated cortisol). Script 13 adds the glymphatic and REM functional distinction more explicitly, but a viewer who watches both will recognise they have received the same core argument twice in different packaging. In a short-form series, this erodes trust that each script delivers distinct value.
**Required fix:** Differentiate the scripts more sharply at the concept level, not just the framing level. Script 5 could focus exclusively on the causes of architectural disruption (what you're doing tonight that degrades tomorrow). Script 13 could focus on what each stage actually does — the specific cognitive functions of deep vs. REM sleep — without re-making the duration argument. The duration reframe should live in one script only.
**Severity:** Minor

## PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** Two issues recur across this batch that suggest gaps in the Shorts Script Agent's current constraints.

**Failure 1 — Incomplete batch delivery.** The agent produced 15 of 20 promised scripts and no promised supporting assets (quote graphics, carousels). This suggests the agent is either hitting a context limit and submitting without flagging the gap, or the output specification is not explicitly requiring a completeness check before submission.

**Producing agent:** Shorts Script Agent

**Recommended prompt fix:** Add an explicit completion gate to the Shorts Script Agent prompt: *"Before submitting any content batch, confirm that all promised scripts, graphics, and supporting assets are present. If any element is missing, flag it explicitly in the submission header rather than submitting a partial batch without notation."* This is a single instruction that would have prevented the most critical issue in this submission.

**Failure 2 — Clinical adjacency handling.** Script 15 handles the brain fog / depression overlap without sufficient guardrails, demonstrating that the agent does not have a clear internal rule for where mechanism explanation ends and clinical territory begins. The disclaimer was present but structurally misplaced — suggesting the agent knows the rule exists but applies it after the fact rather than structurally.

**Recommended prompt fix:** Add to the Shorts Script Agent's safety constraints: *"When any script touches mental health conditions (depression, anxiety, ADHD, OCD, or any diagnosed disorder), the clinical boundary must be established in the first reference to that condition, not after the mechanistic claims have been made. The framing must clearly separate biological mechanisms (acceptable) from clinical equivalence or treatment substitution (not acceptable). If in doubt, remove the clinical reference entirely and restrict the script to the non-clinical mechanism."*
