### ISSUES FOUND

**Issue 1:** Unanchored meta-analysis citation
**Location:** Email 2, paragraph beginning "A later meta-analysis confirmed..."
**Problem:** This is a credibility-critical issue. The Duval and Wicklund attribution is plausible and the 1970s date is roughly accurate for their objectivity theory work. The "later meta-analysis" that "confirmed" this in social contexts is not named, not dated, and not attributed. If this was generated by a content agent drawing on training data, there is a meaningful probability it does not exist as described or that it conflates multiple findings. The brand's credibility depends on not publishing research claims that cannot be traced. A careful reader — or a journalist writing a critical piece — will ask for the citation. It must be verifiable.
**Required fix:** Either identify and name the specific meta-analysis (author, year, journal) and verify it exists and says what is claimed — or rewrite the paragraph to remove the meta-analysis reference and ground the claim in the Duval and Wicklund work alone, which is verifiable. Do not publish an unanchored "later meta-analysis confirmed" claim.
**Severity:** Critical

---

**Issue 2:** Unverifiable reader-behaviour claim for an unpublished book
**Location:** Thank You Page, Objection Handler 3 ("I don't have time to read a whole book right now"), final sentence: "Most readers report that Chapter 1 alone changed something about how they showed up the following week."
**Problem:** This is a social proof claim about reader outcomes, and it cannot be true if the book has not yet been published and distributed to readers. Even if beta readers exist, "most readers report" is a statistically implied majority claim that requires real data from a meaningful sample. Publishing this on a live funnel for a book at launch constitutes a false claim. This is both a credibility violation and a potential legal exposure point.
**Required fix:** Remove this sentence entirely, or replace it with a framing that does not imply aggregated reader data — for example: "The framework in Chapter 1 is designed to produce a single, usable shift in how you direct your attention in conversation. Most readers will know within the first sitting whether it's landed." This makes a capability claim about the content, not a false behavioural claim about a reader population that does not yet exist.
**Severity:** Critical

---

**Issue 3:** "Polyvagal nervous system response" invoked as credential, not explanation
**Location:** Thank You Page, Book Description, paragraph 3: "...explains the polyvagal nervous system response that makes cognitive reframing useless when you're actually in a threat state..."
**Problem:** This is a specificity failure adjacent to a credibility issue. "Polyvagal nervous system response" is used here as a technical-sounding phrase to add scientific weight — but it is not explained, and the claim attached to it ("makes cognitive reframing useless") is stated as absolute fact without qualification. Polyvagal theory, associated with Stephen Porges, is legitimate but also contested in some of its specific claims. Using it as an unexamined authority-signal while making a strong adjacent claim ("useless") is the kind of thing a scientifically literate reader will notice and distrust. Luna Hart's voice is clinically grounded but intellectually honest — overclaiming in the name of science is off-brand.
**Required fix:** Either name the theory properly ("Stephen Porges' polyvagal framework") and soften the adjacent claim ("significantly harder to override" rather than "useless") — or replace the technical name with a plain-language description of the mechanism that doesn't require the credential to do the work. The sentence "when your body is in a threat response, no amount of reframing can override it — because they're not in the same system" (from Objection Handler 2) actually handles this better without the jargon — consider using that formulation here too.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 4:** Email 2 explicitly names the gap in the send sequence
**Location:** Email 2, closing section: "Tomorrow is a rest day in your inbox. On Day 4, I'll send you something..."
**Problem:** Naming a deliberate gap in the email sequence as "a rest day" is a self-conscious production decision being made visible to the reader. It is not harmful per se, but it draws attention to the fact that this is a managed sequence — which is precisely what good email nurture sequences are designed not to feel like. Luna Hart's voice throughout is intimate and direct, as if writing to one person. "Tomorrow is a rest day in your inbox" is a sequence architect speaking, not Luna.
**Required fix:** Remove the sentence "Tomorrow is a rest day in your inbox." Replace the transition with a version that simply looks forward to Day 4's content without flagging the gap: "On Thursday, I'll send you something I think will genuinely reframe how you see this problem — a counterintuitive idea about what charisma actually is that most people never hear." This achieves the forward pull without breaking the intimacy of the voice.
**Severity:** Minor

**Issue 5:** Section 5 (Internal Quality Checklist) must be removed before production
**Location:** Section 5, entire section
**Problem:** This section is an internal production document — a self-audit checklist completed by the producing agent. It must not exist in any version of this document that goes to a designer, publisher, ad platform, or external collaborator. It is not funnel copy. If it were accidentally included in a file sent to a designer or uploaded to a landing page builder, it would expose internal workflow logic and brand process documentation to outside parties.
**Required fix:** Strip Section 5 entirely from the production-ready version of this document. It may be retained in the internal draft as a production record, but the production build must not include it.
**Severity:** Major (production risk, not copy quality risk)

---

**Issue 6:** "For a limited time, it's available at the introductory price of $17. That price will go up."
**Location:** Thank You Page, Book Description, closing two sentences
**Problem:** This is not a Red Flag — the price increase is described elsewhere in the funnel as genuinely tied to a full launch date, which makes it honest urgency rather than false scarcity. However, as it stands here, the claim is made without the qualifying context that appears in Email 5 ("as we move toward the full launch"). In isolation, "that price will go up" reads as a vague urgency gesture rather than a specific, credible condition. A reader who sees only the Thank You Page has no frame for why or when.
**Required fix:** Add a brief qualifying clause: "That price will increase at the full launch" — or "until the launch date in [month]" if the date is confirmed. Anchoring the price change to a real, named condition converts a vague pressure signal into an honest, credible one. Consistency with Email 5's framing is required.
**Severity:** Minor

### PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** Two of the six issues flagged are credibility issues rooted in the same underlying problem — unverified empirical claims presented with false confidence. The unanchored meta-analysis and the unverifiable "most readers report" claim are both instances of a content agent using research-language framing without access to, or verification of, the underlying evidence.

**Producing agent:** Funnel Agent

**Recommended prompt addition:**

Add the following constraint to the Funnel Agent prompt, under a new heading **CITATION AND CLAIM RULES**:

> Any claim that invokes a specific study, named researcher, or statistical finding must be flagged with [VERIFY: source] in the draft. Do not present these claims as confirmed. Any claim about reader or customer behaviour (e.g. "most readers report," "users find that") may not be included for products that have not yet been publicly released. If social proof copy is required before a product launch, use forward-looking capability framing ("designed to produce X") rather than retrospective outcome framing ("readers report X"). The QA Agent will fail any submission containing an unanchored research claim or a pre-launch behavioural statistic.

This single addition would have prevented both Critical-severity issues in this submission.
