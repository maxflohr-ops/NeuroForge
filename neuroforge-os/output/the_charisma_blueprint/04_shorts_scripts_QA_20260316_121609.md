## ISSUES FOUND

---

**Issue 1:** Mirror neuron claim stated as settled mechanism
**Location:** Script 6, paragraph 2 — *"the mechanism is your mirror neuron system. Human brains are wired to read and replicate the physiological state of the people around them."*
**Problem:** The mirror neuron theory of emotional contagion is genuinely contested. The original Rizzolatti research was conducted on macaques; the direct human homologue remains debated, and the claim that mirror neurons are specifically *the mechanism* behind emotional contagion is not scientific consensus. As written, Luna is presenting a contested hypothesis as established fact. If a credentialed viewer challenges this, the brand absorbs the credibility hit. The broader claim — that nervous system states are contagious and measurable — is well-supported. The specific mechanism attribution is the problem.
**Required fix:** Remove or soften the mirror neuron attribution. Replace with a mechanism description that the evidence supports without controversy. Example direction: *"The mechanism is social neuroscience's best-supported finding — human nervous systems are wired to read and entrain to the physiological state of the people around them, faster than conscious thought."* The underlying point holds without the contested specificity.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 2:** Physiological claim stated as established fact
**Location:** Script 10, paragraph 4 — *"Cold water on your face or wrists before you walk in creates a measurable vagal response."*
**Problem:** The diving reflex (cold water on face) does produce a parasympathetic response and is reasonably well-supported. The wrists claim is where this gets shakier — the wrist application is a popular wellness recommendation but does not have the same physiological evidence base as the facial diving reflex. Presenting both with equal confidence and the qualifier "measurable" makes this a credibility risk. If a reader or reviewer checks this, the wrist claim does not hold to the same standard as the face claim. For a brand built on credibility, conflating a well-evidenced mechanism with a wellness-culture assumption is a problem.
**Required fix:** Either remove the wrist specification, or add a light qualifier that separates the two. Example direction: *"Cold water on your face activates a measurable parasympathetic response — your body's own brake. The same logic applies to cooling your pulse points, though the effect is less dramatic."* Alternatively, cut the wrists reference entirely and keep only the face application.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 3:** Potential medical/clinical scope concern
**Location:** Script 10 throughout — topic is social anxiety; specific physiological interventions are described as treatment-adjacent approaches
**Problem:** Script 10 addresses social anxiety and describes physiological techniques as things that "actually reach" the nervous system in ways cognitive reframing cannot. This is not a medical claim in the strict sense — Luna is not diagnosing or prescribing — but the framing is close enough to clinical territory that it warrants a qualified eye. The language "social anxiety can't be thought your way out of" followed by specific physiological protocols reads, to a careful reader, as therapeutic instruction rather than general self-improvement content. This is a gray zone, not a Red Flag, but it needs a line of qualification — Luna is speaking from a psychology/coaching perspective, not a clinical one — to maintain defensible positioning.
**Required fix:** Add one brief qualifier in the script that frames this as general self-improvement rather than clinical intervention. Example: something as light as "These aren't medical interventions — but they are the inputs your nervous system actually responds to" establishes the correct lane without undermining the content's authority.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 4:** Formatting gap — missing hashtag label
**Location:** Script 15, post-body section — hashtags are present but the **Hashtags:** label is absent before the tag block
**Problem:** Minor production inconsistency. All other scripts use the **Hashtags:** label; Script 15 omits it. Creates a small formatting irregularity that a designer or social media manager would need to resolve.
**Required fix:** Add the **Hashtags:** label before the hashtag block in Script 15 to match all other scripts in the batch.
**Severity:** Minor

---

**Issue 5:** Script 15 hook is the weakest entry point in the batch
**Location:** Script 15, hook — *"Understanding what makes connection work doesn't make it less real. It makes the things that were blocking it stop getting in the way."*
**Problem:** This hook opens by naming a concern the viewer may not have brought to the video. It begins defensively — answering an objection before establishing that the viewer holds the objection. The hooks that perform best in this batch (Scripts 7, 3, 11) either name a specific internal experience the viewer recognises immediately, or make a counterintuitive claim that creates immediate tension. Script 15's hook does neither. It starts on a conditional frame that requires the viewer to locate themselves in it before the video earns their attention. On a platform where the hook decides whether the viewer stays past second two, this is a structural risk.
**Required fix:** Reframe the hook to open on an experience or claim rather than an objection-response. The content of the script is strong — the entry point is the problem. Direction: something that names the moment a person feels like they're "engineering" connection — the uncomfortable feeling that studying social skills makes them feel fake — rather than opening with the reassurance before the discomfort has been established.
**Severity:** Minor

## PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** The credibility issues in this batch are not fabrications — they are precision failures. The agent selected real mechanisms (emotional contagion, vagal response) and stated them with more certainty than the evidence warrants, or with a specificity that exceeds what the cited mechanism can support. The mirror neuron attribution is the clearest example: the underlying claim is defensible; the specific mechanism named is not.

**Producing agent:** Shorts Script Agent

**Recommended prompt addition:** Add the following instruction to the Shorts Script Agent prompt under the Credibility section:

*"When citing a psychological or physiological mechanism, apply a one-sentence internal check before finalising: 'Is this the established consensus, or is this one model among several?' If the latter, either soften the attribution ('one mechanism that may explain this is...') or describe the effect without naming the mechanism. The effect of emotional contagion is well-evidenced; the mirror neuron explanation for it is not. State what is known; hedge what is theorised. NeuroForge's credibility depends on this distinction."*

This single addition would have caught both Issues 1 and 2 in this batch and would prevent the same class of error recurring across future mechanism-led scripts.
