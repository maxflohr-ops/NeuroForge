### ISSUES FOUND

**Issue 1:** Unattributed clinical concept presented as established fact
**Location:** Chapter 3, Core Idea — *"The fawn response (appease, placate, shrink to preserve safety) is as involuntary as fight or flight, and equally trainable once it's been identified."*
**Problem:** The "fawn response" as a distinct fourth trauma response is a concept associated specifically with Pete Walker's work on complex PTSD, and its clinical status is contested — it is not a universally recognised neurological category in the way fight, flight, and freeze are. Presenting it as equivalent to fight/flight without attribution or a credibility qualifier risks a Credibility Red Flag if the manuscript later presents this as peer-reviewed neuroscience. At blueprint stage this is a Major issue because it will shape how the chapter is written, and if the manuscript agent writes it as established neurological fact, it will need to be redone. The word "biological" in the Chapter 3 title — *"The Fawn Response — What Your Nervous System Does Before You've Decided"* — amplifies the risk.
**Required fix:** Add a credibility framing note in Chapter 3's Core Idea section. The chapter should be instructed to present the fawn response as a widely used conceptual framework for describing a recognisable pattern of appeasement behaviour under threat — crediting Pete Walker or popularising authors where appropriate — rather than as established neuroscience equivalent in status to the fight/flight/freeze literature. The chapter title should be reviewed to ensure "Your Nervous System" language doesn't overclaim the biological basis.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 2:** Attachment style claims require clinical boundary note
**Location:** Chapter 2, Key Points — *"Attachment style as hidden architecture: how anxious, avoidant, and disorganised attachment patterns manifest specifically as confidence problems in adult relationships (without requiring the reader to complete an attachment deep-dive — this is contextual, not clinical)"*
**Problem:** The parenthetical "(without requiring the reader to complete an attachment deep-dive — this is contextual, not clinical)" is a blueprint-level note, not instruction to the manuscript agent about how to handle the clinical boundary in the actual text. Attachment theory in adult relationships intersects with clinical territory — attachment disorders and trauma — and the manuscript agent needs an explicit instruction that the chapter must present attachment styles as a *framing tool*, not a diagnostic framework, and must not present any attachment style as a predictor of specific psychological outcomes. The existing note is reassuring to a human reader but will not reliably constrain the manuscript agent's output.
**Required fix:** Add an explicit credibility instruction to Chapter 2's Key Points section: *"This section must frame attachment styles as conceptual language for recognising patterns, not as clinical diagnoses. The chapter must not state or imply that anxious, avoidant, or disorganised attachment 'causes' specific outcomes. Language should be observational and tentative throughout this section."*
**Severity:** Major

**Issue 3:** Banned word in Subtitle 2
**Location:** Section 2, Subtitle Options — *"The Relational Guide to Building Self-Trust, Setting **Limits**, and Reclaiming Your Voice"*
**Problem:** "Reclaiming Your Voice" is not itself a banned phrase, but reviewing the subtitle more carefully: "Setting Limits" appears here as a subtitle option. This is not the issue. The issue is **"Reclaiming Your Voice"** — this is a motivational cliché that sits in the same register as banned phrases ("unlock," "empower") and would read as generic self-help copy. It undercuts the specificity and differentiation Luna Hart's brand depends on, and is incongruous with the precision visible in the rest of the blueprint. It also does not appear in the body of the book — no chapter promises to "reclaim your voice," which makes it doubly unsupported.
**Required fix:** Replace "Reclaiming Your Voice" in Subtitle 2 with language that is specific to the book's actual content — for example: *"The Relational Guide to Building Self-Trust, Setting Limits, and Expressing What You Actually Think"* or *"The Relational Guide to Building Self-Trust, Setting Limits, and Saying What You Mean."* The replacement should be concrete and behaviour-specific, not metaphorical.
**Severity:** Minor

**Issue 4:** Tonal drift — analytical register inconsistent with Hart voice
**Location:** Section 10 (Differentiation Note), final paragraph — *"The Luna Hart voice — warm but precise, emotionally intelligent but never vague — makes this the book that reads like a knowledgeable friend who has thought very carefully about your specific problem, rather than a theory text or a motivational guide."*
**Problem:** This sentence describes the Luna Hart voice from the outside rather than inhabiting it. The Differentiation Note as a whole is written in a pitch-deck register — accurate and well-argued, but noticeably cooler and more analytical than the rest of the blueprint. This is not a Red Flag (Differentiation Notes are structural, not reader-facing), but it is a tonal inconsistency that could mislead the manuscript agent about what register the book should be written in if they are using the blueprint as a voice reference. The problem is specifically in the final paragraph of the Differentiation Note, where self-description tips into self-promotion.
**Required fix:** The Differentiation Note is a structural section and does not need to be in Hart's voice. However, add a note at the top of the Differentiation Note section clarifying: *"[Internal reference only — not reader-facing. Voice reference for manuscript agent: see Chapter Promise language throughout, which reflects the correct register for the written text.]"* This prevents the Differentiation Note from being read as a voice model.
**Severity:** Minor

**Issue 5:** Exercise mismatch in Workbook/Exercise Map
**Location:** Section 9, Workbook/Exercise Map, Chapter 10 row — *"A five-instance tracking log of compliment receipt without deflection"*
**Problem:** The Chapter 10 exercise description in the body of the blueprint (Section 6, Chapter 10) states: *"Readers track five instances over one week."* The Workbook/Exercise Map correctly reflects this. However, reading the full Chapter 10 exercise description closely: the exercise asks readers to track instances and note *"what the deflect impulse was, what they did instead, and how it felt"* — three tracking fields. The Workbook/Exercise Map summarises this as producing *"the practice of letting positive information land"* — which is accurate as far as it goes, but omits that the exercise also produces a *personalised deflection pattern inventory* (what the specific impulse was). This omission is minor but means the map does not accurately represent what the exercise produces, which matters because the map is cited as a reader-facing quick reference in the Back Matter plan.
**Required fix:** Update the Chapter 10 row in the Workbook/Exercise Map to read: *"A five-instance tracking log of compliment receipt without deflection, producing a personal record of the specific deflection impulse and the experience of overriding it."*
**Severity:** Minor

**Issue 6:** Chapter 11 professional support note needs stronger instruction
**Location:** Chapter 11, Key Points — *"A note on professional support: named clearly and warmly — some of this work requires the specific conditions that therapy provides, and that is not a failure of the reader or of this book. The frameworks here are complementary to, not a replacement for, that work."*
**Problem:** Chapter 11 addresses confidence erosion in potentially abusive relational dynamics. The blueprint appropriately flags that professional support should be named, and the framing instruction is warm and appropriate. However, "named clearly and warmly" is a tone instruction, not a content boundary instruction. The manuscript agent writing this chapter will be handling material that could shade into descriptions of coercive control, gaslighting, and emotional abuse — all of which carry specific clinical and legal dimensions. The existing instruction does not tell the agent what *not* to do: specifically, the chapter must not attempt to diagnose the relationship type, must not use clinical language (coercive control, narcissistic abuse, DARVO) without appropriate framing, and must not position the book's tools as sufficient for readers who are still inside an unsafe relationship dynamic.
**Required fix:** Add a content boundary instruction to Chapter 11's Key Points: *"The manuscript agent must not use clinical diagnostic language (coercive control, narcissistic abuse, gaslighting as a clinical term) without appropriate qualification. The chapter must not imply that the tools in this book are sufficient for readers who are currently in an unsafe or actively harmful relationship — the professional support note must make the specific recommendation to seek qualified support in that context, not simply offer it as an optional add-on. The chapter must not attempt to help the reader determine whether their relationship was abusive — that categorisation is outside the scope of this book."*
**Severity:** Critical

### PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure observed:** The Book Blueprint Agent produced structurally excellent content but did not include sufficient credibility boundary instructions for chapters that handle clinical or semi-clinical material. The fawn response chapter and the Chapter 11 erosive-relationship chapter both required post-hoc credibility framing that should have been built into the blueprint at generation time. This is a systematic gap, not a one-off error — any blueprint touching trauma, attachment, the nervous system, or relational harm will have the same vulnerability.

**Producing agent:** Book Blueprint Agent

**Recommended prompt addition:** Add the following instruction to the Book Blueprint Agent's chapter outline generation rules:

*"For any chapter that references clinical psychology concepts (nervous system responses, attachment styles, trauma responses, therapeutic frameworks), include an explicit credibility boundary note in the Key Points section. This note must specify: (a) how the concept should be framed — as a conceptual tool or as established clinical science; (b) what diagnostic or causal claims the manuscript agent must not make; and (c) whether a professional support recommendation is required, and if so, what it must say. Do not assume that a general 'this is contextual, not clinical' parenthetical is sufficient — it will not constrain the manuscript agent reliably."*
