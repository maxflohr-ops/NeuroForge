## ISSUES FOUND

---

**Issue 1:** Internal self-check section included in deliverable package
**Location:** Section 5 — "Funnel Copy Quality Check" — entire section
**Problem:** This section is a producing agent's self-assessment checklist. It is not copy. If this file goes to a designer or is uploaded to an email platform as written, it will either appear in production output or cause confusion about what is and isn't live content. It also creates a false sense of completion — a checklist saying "no banned language" does not substitute for QA review, and its presence in the package creates ambiguity about whether QA has already occurred.
**Required fix:** Strip Section 5 entirely from the client-facing or handoff package. If it is useful as an internal record, it should be filed separately, clearly labelled as a pre-submission internal note, and not included in the content package that crosses to design or production.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 2:** Testimonial placeholders are not actionable collection briefs
**Location:** Section 1 — Social Proof Placeholder — all three testimonial slots
**Problem:** The placeholders contain guidance on what themes to look for, which is useful, but they do not name who is responsible for collection, what the collection method is, what the deadline is, or what format the final testimonial should take. As written, a human editor receiving this file cannot action the collection — they have a description of what's wanted, not a brief. This is a production readiness gap, not a copy failure, but it will stall the handoff.
**Required fix:** Each testimonial placeholder should be converted to a brief that names: (1) who owns the collection task, (2) the collection method (outreach to existing customers, survey, etc.), (3) the word count target, and (4) the approval step before it goes live. The thematic guidance already written is good and should be retained as part of the brief.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 3:** URL placeholder appears three times without tracking parameter guidance
**Location:** Email 3 body — "[LANDING_PAGE_URL]"; Email 4 body — "[LANDING_PAGE_URL]"; Email 5 body — "[LANDING_PAGE_URL]"
**Problem:** Three separate email links pointing to the same URL placeholder. There is no note indicating whether these should carry UTM parameters (source, medium, campaign) for attribution. If the links go live without tracking parameters, performance data for the email sequence will be unattributable by email — a significant analytical gap for a funnel that will inform future optimisation.
**Required fix:** Add a production note at each [LANDING_PAGE_URL] instance, or in a header note at the top of Section 3, specifying: (a) the base URL to be inserted, and (b) the UTM parameter structure to be applied per email (e.g., utm_source=email / utm_medium=welcome_sequence / utm_campaign=confidence_code / utm_content=email3). If NeuroForge does not use UTM tracking, this should be stated explicitly as a known gap.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 4:** Unattributed performance claim presented as fact in a production document
**Location:** Section 1 — Testimonial 3 placeholder — "Testimonials that name a specific outcome ('I caught myself about to apologise and stopped') outperform general praise by a significant margin"
**Problem:** This is presented as a factual claim inside what is otherwise a content package. It may be accurate, but "by a significant margin" is unsourced. If a human editor reads this and passes it through without checking context, there is a small but real risk it surfaces somewhere it shouldn't — or is cited as NeuroForge's own research claim. More practically: it reads like the producing agent editorialising inside a client deliverable, which is unprofessional presentation.
**Required fix:** Reframe this sentence as a production rationale rather than a factual claim. Replace "outperform general praise by a significant margin" with "tend to convert better than general praise" or simply delete the performance claim and leave the guidance: "Look for testimonials that name a specific moment or outcome rather than general praise."
**Severity:** Minor

---

**Issue 5:** Ad 3 Headline 3 underperforms relative to the rest of the ad copy
**Location:** Section 4 — Search Ad 3 — "Headline 3: Free Guide From Luna Hart"
**Problem:** Headline 3 in Ad 3 is a name drop where the other two headlines are idea-led. For a cold search audience targeting "how to stop needing approval" or "attachment style confidence," Luna Hart is not yet a recognisable name — using it as the third headline wastes the slot. The other ads' Headline 3 slots carry a specific benefit or reframe ("Name It. Then Change It." and "Free Assessment: Find Yours"). This one abandons the pattern without sufficient reason.
**Required fix:** Replace "Free Guide From Luna Hart" with a headline that carries a specific benefit or reframe relevant to the targeting keywords. Suggestion direction (not a rewrite): something that speaks to the why-doesn't-generic-advice-work angle already in the ad body, or names the specific mechanism (the approval loop, the fawn response). Luna Hart attribution can be handled in the description line or on the landing page.
**Severity:** Minor

---

**Issue 6:** "Introductory price" claim appears twice with no supporting context
**Location:** Thank You Page — Book Description — "This is an introductory price"; Email 4 — "That's an introductory price, and it reflects how I want people to access this work"
**Problem:** The claim is handled honestly relative to the Red Flag threshold — there is no fake countdown, no false scarcity. However, "introductory price" implies a future price increase. If the price does not increase, this becomes a credibility problem over time, particularly for readers who purchase later and find the price unchanged. It also appears twice in the same funnel, which doubles the exposure to that risk.
**Required fix:** Either (a) confirm that a price increase is planned and document the new price point so the copy stays accurate after the change, or (b) replace "introductory price" with neutral framing such as "The book is $17" without the introductory qualifier. If the introductory framing is retained, add a production note confirming the planned price and the date of the change, so the copy can be updated accordingly.
**Severity:** Minor

## PASSED ELEMENTS

**1. The approval-seeking-as-anxiety-management reframe in Email 3 is the strongest piece of intellectual content in the package.**
The loop is explained in plain terms (internal uncertainty → temporary relief → no structural update → dependency increases), the mechanism is named without jargon, and the pivot to the structural change ("building an internal reference point") is precise and usable. This is the kind of insight that earns the brand the "genuinely different" positioning it is asking testimonials to confirm. It will do more trust-building work than any social proof placeholder.

**2. The three-to-five second window framing in Email 2 is a specific, memorable mechanism that the audience has not read elsewhere in this form.**
"The capitulation has already happened three seconds before you got there" directly addresses why standard assertiveness advice fails — and does so in a way that is both technically defensible (fawn response as pre-conscious threat response) and immediately recognisable to the target reader. The one-breath intervention is appropriately modest for an email ask, which is exactly right.

**3. The objection handlers on the thank you page are unusually good for this content category.**
All three objections are named accurately (the reader who won't finish, the reader who's already tried, the reader with no time), and each handler addresses the real objection rather than deflecting it. "This book works at the level of mechanism, not motivation" is a clean, credible differentiator that respects the reader's prior experience rather than dismissing it.

**4. The email sequence architecture earns the right to sell before it does.**
Emails 1 and 2 deliver standalone value without any commercial ask. The offer in Email 3 is a soft mention at the end of a substantive email, not a pivot to selling. The direct offer in Email 4 leads with a specific reader story before naming the product. Email 5 is notably restrained — "I'm not going to make this awkward" is the correct register for a final nudge to an audience that has not converted after four emails. This is a sequence that builds trust as designed.

**5. The banned language check is clean throughout the entire package.**
"Transformative," "unlock," "game-changer," "life-changing," "transform your life," "journey," and "empower" do not appear in any of the five sections. The motivational register is absent throughout — the copy stays in the specific, behavioural, mechanistic register that is both Luna Hart's voice and NeuroForge's brand standard. This is not trivial across a package of this length.

## PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** The producing agent included its own quality self-check (Section 5) as an embedded section within the client-facing deliverable package. This is a structural error — a conflation of the agent's internal verification process with the content output itself. It appeared in a complete, well-formatted package, suggesting the agent has a self-check routine but no instruction that the self-check output should be withheld from the final deliverable.

**Producing agent:** Funnel Agent

**Recommended prompt change:** Add the following instruction to the Funnel Agent's output formatting rules: *"Do not include any internal quality check, self-assessment, or checklist in the final content package. If you perform a pre-submission check, record your notes in a separate internal log section clearly labelled [INTERNAL — NOT FOR HANDOFF] and confirm in your submission header that a check was completed. The content package delivered to QA should contain only live copy, production notes addressed to designers or platform operators, and clearly labelled placeholder instructions."*

**Secondary note:** The testimonial placeholder format — which describes what is wanted but does not assign ownership or method — recurred across all three slots. A prompt addition directing the agent to include a minimum collection brief (owner, method, deadline, format) alongside any placeholder would resolve this without requiring a QA flag in future cycles.
