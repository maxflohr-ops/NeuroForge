### ISSUES FOUND

**Issue 1:** Overclaiming in the "Why the Standard Advice Doesn't Work Here" section
**Location:** Section "Why the Standard Advice Doesn't Work Here," paragraphs on affirmations, power poses, and "fake it till you make it"
**Problem:** Three consecutive claims are structured as definitive statements — "Affirmations do not work because...," "Power poses do not work because...," "'Fake it till you make it' does not work because..." — with the reasoning given as established fact. The power pose literature in particular is actively contested (the original Carney/Cuddy findings have faced significant replication challenges, but so has the dismissal of them). Stating that these approaches definitively do not work, and explaining precisely why, without any epistemic hedging, puts a claim in Luna Hart's voice that could be challenged by a reader familiar with the literature or by a journalist. This is a credibility risk, not merely a precision issue. The argument the section is making — that these tools address the wrong level of the problem — is actually sound and persuasive. It does not require the absolute framing to land. The absolutism weakens it by making it falsifiable on the wrong grounds.
**Required fix:** Soften the framing from "X does not work" to "X does not address this problem" or "X wasn't designed for this." The argument is about fit-to-problem, not about the tools being universally ineffective. Reframe all three paragraphs around that distinction. Example reframe: "Affirmations aren't failing you because you're doing them wrong — they're addressing a different problem than the one you're dealing with here." This preserves the argument, removes the overclaim, and is actually a stronger rhetorical position.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 2:** Chapter ending partially signals the next chapter rather than earning the close
**Location:** Final paragraph, last two sentences — "The person you become around other people was shaped by something, which means the shape can change. To understand how that shaping happened, we need to go back a little further — to the original relationships where you first learned whether it was safe to be fully yourself."
**Problem:** The final two sentences function as a chapter preview rather than a chapter close. "To understand how that shaping happened, we need to go back a little further" is a table-of-contents sentence — it tells the reader what is coming rather than leaving them with something resonant from this chapter. The sentence before it ("it makes it workable") is the actual close — clean, earned, and thematically coherent with the chapter's central argument. The preview sentence weakens it by pivoting away from the insight just landed.
**Required fix:** Cut the final sentence entirely: "To understand how that shaping happened, we need to go back a little further — to the original relationships where you first learned whether it was safe to be fully yourself." End on "The person you become around other people was shaped by something, which means the shape can change" — or, if a transitional thread to Chapter 2 is editorially required, move it to a brief italicised chapter-end note rather than embedding it in the close. The close itself should land, not point forward.
**Severity:** Minor

**Issue 3:** One passage risks slight tonal drift toward clinical register
**Location:** Section "Where Confidence Actually Goes," paragraph beginning "This is not weakness. It is a learned response to a specific relational environment..."
**Problem:** This is a minor flag. The phrase "learned response to a specific relational environment" briefly shifts into a clinical/therapeutic register that is slightly cooler and more diagnostic than the surrounding voice. Luna Hart's voice, as established throughout this chapter, sits in the space between intelligent friend and practitioner — it does not tip fully into clinical language. This phrase tips. It is the only moment in the chapter where this happens, which is why it is a minor rather than a major flag. ### PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** The primary failure in this chapter — overclaiming in the "standard advice doesn't work" section — reflects a recurring risk in Manuscript Agent output when the chapter's argument involves contrasting the faculty member's approach with the existing literature. The agent tends to frame the contrast as "X does not work" rather than "X does not address this specific problem," because the stronger rhetorical move feels more persuasive at sentence level. This is a local optimisation that creates a global credibility risk.

**Producing agent:** Manuscript Agent

**Recommended prompt addition:** Add the following instruction to the Manuscript Agent's credibility constraints section: *"When a chapter argues that a conventional approach is insufficient or misdirected, the framing must be fit-to-problem, not universal efficacy. Write 'this approach doesn't address the problem you are dealing with here' rather than 'this approach does not work.' The faculty member is not claiming the tool is globally ineffective — they are claiming it is the wrong tool for this reader's specific situation. This distinction protects credibility and is usually a stronger argument."*
