## ISSUES FOUND

---

**Issue 1:** Overclaim on attachment research
**Location:** Script 9, paragraph 3 — *"Attachment research is clear on this"*
**Problem:** This asserts scientific consensus without naming any researcher, study, or theoretical framework. "Clear on this" is a strong claim. Attachment theory is well-established, but the specific mechanism being described — that childhood templates "run silently behind adult relationships" — is a simplified extrapolation from attachment theory, not a direct finding. As written, this phrase could be read as citing a body of research in support of a specific claim the research doesn't directly make in those terms. This is a credibility risk for NeuroForge. If a viewer with any psychology background reads it, it registers as vague authority-borrowing.
**Required fix:** Remove or soften the framing. Replace "Attachment research is clear on this" with something like "There's a well-established idea in developmental psychology that..." — or drop the research appeal entirely and let Luna state it as her framing rather than as scientific consensus. Do not add a specific citation that wasn't in the original.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 2:** Quasi-clinical term — "hypervigilance"
**Location:** Carousel 3, Slide 4 — *"sometimes it's hypervigilance — monitoring others' moods to stay ahead of conflict"*
**Problem:** "Hypervigilance" is a clinical term with specific diagnostic associations — it appears in the DSM criteria for PTSD and C-PTSD. Using it in a carousel without clinical context implies a diagnostic framing that NeuroForge content must not make. A reader with a trauma history could read this and take it as a soft diagnosis or as confirmation of a clinical condition. This is a Safety red flag risk — it doesn't quite trigger automatic fail because it's used descriptively rather than diagnostically, but it is close enough to require a fix before publication.
**Required fix:** Replace "hypervigilance" with a behavioural description that makes the same point without the clinical term. Suggested direction: "constant mood-monitoring — scanning for shifts in other people's emotional state to stay ahead of conflict." Same meaning. No clinical baggage.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 3:** Tonal inconsistency — "compliance with good PR"
**Location:** Script 13, hook — *"you don't have confidence. You have compliance with good PR"*
**Problem:** This is the only moment in the batch where the voice tips into clever-sharp rather than warm-clear. Luna Hart's established voice in this batch is intimate, direct, and psychologically grounded — she never deploys wit at the audience's expense. "Compliance with good PR" is a sharp line that works intellectually but reads as slightly superior in register — it sounds more like a brand copywriter than Luna. The visual direction even flags this: "deliver it calmly, not triumphantly" — which suggests the production team already sensed the risk. The body of Script 13 is strong and fully on-voice. It's this one line.
**Required fix:** Revise the hook so the reframe is delivered with Luna's characteristic directness but without the rhetorical flourish. Suggested direction: make the hook name the experience rather than the label. The body of the script ("confidence-shaped behaviour performed in favourable conditions") is actually a stronger and more on-voice version of the same idea — pull from that register.
**Severity:** Minor

**Issue 4:** Word count verification required — four scripts may exceed the 150-word limit for 60-second scripts
**Location:** Scripts 3, 4, 9, and 20 (all listed at 50–55 seconds)
**Problem:** The system prompt specifies "body is under 150 words for a 60-second script." Scripts listed at 55 seconds are pushing toward that limit. Scripts 3, 4, 9, and 20 read long in delivery — particularly Script 9, which includes a multi-clause explanatory section on attachment templates. If these exceed 150 words at natural delivery pace, they will run over time in production and require on-set editing. This is a production readiness issue, not a content issue — but it needs to be confirmed before handoff.
**Required fix:** Word-count each of these four scripts. If any exceeds 150 words, identify the section that can be trimmed (typically the mechanism-explanation section is where bloat occurs) and flag for the human editor with a suggested cut point.
**Severity:** Minor

---

**Issue 5:** Duplicate angle — Scripts 2 and 10 cover the same core mechanism
**Location:** Script 2 (over-apologising as confidence leak) and Script 10 (self-signalling mechanism of habitual apology)
**Problem:** Both scripts address over-apologising. Script 2 frames it as a signal and offers the "sorry → thank you" swap. Script 10 frames it as self-signalling and offers the pause practice. The angles are genuinely distinct — Script 2 is behavioural, Script 10 is mechanistic — so this does not constitute the same idea repeated. However, in a batch of 20 scripts intended for sequential publishing, running these in proximity will read as repetition to regular followers. This is a scheduling note, not a quality failure.
**Required fix:** Flag for the human editor and content scheduler: Scripts 2 and 10 should not be published in the same week. Recommend at least a two-week gap between them.
**Severity:** Minor

---

**Issue 6:** Carousel 4, Slide 6 — logical tension in the framing
**Location:** Carousel 4, Slide 6 — *"And if it does go badly — that's data too. Not proof you were right to stay silent forever. Data about this relationship, this person, right now."*
**Problem:** This slide is doing something useful — pre-empting the reader's fear that the conversation will confirm their worst fears — but the framing is underdeveloped at carousel length. "Data about this relationship, this person, right now" implies that a bad outcome is purely informational and actionable — but for readers who have experienced genuine relational harm, this may read as dismissive of real risk. Luna's voice elsewhere in the batch is careful to validate the nervous system's logic before redirecting it. This slide skips the validation step and goes straight to reframe, which is slightly out of register for her.
**Required fix:** Add one brief acknowledgment before the reframe — something that validates that a bad outcome is genuinely hard, before framing it as data. This doesn't need to be long. One sentence. The slide as written risks feeling glib to readers with real relationship history.
**Severity:** Minor

## PASSED ELEMENTS

1. **Script 7's physiological specificity is the strongest piece of mechanism writing in the batch.** "Your chest tightens slightly. Your thoughts scatter. And without quite deciding to, you start softening your position" — this is exact behavioural description, not psychological generalisation. It names the microsecond experience rather than the category. This is the standard the rest of the mechanism scripts should be held to.

2. **The "sorry → thank you" reframe in Script 2 is the best action step in the batch.** It is specific, immediately usable, behavioural rather than attitudinal, and requires no prior psychological understanding to implement. It also models the principle it's teaching — which is good pedagogical design.

3. **Script 15's "structural, not decorative" distinction is a genuinely fresh reframe of a very familiar self-help topic.** Most content critiquing affirmations either dismisses them entirely or defends them. This script offers a third position — not that affirmations are wrong, but that they operate at the wrong level for this specific problem — and it does so in Luna's voice without condescension toward people who use them.

4. **Carousel 5 is the strongest carousel in the batch.** The four-step structure maps directly onto the three-part sentence from Script 19, but extends it with Step 4 (tolerate the discomfort) — which is the step most communication frameworks omit and the one most readers will find most resonant. The final slide landing on "one held moment at a time" as confidence-building is entirely consistent with the batch's core thesis.

5. **Voice consistency across all 20 scripts is genuinely high.** No script breaks into lecture mode, no script moralises, no script uses motivational filler. Luna's characteristic move — validate the nervous system's logic first, redirect second — is present in nearly every script and functions as a coherent brand signature across the batch.

## PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure observed:** The one recurring issue in this batch is the use of psychological and clinical language that is accurate in register but either overclaims research consensus or carries clinical associations the brand cannot support (see Issues 1 and 2). This is a specific failure mode: the Shorts Script Agent is generating credible-sounding psychological framing — which is appropriate for Luna Hart — but occasionally tips across the line from "informed lay explanation" into "implied clinical authority."

**Producing agent:** Shorts Script Agent

**Recommended prompt addition:** Add the following constraint to the Shorts Script Agent prompt under Luna Hart's faculty-specific instructions:

*"When referencing psychology research or established frameworks (attachment theory, nervous system regulation, etc.), describe the concept behaviourally rather than asserting scientific consensus. Do not use phrases like '[field] is clear on this' or '[research] shows' without a named, verifiable source. Do not use DSM-associated clinical terms (hypervigilance, dissociation, window of tolerance, complex trauma, etc.) in scripts — replace with behavioural descriptions. Luna's authority is her framing, not her citations."*

This single addition would have prevented both Issues 1 and 2 in this batch and would function as a standing guardrail for all future Luna Hart scripts involving psychological mechanism content.
