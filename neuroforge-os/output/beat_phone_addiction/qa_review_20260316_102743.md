### ISSUES FOUND

**Issue 1:** Unverified statistic presented with false precision
**Location:** "The Features Are Not Accidents" section, paragraph 2 — "he has estimated that the design costs users approximately 200,000 hours of collective time every day"
**Problem:** This is a specific numerical claim attributed to a named individual. If Raskin said this, it needs to be sourced. If the number cannot be verified to a specific interview, article, or testimony, it cannot appear in print as a factual attribution. A fabricated or misremembered precise statistic is a Red Flag-adjacent failure — if challenged publicly, this damages NeuroForge's credibility in exactly the domain (rigour, no overclaiming) the brand depends on.
**Required fix:** Research Agent must verify the source for this specific figure and provide the citation (publication, date, context). If unverifiable, the sentence must be rewritten to remove the number or attribute it to Raskin's general public statements without the specific figure. Do not retain the "200,000 hours" claim without a traceable source.
**Severity:** Critical

---

**Issue 2:** Paraphrase presented in quotation-adjacent framing
**Location:** "The Problem Was Designed, Not Discovered" section, paragraph 3 — "described this directly in public testimony: that the attention economy is essentially a race to the bottom of the brain stem"
**Problem:** The phrase "race to the bottom of the brain stem" is presented after "described this directly" — which signals a direct quotation or close paraphrase to the reader. This is a paraphrase, and Harris's actual phrasing varies across interviews and testimony. The word "directly" is doing false work here. If this is paraphrased, it must be framed as such. If the editor wants to retain it as a quote, they need the verbatim transcript.
**Required fix:** Either (a) verify the exact quotation and replace the paraphrase with the verbatim phrase in quotation marks with a source citation, or (b) reframe the attribution — e.g., "Harris has argued repeatedly, in public testimony and interviews, that the attention economy is essentially a race to the bottom of the brain stem" — and remove the word "directly," which implies verbatim accuracy the text does not deliver.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 3:** One subheading is a section label, not a pull
**Location:** "The Mechanism Map" subheading, exercise section
**Problem:** "The Mechanism Map" tells the reader what the section contains. It does not make a claim, create curiosity, or generate tension. Compare to "The Features Are Not Accidents" — that subheading implies a revelation and creates pull. "The Mechanism Map" is the one subheading in the chapter that fails the entertainment checklist item: *subheadings create pull (claim or curiosity) — not just labels for the section below.*
**Required fix:** Reframe the subheading to make a claim or create curiosity. Options might lean toward something that implies why naming the mechanism matters — the subheading should make the reader want to do the exercise, not just describe that an exercise exists. The editor should propose and approve the revised subheading.
**Severity:** Minor

**Issue 4:** Expository flat patch reduces momentum in one section
**Location:** "What Your Phone Wants Versus What You Want" section, paragraphs 2–3
**Problem:** The section opens well with the platform-versus-user opposition, but paragraphs 2 and 3 — "What you want is different. The reader is being described at, not pulled forward. This is the one section where the Entertainment checklist item — *every section creates a micro-tension: a question that needs answering, a claim that needs proving* — is not met. The tension is present at section open and close but drops in the middle.
**Required fix:** The editor should review paragraphs 2–3 of this section and identify whether the content can be compressed or reframed to reintroduce tension. The core point (your goals and the platform's goals are structurally opposed) is already made in paragraph 1 and the final paragraph — the middle currently restates rather than advances it. Consider whether one of those paragraphs can be cut or sharpened to remove the flat patch without losing the logical scaffolding.
**Severity:** Minor

### PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** Both credibility issues in this chapter relate to the same failure mode — specific numerical or verbal attributions that are asserted with more precision than the sourcing supports. The Raskin figure is presented as a known fact; the Harris paraphrase is framed as direct testimony. Neither has a verifiable citation attached. This suggests the Manuscript Agent is generating specific-sounding attributions based on training data approximations rather than verified sources.

**Producing agent:** Manuscript Agent

**Recommended prompt change:** Add the following instruction to the Manuscript Agent's credibility rules: *"Any attribution that includes a specific number, statistic, or direct quotation must be flagged with [VERIFY] immediately following it in the draft. Do not present specific figures or attributed phrases as confirmed facts. The Research Agent holds verified citations; the Manuscript Agent must request confirmation before treating any specific claim as sourced."* This creates a verification handshake between agents rather than allowing the Manuscript Agent to assert precision it cannot confirm.
