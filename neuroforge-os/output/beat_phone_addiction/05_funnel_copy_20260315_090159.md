Here's the counterintuitive idea I mentioned.

Most people think of attention as a single capacity — a muscle you either have or you don't, strong or weak, present or absent. That's the frame behind every "just focus" instruction you've ever received. And it's wrong in a way that matters practically.

Attention is a stack. It has at least four distinct layers: the ability to orient toward a task (pointing your focus at the right thing), sustain engagement with it (staying there without being pulled away), suppress interruption (keeping competing inputs from breaking in), and re-engage after disruption (finding your way back to where you were).

Phone use degrades all four layers simultaneously, but it degrades them at different rates and through different mechanisms. The suppression layer goes first — you lose the ability to keep the phone out of peripheral awareness even when you're not looking at it. Research consistently shows that a phone sitting face-down on a desk reduces working memory performance, not because you're looking at it but because some fraction of your attention is monitoring it. The device doesn't need to interrupt you. Its presence is the interruption.

The re-engagement layer goes next. You've probably heard the 23-minute statistic — that's how long it takes, on average, to return to full engagement with a task after a phone interruption. What most people don't know is that most people get interrupted again before they complete that recovery. A typical interrupted work session isn't one 23-minute recovery — it's a sequence of incomplete recoveries, each one starting from a lower baseline than the last.

Here's the reframe: you're not trying to "focus better." You're trying to rebuild four separate infrastructure layers, each of which responds to a slightly different intervention.

The 5-Day Reset is already addressing the suppression layer — removing the device from your field of attention during work. The boredom tolerance work from Email 2 is rebuilding the re-engagement layer. The morning protocol in Day 3 of the Reset is protecting the orientation layer.
