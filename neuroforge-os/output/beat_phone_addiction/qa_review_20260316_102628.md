### VERDICT

**CONDITIONAL PASS**

The blueprint is structurally excellent and voice-consistent, but contains several specific figures and claims presented as established fact that require source verification or qualification before the Manuscript Agent is briefed — if those figures are fabricated and make it into the manuscript, the book fails the Credibility standard at a later and more expensive stage.

### ISSUES FOUND

**Issue 1:** Potentially unverified specific statistic — 23-minute interruption recovery cost
**Location:** Chapter 3, Key Points: "the 23-minute recovery cost of a phone interruption"
**Problem:** This figure is widely cited in productivity writing and is attributed to Gloria Mark's research at UC Irvine, but the specific "23 minutes" number is frequently misrepresented — the original research measured recovery time after *self-interruption* in a specific workplace context, and the figure is often stripped of its methodology and applied universally. If the Manuscript Agent cites this as a clean, universal fact without qualification, it constitutes a credibility failure. The risk is not that the figure is wrong — it may be defensible — but that it is being treated as a settled statistic when it requires methodological context.
**Required fix:** Research Agent to locate the original Gloria Mark citation and confirm: (1) the exact figure, (2) the study context, (3) whether the claim can be made in the specific form the blueprint intends. If verified, include methodological qualifier in the manuscript brief. If not verifiable in the intended form, instruct the Manuscript Agent to present it as a research finding with appropriate framing, not a clean data point.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 2:** Unverified specific figures presented as established data — pickup frequency and willpower collapse window
**Location:** Transformation Arc, Before state, item 1: "over 100 times per day"; Core Promise: "willpower and app-based restrictions… collapse within 48–72 hours"
**Problem:** "100+ pickups per day" is a figure that has circulated in various forms (some studies suggest higher, some lower, methodology varies significantly by how "pickup" is defined). The 48–72 hour willpower collapse window is not attributed to any research — it reads like a plausible estimate stated as a verified interval. Neither figure is footnoted or qualified. In a blueprint, this is a planning risk; in a manuscript, it becomes a fabricated citation.
**Required fix:** Research Agent to verify both figures with sources before the manuscript brief is issued. If verified, include source notation in the brief. If not verifiable at the stated precision, revise to a qualified range ("research estimates range from X to Y") or remove the specific figure and replace with a description of the pattern.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 3:** Potential title conflict — "Stolen Focus" appears as both a title option and a named competing book
**Location:** Section 1, Title Options, Option 5: *"Stolen Focus: Reclaim Your Attention from the Machine Designed to Take It"*; Section 10, Differentiation Note: "Johann Hari's *Stolen Focus*"
**Problem:** The fifth title option uses "Stolen Focus" as its opening phrase — which is also the exact title of a published, commercially successful book by Johann Hari (2022). This creates a direct naming conflict that would fail at trademark screening and signals either an oversight or an assumption that the phrase is generic. The Differentiation Note correctly identifies Hari's book as a competitor, which makes the title option doubly anomalous — the blueprint appears to be recommending a title that echoes a named competitor.
**Required fix:** Remove Title Option 5 from the document. If the underlying concept (direct accusation + concrete promise) is valuable, instruct the Book Architect Agent to develop a replacement option that does not share language with a competitor title. The recommended title is not affected — this issue is isolated to Option 5.
**Severity:** Critical

---

**Issue 4:** Notification source count presented without attribution
**Location:** Chapter 4, Key Points: "most users have 40–80 active notification sources"
**Problem:** This is a specific quantitative range presented as an established fact. It may be accurate, but it appears unattributed. In a blueprint it is a planning note; in a manuscript it becomes either a sourced claim or a fabrication.
**Required fix:** Research Agent to identify the source for this figure before the Chapter 4 manuscript brief is issued. If a reliable source exists, note it in the brief. If not, the Manuscript Agent should be instructed to frame this as an observation the reader can verify themselves ("count your active notification sources — the number is almost certainly higher than you expect") rather than a stated statistic.
**Severity:** Minor

---

**Issue 5:** Blueprint Quality Check is formatted as a deliverable section rather than an internal process note
**Location:** Section 11, Blueprint Quality Check
**Problem:** This section performs the agent's own quality assessment and presents it as part of the blueprint deliverable. It is not useful to a human editor — it is internal process documentation that belongs in the agent's output log, not in a document being handed to a writing team. Its presence also creates a subtle credibility issue: a self-certified checklist carries no independent weight, and its inclusion may cause a human editor to assume quality checks have been completed when the issues flagged above have not been resolved.
**Required fix:** Remove Section 11 from the blueprint deliverable. Retain it as an internal agent output if needed for the Prompt Library log. The QA report (this document) is the quality check — not the producing agent's self-assessment.
**Severity:** Minor

---

**Issue 6:** Chapter 6 is filed under "Environment Design" in the chapter phase structure but addresses internal psychology
**Location:** Blueprint Quality Check self-classification; Chapter 6 transition; Section 11 phase description ("Diagnosis (1–4) → Environment Design (5–6)")
**Problem:** The four-phase structure groups Chapter 6 (boredom tolerance training) with Chapter 5 (environmental redesign), but the two chapters address categorically different problems — external architecture vs. internal conditioning. A human editor preparing section headers or a designer building a book's structural argument may misrepresent the logic if the phase grouping is taken at face value. This is a minor structural labelling issue, not a content problem — the chapter itself is correctly conceived.
**Required fix:** Revise the phase description in any summary reference. Suggested: "Environment Design (Ch. 5) → Internal Conditioning (Ch. 6) → Structural Protocols (Ch. 7–10)." This more accurately reflects the book's actual logic and prevents a mislabelling in production.
**Severity:** Minor

### PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** The Book Architect Agent produced multiple specific quantitative claims (23-minute recovery time, 100+ daily pickups, 48–72 hour willpower collapse, 40–80 notification sources) without source notation and without flagging them as requiring Research Agent verification. The agent treated these figures as established facts rather than claims requiring independent confirmation before being passed to a writing team.

**Producing agent:** Book Architect Agent

**Recommended prompt change:** Add the following instruction to the Book Architect Agent's prompt under a "Data and Statistics Protocol" heading:

*"When including any specific quantitative figure in a blueprint — pickup frequencies, recovery times, statistical ranges, neurological timelines — you must either: (a) note the source in brackets immediately after the figure, e.g. [Source: Gloria Mark, UC Irvine, 2008], or (b) flag the figure with [VERIFY] and a note that the Research Agent must confirm before the manuscript brief is issued. Never present a specific number as a settled fact without one of these two markers. A blueprint that passes specific unverified figures to the Manuscript Agent creates a fabricated citation risk at the most expensive possible stage."*

**Secondary note:** The inclusion of a self-certification checklist (Section 11) as a deliverable section should be addressed. Add a line to the Book Architect Agent's output format instructions: *"The Blueprint Quality Check is an internal process log. Do not include it in the deliverable document. The QA Agent performs the quality check."*
