**SCRIPT 6**
**Hook:** Your phone is not addictive because of the content. It is addictive because of the timing.
**Faculty:** Kai Ren
**Topic:** Variable reward schedules and dopamine
**Estimated Duration:** 50 seconds

---

**Your phone is not addictive because of the content. It is addictive because of the timing.**

Dopamine does not spike when you get a reward. It spikes in anticipation of a reward that may or may not arrive. This is called a variable reward schedule — and it is the most powerful conditioning mechanism in behavioural science.

Slot machines run on variable reward. Sometimes you pull the lever and win. Sometimes you do not. The uncertainty is the mechanism — it keeps you pulling. Pull-to-refresh works identically. Sometimes you pull and there is something new. Sometimes there is not. Your dopamine system does not know which one this will be. That is why you keep checking.

This is not a flaw in your character. It is a feature of your neurology being deliberately exploited by a system that was designed by teams of engineers whose job was to maximise your return rate.

Understanding this does two things. It removes the shame — you are not weak, you are a normal human nervous system responding to precision conditioning. And it tells you where to intervene. You cannot fight the dopamine spike. But you can eliminate the trigger that fires it.

Remove the notifications. Remove the apps from your home screen. Increase the steps to get in. Make the slot machine harder to reach.

The recalibration goes the other direction too. Reduce the low-effort input for long enough and the baseline drops back down. Deep work starts to feel engaging again. Conversation starts to feel sufficient. The boredom you feel in the first week of a phone protocol is not permanent — it is withdrawal. It has a known timeline.

Expect 10 to 14 days before the baseline shifts. Work through it, not around it.

What you have done is removed the stimulus that initiates most of your pickup chains. The reflex still exists, but it now has nothing to fire on. Without the trigger, the behaviour chain does not start.

This is not about willpower in the moment. It is about eliminating the moment entirely.
