### ISSUES FOUND

---

**Issue 1:** Unanchored percentage claim
**Location:** Lead Magnet Description, Bullet 2 — *"a tiered classification system that eliminates the triggers driving 80% of your compulsive checks"*
**Problem:** "80%" is stated as a factual figure with no source, no qualifier ("research suggests," "in testing," "typically"), and no mechanism for how that number was derived. Sceptical readers — and this audience is explicitly sceptical — will notice this. A single unsourced percentage in otherwise credible copy undercuts the credibility of the surrounding claims. It also opens a potential liability: if a customer argues the protocol didn't eliminate 80% of their triggers, this language is a measurable promise.
**Required fix:** Either (a) remove the percentage entirely and replace with a specific description of what the audit removes ("eliminates the triggers behind most reflexive checks — notifications, badge counts, and home screen placement"), or (b) add a soft qualifier ("In most cases, the top three notification categories account for the majority of compulsive check triggers — this audit identifies and removes them"). Do not add a citation unless you have a specific, verifiable source.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 2:** Contested statistic deployed without qualification
**Location:** Email 3, paragraph 4 — *"Research consistently shows that a phone sitting face-down on a desk reduces working memory performance"* and *"You've probably heard the 23-minute statistic — that's how long it takes, on average, to return to full engagement with a task after a phone interruption."*
**Problem:** Two issues in proximity. First, the face-down phone and working memory claim is real (Ward et al., 2017, University of Texas) but "research consistently shows" overstates the replication picture — this finding comes from a limited body of work and the effect size varies. Second, the 23-minute statistic derives from Gloria Mark's research on digital interruptions generally, not specifically phone interruptions. Its circulation in popular media has moved it away from its original context. A reader who looks it up may find the framing here slightly misapplied, which damages credibility with the exact audience (sceptical, detail-oriented) this funnel is targeting. Neither is a fabricated citation — both are real research references — so this does not trigger an automatic red flag. But both need softening before the funnel runs on cold traffic.
**Required fix:** For the face-down claim: change "research consistently shows" to "research suggests" or name the study if you are comfortable with the citation (Ward et al., 2017 is defensible). For the 23-minute figure: add a qualifier — "You've probably heard the 23-minute statistic — the research figure for how long it takes, on average, to recover full task engagement after a digital interruption." The word "digital interruption" is more accurate to the original research context than "phone interruption."
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 3:** Google Ads description character count likely over limit
**Location:** Section 4, Ad 1, Description 1 — *"Your phone was built to maximise your return rate. Willpower-based fixes fail because they work downstream of the problem."*
**Problem:** Google Ads descriptions are capped at 90 characters. This description reads at approximately 118 characters. The production note in the ads section mentions character count verification for headlines but does not flag this description. If uploaded as written, it will be rejected or auto-truncated by Google, potentially mid-sentence, with unpredictable results.
**Required fix:** Recount all four descriptions in the Search Ads section against the 90-character limit. Ad 1, Description 1 specifically needs trimming. Possible shorter version: "Phone apps are engineered to maximise your return rate. Willpower fixes fail because they're downstream of the problem." — verify at 90 characters.
**Severity:** Major (production blocker)

---

**Issue 4:** "Before/After" block tells rather than shows — reads as outcome list
**Location:** Section 2, Book Description, "Before:" and "After:" paragraphs
**Problem:** The Before paragraph works — "low-grade negotiation with yourself about not picking up the phone" is specific enough to create recognition. The After paragraph is weaker: "The environment is redesigned. The triggers are gone or structurally costly. The work block runs for 90 minutes." This is a list of outcomes stated flatly, not an image of what those outcomes feel like from the inside. For a $17 impulse purchase, the After block needs to close the gap emotionally — not with sentimentality, but with precision about what it's like to be the person in the "after" state. In its current form, it reads like a spec sheet.
**Required fix:** Rewrite the After paragraph to place the reader inside the experience rather than listing its properties. The Before paragraph's technique — naming the specific micro-moment ("low-grade negotiation") — should be applied to the After as well. Example direction: "You sit down to work. The phone is face-down across the room. You don't think about it. The block runs." The specificity of the absence is more persuasive than a list of what has changed.
**Severity:** Minor

---

**Issue 5:** Objection handler tone drifts toward lecture register
**Location:** Section 2, Objection Handlers, "I've tried self-help before" — *"The frameworks you've read before addressed mindset or motivation. This one addresses architecture. If you've changed how you think about your phone use without changing the environment around it, you've been solving the wrong variable. The method was wrong, not you."*
**Problem:** This is accurate and the closing line ("The method was wrong, not you") is well-placed. But the opening two sentences read as instruction rather than voice — closer to a methodology note than copy written by Kai Ren. "The frameworks you've read before addressed mindset or motivation. This one addresses architecture." is stated with the flatness of a product comparison, not the diagnostic confidence of the faculty voice. It's a small drift, but in a section that is otherwise voice-consistent, it registers.
**Required fix:** Adjust the opening to carry more of Kai Ren's characteristic diagnostic register. The distinction being drawn (mindset/motivation vs. architecture) is the right one — it just needs to arrive with more specificity. Direction: name something the reader actually did, not just what category of approach they used. Example direction: "If you've taken notes in a productivity book, set intentions, or told yourself you'd be better about it starting Monday — that's a motivation-layer intervention. Your phone doesn't run on motivation. It runs on architecture."
**Severity:** Minor

### PASSED ELEMENTS

1. **Email 2's core reframe is precisely constructed.** "You feel bored because you check your phone. Not before — after." The sentence break is doing real work. The reversal is stated baldly enough that it lands as a genuine counter-claim, not a repackaged insight. The follow-on mechanism explanation (dopamine baseline recalibration, reward signal frequency) earns the reframe rather than leaving it unsupported. This is the sharpest single passage in the package.

2. **The lead magnet description block opens without warm-up.** "You sit down to do real work. Eight minutes in, Instagram is open. You didn't decide to open it. You just looked down and there it was." This is scene-setting that earns its place — it names a specific micro-moment (eight minutes, not "a short while"; Instagram, not "social media") and the final observation ("You just looked down and there it was") is accurate enough to function as recognition rather than accusation. The reader does not feel described; they feel seen.

3. **Email 3's attention stack framing is the strongest original content in the package.** Segmenting attention into four separable layers (orientation, sustenance, suppression, re-engagement) and mapping specific degradation rates and mechanisms to each is a genuinely diagnostic frame that this audience — intelligent, systems-oriented, previously unsuccessful with standard approaches — will not have encountered in this form. The connection back to the Reset ("The 5-Day Reset is already addressing the suppression layer") retroactively gives the lead magnet a conceptual framework it didn't explicitly have. This is smart funnel architecture.

4. **The testimonial collection guidance is the best element in Section 1.** Instructing the team to ask three specific questions ("What had you tried before that didn't work? What specific change did you make? What did you notice in the first week?"), and explicitly noting that hedging language ("I was sceptical but...") should be preserved rather than edited out because it increases credibility with this audience — this is unusually precise production guidance. It reflects a real understanding of what converts sceptical readers and will produce significantly better social proof than generic solicitation.

5. **Email 5 closes the sequence without pressure and without softening.** "If you're not ready for that yet, that's straightforward — you'll keep getting practical content from me on focus, attention, and operational discipline." The word "straightforward" does something specific: it removes the implied judgment from "no problem" or "that's totally okay" (which are reassurances, and reassurances imply the reader might need reassurance). It reads as the faculty member's genuine indifference to whether the reader buys, which is the most credible possible register for a final pitch. "Either way, you're in the right place" closes without a motivational tone. This is well-judged.

### PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** No red flags. The conditional pass is driven by two statistical claims that are real but imprecisely framed, one production spec issue, and two minor voice/conversion issues. The producing agent appears to have a consistent strength in structural framing and voice consistency, with a recurring weakness in how it handles research citations — it uses real statistics but applies them slightly beyond their documented scope ("research consistently shows" for a limited replication base; phone-specific framing for a digital-interruption general finding).

**Producing agent:** Funnel Agent

**Recommended prompt addition:** Add the following instruction to the Funnel Agent's credibility guidelines:

> *When citing statistics or research findings, apply the following rule: if you cannot name the specific study, author, and year from which the figure derives, do not state it as a definitive fact. Use qualified language: "research suggests," "evidence points to," "studies have found." If you can name the study, include the citation in a bracketed note for the editor to verify. The audience for NeuroForge funnels is sceptical and detail-oriented. A single statistic that does not survive a 30-second search damages the credibility of all surrounding copy. Default to specific and qualified over specific and overstated.*

This single addition would have resolved Issues 1 and 2. Issue 3 (character count) may reflect a gap in the production verification step — the agent should be prompted to recount all description fields, not just headline fields, against platform limits before marking production-ready.
