### ISSUES FOUND

---

**Issue 1:** Title collision with a major published work
**Location:** Title Options, Option 5 — *Stolen Focus: Reclaim Your Attention from the Machine Designed to Take It*
**Problem:** *Stolen Focus* is the exact title of Johann Hari's 2022 bestseller, published by Crown and widely distributed. This title cannot be used. More importantly, the Differentiation Note in Section 10 names Hari's *Stolen Focus* as a direct competitor — meaning the Blueprint simultaneously proposes to use a competitor's title and positions the book against that competitor. This is a legal risk and a brand credibility risk. If this title were passed through to a designer or listed on a publishing brief, it would cause a serious problem.
**Required fix:** Remove Option 5 from the title list entirely. It is not usable in any form. The remaining four options are clean.
**Severity:** Critical

---

**Issue 2:** Unverified quantitative claim — 23-minute interruption recovery cost
**Location:** Chapter 3 bullet point: *"How the 23-minute recovery cost of a phone interruption compounds across a working day"*
**Problem:** The "23 minutes to recover from an interruption" figure is widely cited in productivity content and is commonly attributed to Gloria Mark at UC Irvine. The actual research finding is more nuanced than the popular framing — the study examined all workplace interruptions, not phone-specific interruptions, and the figure varies significantly by task type, interruption type, and individual. If the manuscript cites this as a clean fact without qualification, it will be challenged by informed readers and could undermine credibility. It is not fabricated — but it is routinely overclaimed.
**Required fix:** The Manuscript Agent must be briefed to (a) verify the original source, (b) present the finding with appropriate precision — naming the researcher, the study context, and the actual range rather than a single dramatic number, and (c) not present it as a phone-specific statistic when the original data is broader. The Blueprint can retain the reference, but the briefing note for the Manuscript Agent must flag this explicitly.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 3:** Unverified quantitative claim — phone pickup frequency
**Location:** Transformation Arc, Before State, Point 1 — *"Picks up their phone over 100 times per day, the majority of those pickups uninitiated by a conscious decision"*
**Problem:** The 100+ daily pickups figure is plausible and consistent with various usage studies, but no source is named and the figures across studies vary considerably (some report averages of 58, some over 150, depending on the population and methodology). The "majority uninitiated by a conscious decision" qualifier is a strong specific claim that needs a source or must be framed as the author's observed estimate rather than a cited fact. In a blueprint this is acceptable as a working figure, but the Manuscript Agent needs to know this requires either sourcing or an explicit authorial framing at manuscript stage.
**Required fix:** Flag for Manuscript Agent: this figure requires either a cited source with appropriate precision, or explicit framing as an estimate (e.g., "research varies, but most studies place the figure above 80 for heavy users"). Do not present as a clean fact without attribution.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 4:** "Boredom tolerance" chapter references "distress tolerance model" without sufficient differentiation from clinical DBT framing
**Location:** Chapter 6, Key Points — *"The distress tolerance model: sitting with discomfort not as a spiritual practice but as a neurological recalibration — the evidence for why graduated exposure works and what the timescale looks like"*
**Problem:** "Distress tolerance" is a specific clinical term from Dialectical Behaviour Therapy (DBT), where it has a defined meaning in the context of treating borderline personality disorder and trauma. Using this term without acknowledgment of its clinical origin risks either (a) appearing to make implicit mental health treatment claims, which is a Red Flag category, or (b) being flagged by informed readers as an inappropriate appropriation of clinical language for a productivity context. The underlying idea — graduated exposure to discomfort as a trainable skill — is sound and does not require the clinical framing.
**Required fix:** Manuscript Agent should avoid the phrase "distress tolerance model" and instead use language native to the Kai Ren frame: graduated discomfort exposure, boredom recalibration, or threshold training. The mechanism is valid; the terminology borrows clinical weight it does not need and carries risk it should not carry.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 5:** Chapter 6 phase label inconsistency — classified as "Environment Design" when it belongs to the next phase
**Location:** Blueprint Quality Check, self-assessment: *"The structure moves through four clean phases: Diagnosis (1–4) → Environment Design (5–6) → Structural Protocols (7–10)"*
**Problem:** The self-assessment groups Chapter 6 (boredom tolerance training) with Chapter 5 (environmental design) under the "Environment Design" phase. This is a weak grouping — boredom tolerance is explicitly an internal trigger intervention, not an environmental one. The Blueprint's own chapter introductions make this distinction clearly ("the environment redesign in Chapter 5 raises the cost of external triggers... Chapter 6 addresses the internal trigger directly"). The phase label in the quality check misrepresents the chapter's function.
**Problem it creates:** If this phase label is carried forward to a table of contents or marketing description, it understates what the book does. A reader who buys the book for environmental design gets Chapter 6 as a surprise pivot. More importantly, the internal logic of the book is stronger than the phase label suggests — the phase should be renamed to reflect both dimensions.
**Required fix:** Rename the second phase in the Blueprint Quality Check and any table of contents draft to something like "External and Internal Architecture (5–6)" or "Environment and Trigger Design (5–6)" — a label that accurately covers both what Chapter 5 and Chapter 6 deliver.
**Severity:** Minor

---

**Issue 6:** Differentiation Note reads as a pitch document rather than a reader-facing or editorial-facing framing
**Location:** Section 10, Differentiation Note, throughout
**Problem:** The note accurately identifies the gap in the market and the three competing titles. However, it is written in the register of a publishing pitch — "What none of them deliver is what the target reader of this book is specifically looking for" — rather than as an editorial brief that helps the Manuscript Agent understand what this book must do differently at the sentence and argument level. It names the gap but does not give the writer anything specific to do with that information. The competing books are summarised accurately but briefly enough that a Manuscript Agent could not use this section to avoid the specific failure modes of each competitor.
**Required fix:** Add a brief "avoid these specific failure modes" note for each competitor: (1) Newport — avoid becoming a philosophical argument for minimalism with no operational payload; (2) Eyal — avoid the self-help register and the values-alignment framing that can feel like therapy; (3) Hari — avoid the journalist's voice and the cultural outrage framing. These are writing-level instructions, not positioning notes, and they belong in the brief.
**Severity:** Minor

### PASSED ELEMENTS

1. **The transformation arc is the strongest in the NeuroForge blueprint library reviewed to date.** Every before/after state describes a measurable behaviour or observable condition — "cannot sustain focused work for more than 8–12 minutes," "completes 90-minute focused work blocks as a standard operating condition" — with no emotional vagueness. This is the Kai Ren standard executed correctly.

2. **Chapter 9 (The Performance Loop) is genuinely original.** Separating the creator's performance monitoring compulsion from the consumer's scrolling compulsion is a real insight that the three named competitors do not address with specificity. The anatomy of the publish-anticipate-check-interpret loop is precise and will land with the exact reader this book is targeting.

3. **The exercise architecture is excellent.** Every exercise produces a specific, named output that feeds directly into the next chapter — the Interruption Log feeds the environment design, the Full System Audit is the input document for Chapters 5–7, the Baseline Audit produces a before-snapshot remeasured at Days 15 and 30. The workbook map in Section 9 makes this visible at a glance. This is what distinguishes a protocol book from a concept book, and it is done correctly here.

4. **The front matter plan demonstrates genuine craft awareness.** The instruction to open with a second-person, present-tense micro-scene before naming the mechanism — and the explicit list of what the book is *not* — gives the Manuscript Agent enough specificity to open the book correctly without requiring a rewrite at QA stage. The "it is not asking for abstinence, it is not anti-technology, it is not going to tell the reader to move to the countryside" framing is exactly the right tone-setter for the Kai Ren audience.

5. **The chapter transition statements do genuine structural work.** Each transition does not merely say "next we will cover X" — it names the logical dependency between chapters ("Understanding the neurological cost sets up the logical question... Chapter 3 builds that picture"). This is a blueprint-level craft choice that will make the Manuscript Agent's job significantly easier and reduce the risk of chapters that feel disconnected.

### PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** Two out of three issues rated Major involve specific quantitative claims being carried forward in the blueprint without sourcing flags. The blueprint format does not currently require the Book Architect Agent to distinguish between verified claims and working figures. This means the Manuscript Agent will receive these figures without a signal that they require verification, and may present them as clean facts in the manuscript.

**Producing agent:** Book Architect Agent

**Recommended prompt change:** Add the following instruction to the Book Architect Agent prompt under Key Points to Cover guidelines:

*"When including specific statistics, study findings, or quantitative claims in a chapter outline, mark each one with [VERIFY] if the source has not been confirmed. The Manuscript Agent will not independently audit sources — if a figure is unverified at blueprint stage, it must be flagged here so the Research Agent or human editor can confirm it before it appears in prose. Unmarked figures will be assumed verified."*

This single addition would have flagged both the 23-minute interruption figure and the 100-pickups-per-day figure at blueprint stage, preventing them from travelling silently into the manuscript.
