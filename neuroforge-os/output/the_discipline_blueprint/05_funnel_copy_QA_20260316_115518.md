## ISSUES FOUND

**Issue 1:** Named protocols require manuscript verification
**Location:** Thank You Page book description; Email 4 bullet list; Email 3 CTA paragraph
**Problem:** The funnel names four specific internal frameworks — "Resistance Moment Protocol," "Trust Deposit Protocol," "Self-Trust Rebuild Sequence," and "Recovery Protocol" — as features of the book. If these names do not appear in the manuscript verbatim, or if the content under those names does not match what the funnel promises, the copy is overclaiming relative to the product. A reader who buys based on "The Resistance Moment Protocol" and finds a chapter that covers the same idea under a different name will experience a trust break. This is not a fabrication issue in the copy itself, but it is a pipeline continuity issue that could become a credibility issue at point of purchase.
**Required fix:** Cross-reference all four named protocols against the current manuscript draft. Confirm each exists under the exact name used in the funnel, or align the naming between funnel and manuscript before launch. Do not publish the Thank You page or email sequence until this is confirmed.
**Severity:** Critical

---

**Issue 2:** [LANDING_PAGE_URL] placeholder unresolved in three emails
**Location:** Email 2 (final paragraph), Email 3 (penultimate paragraph), Email 4 (penultimate paragraph)
**Problem:** The URL placeholder appears three times and has not been populated. This is a functional failure — if deployed as-is, three emails in the welcome sequence will contain a broken or literal placeholder link. Any reader who clicks will either get a 404 or see raw placeholder text, which damages both conversion and brand credibility immediately.
**Required fix:** Populate [LANDING_PAGE_URL] with the live book landing page URL before the sequence is loaded into the email platform. Confirm link tracking is applied if the platform supports it.
**Severity:** Critical

---

**Issue 3:** Internal metadata embedded in the production deliverable
**Location:** Section 5 (entire section) and the "Additional Quality Notes" block following it
**Problem:** Section 5 is a self-completed agent quality checklist. The Additional Quality Notes block is internal strategic commentary explaining sequencing decisions. Neither of these is publishable copy. If this document is handed to a developer, designer, or email platform operator without being stripped, there is a risk that internal process documentation ends up visible in the CMS, or that the receiving party is confused about what is live copy and what is metadata. Additionally, if the Additional Quality Notes block is read as part of the funnel brief, it may create misalignment — the notes describe the sequence logic in a way that should be in a separate strategy document, not embedded at the end of a copy package.
**Required fix:** Strip Section 5 and the Additional Quality Notes entirely from the production deliverable. Route them to the human editor or project manager as a separate internal document. The production file handed to any implementation resource should contain only Sections 1–4.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 4:** "There is a…" accumulation in book description block weakens closing section
**Location:** Thank You Page, Book Description, paragraphs beginning "There is a chapter on self-trust…" through "…what your daily behaviour is actually constructing over five years."
**Problem:** Three consecutive sentences open with "There is a…" The structure works once (it has a directness consistent with Voss voice) but by the third instance it reads as an inventory list rather than a persuasive build. The momentum created by the first half of the book description — which is genuinely engaging — flattens here. For a reader who arrived from the lead magnet with real interest, this is the section that should be closing the sale, and it decelerates at the wrong moment.
**Required fix:** Restructure these three sentences so they are not parallel in form. The self-trust chapter description is the strongest of the three — consider leading with it and varying the syntax of the following two. Do not reduce the specificity; only change the sentence-opening pattern.
**Severity:** Minor

---

**Issue 5:** "twelve more ideas at this level" — vague claim in Email 3
**Location:** Email 3, final CTA paragraph: "the book has twelve more ideas at this level"
**Problem:** This is the one place in the funnel where specificity breaks. The rest of the copy earns its claims — it names protocols, describes mechanisms, cites word counts. "Twelve more ideas at this level" is a quantity without content. It does not tell the reader what those ideas are, what level means in this context, or why twelve is meaningful. For a Voss audience that is specifically sceptical of vague promises, this is a mild but noticeable gear-change.
**Required fix:** Either replace "twelve more ideas at this level" with a short, specific named example ("the full Trust Deposit Protocol, the environmental audit, and nine more frameworks in the same mode"), or remove the quantity claim entirely and replace with a single concrete named idea from the book that extends the credit score concept directly. Do not keep the unanchored number.
**Severity:** Minor

---

**Issue 6:** Email 4 "picture a man" framing — minor voice softness
**Location:** Email 4, opening four paragraphs
**Problem:** The third-person "picture a man" device is more novelistic than the rest of the Voss corpus in this funnel. It works — the character is recognisable and the pivot to "this is you" is implicit without being stated — but it is the one moment in the package where the voice drifts slightly toward storytelling mode. Voss elsewhere speaks directly to the reader ("you are lying there," "you said you'd be up at 6") rather than using a surrogate. This is not an off-voice failure, but it is a detectable softening in a sequence that is otherwise consistently direct.
**Required fix:** This is a minor note, not a required change before launch. If the human editor agrees the device works, it can stay. If the editor wants to tighten voice consistency, the fix is to convert the "picture a man" framing into direct second-person — "You've read the books. Atomic Habits. Can't Hurt Me…" — which would be more characteristic of the rest of the sequence.
**Severity:** Minor

## PASSED ELEMENTS

1. **The strongest single line in the package** — "A document you complete today is a tool. A document you open on Friday is something you meant to do." (Email 1) This is exactly what Voss copy should do: one short observation that makes the reader aware of their own behaviour without explaining itself. It does not moralize. It just names the thing. This is the line a reader quotes to someone else.

2. **Email 5 is the best structural execution of the Voss close** — the deliberate brevity, the explicit "I'm not going to tell you you're running out of time. It's a book. It will still be there," and the low-pressure offer followed by a genuine statement of ongoing value. Most email sequences either go too hard on the final close or trail off without landing anything. This one lands cleanly and leaves the reader's trust intact regardless of whether they buy. That is hard to write.

3. **The testimonial placeholder brief is correct in both format and instinct** — specifying "no star ratings — this audience finds them hollow" is an accurate read of the Voss audience, and the direction given for each testimonial (what to seek, what to avoid) is precise enough to be actionable for a real testimonial collection effort. This is not generic "add testimonials here" filler; it is a working brief.

4. **The objection handler for "I've tried self-help before"** — short, structurally sound, and correctly identifies the mechanism ("you've tried motivation, not structure") rather than simply dismissing the objection. It does not condescend. It reframes without arguing. It is the strongest of the three objection handlers.

5. **The Google Ads copy is on-target for the search intent** — the targeting logic is visible in the copy (Ad 1 matches the "why can't I stay disciplined" query by naming the structural misdiagnosis; Ad 3 matches "how to stop quitting on yourself" with the pre-decision framing). ## PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** No failures severe enough to constitute a systemic prompt issue. The two Critical issues (protocol name verification, URL placeholder) are pipeline hygiene issues rather than copy quality failures — the Funnel Agent produced strong copy. The recurring Minor issues (vague quantity claim, "there is a" accumulation) suggest a pattern where the agent produces highly specific copy across the main body of a section and then slightly softens under pressure in closing/CTA moments, likely because closing CTAs are structurally formulaic prompts and the agent defaults to slightly more generic language there.

**Producing agent:** Funnel Agent

**Recommended prompt change:** Add a specific instruction to the Funnel Agent prompt requiring that all internal protocol names used in funnel copy be flagged in a **[PIPELINE CHECK]** note at the end of the deliverable listing every named framework referenced — with a reminder that the name must match the manuscript exactly. This would catch the Critical Issue 1 pattern at production rather than at QA. Additionally, add an instruction that Section 5 / self-QA metadata must be delivered in a separate clearly labelled block titled **[INTERNAL — DO NOT PUBLISH]** rather than embedded in the body of the copy package, preventing the production file hygiene issue from recurring.
