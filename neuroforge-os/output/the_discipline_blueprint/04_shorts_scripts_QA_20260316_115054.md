**Issue 3:** Content overlap between Scripts 5 and 9
**Location:** Script 5 (*"The Moment Discipline Fails"*) and Script 9 (*"The Resistance Moment / Psychological Architecture of Failure"*)
**Problem:** Both scripts use the "thirty seconds before you quit" framing as their central mechanism. Script 5 introduces it; Script 9 essentially re-explains it with marginally more protocol detail. As individual pieces of content in a published short-form series, a viewer who encounters both will register this as repetition, which signals content padding and dilutes the perceived value of the series. The checklist requires: *only one idea per script.* Both scripts technically comply individually, but the batch fails the spirit of that rule — two scripts are effectively the same idea.
**Required fix:** Differentiate Scripts 5 and 9 at the concept level, not just the framing. Script 5 can retain the "identify the moment" angle. Script 9 should be rebuilt around a distinct mechanism — options include the role of environment in the resistance moment, the specifics of writing an implementation intention, or the difference between a rule and a commitment. The thirty-second window should appear in one script only.
**Severity:** Major

**Issue 4:** "Hedonic adaptation" is used with an imprecise definition in Script 6
**Location:** Script 6, paragraph 3 — *"This process is called hedonic adaptation. It is not a flaw in your character. It is a feature of human cognition. And it produces the same timeline every time: maximum engagement in week one, significant drop by week two, near-baseline by week three."*
**Problem:** Hedonic adaptation is a real and well-documented phenomenon, but it describes the diminishing emotional response to both positive and negative stimuli over time — not specifically the motivation-drop cycle in new habit formation (which is more accurately attributed to novelty-driven dopamine response decay or implementation intention failure). The script presents the term as if it precisely names the described mechanism. A psychologically literate viewer will notice the imprecision, and it creates a small but real credibility risk for a brand whose identity depends on getting the science right.
**Required fix:** Either (a) replace "hedonic adaptation" with a description of the actual mechanism — e.g., *"the novelty response — the spike of engagement your brain produces when something is new — is designed to fade"* — or (b) use the term but narrow its application: *"This is a version of hedonic adaptation — your brain's tendency to return to baseline as novelty fades."* Option (a) is preferable for this voice. Do not remove the mechanistic framing — that is one of the script's strengths. Only correct the label.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 5:** "Stop consuming. Start executing." in Script 14 approaches a motivational cliché
**Location:** Script 14, penultimate paragraph — *"Stop consuming. Start executing. One commitment, kept, today."*
**Problem:** The Voss voice is defined by its avoidance of motivational language. This line — while brief — is structurally a call-to-action that sounds like the kind of phrase that appears on motivational Instagram accounts. It is the only moment in the batch where the voice slips toward the register the brand explicitly rejects. The rest of Script 14 is strong; this landing undercuts it.
**Required fix:** Replace with a conclusion that maintains the analytical register. The script has been building toward the point that behaviour — specifically one kept commitment — is the mechanism of change. Land on the mechanism, not the instruction. Example direction (not a rewrite): close on the data-point logic already established in the script — the next kept commitment changes the record, and the record is the only thing that changes this.
**Severity:** Minor

**Issue 6:** Script 10's close defaults to familiar self-help rhythm

For a brand operating at the top 1% standard, the close of a script is where the voice should be most distinctly itself. These lines could appear in content from a dozen other creators. They do not feel like Voss.
**Required fix:** Revise the final three sentences to close on a specific, clinical observation consistent with the script's debriefing register. The script has been explaining mental toughness as a trainable capacity — close on the training logic, not the encouragement. Avoid "start now" constructions.
**Severity:** Minor
