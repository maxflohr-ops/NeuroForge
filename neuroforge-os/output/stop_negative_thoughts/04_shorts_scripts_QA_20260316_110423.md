### ISSUES FOUND

**Issue 1:** Script 16 truncated mid-sentence
**Location:** Script 16, body copy — ends at "What clinical trials on this technique consistently find is"
**Problem:** The script is incomplete and unpublishable. Thought scheduling (worry postponement) is a legitimate and well-supported clinical technique — this is actually one of the stronger topic choices in the batch. Truncating the mechanism description and omitting the action step, CTA, visual direction, text overlay, and captions means the script fails every production checklist item simultaneously.
**Required fix:** Return Script 16 to the Shorts Script Agent for completion. The brief should specify: finish the mechanism explanation (what the trials find), add the specific implementation step, close with the CTA, and complete all production elements (visual direction, text overlay, caption hook, TikTok caption, hashtags).
**Severity:** Critical — script is not reviewable in its current state and cannot proceed

**Issue 2:** Unverified citation — Joanne Wood / University of Waterloo affirmations study
**Location:** Script 12, body copy — "Research by Joanne Wood at the University of Waterloo found that positive self-statements produced more negative mood in people with low self-esteem"
**Problem:** This is a specific named researcher, specific institution, and specific finding. If fabricated or misattributed, this is a Red Flag — automatic fail. The claim itself is consistent with published research in this area (Wood et al. have published on this topic), but the framing as stated requires verification before publication. NeuroForge's credibility standard does not permit unverified named citations in published content.
**Required fix:** Verify the citation against the original source before publication. If confirmed accurate, add the year and publication for the human editor's reference notes (even if not spoken on screen). If unverifiable, instruct the agent to remove the named citation and reframe as: "Research in this area has found..." — the point holds without the attribution, and an unverifiable specific claim is worse than a general one.
**Severity:** Critical — potential Red Flag pending verification

---

**Issue 3:** Scripts 3 and 13 are substantively duplicative
**Location:** Script 3 (inner critic — origin, why it sounds authoritative, creating distance) and Script 13 (inner critic — grammar of self-criticism, observer distance)
**Problem:** Both scripts address the inner critic, both explain that it speaks in first person, both explain it was constructed from external environments, and both deliver the same technique (third-person labelling / "the critic is telling me"). The hooks are different but the content delivered is functionally the same. In a 20-script package, this wastes one slot and reduces the perceived range and value of the content. A viewer who watches both in sequence will feel they have been given the same video twice.
**Required fix:** One of these scripts must be significantly differentiated or replaced. Option A: Retain Script 3 (stronger on origin/construction of the inner critic) and replace Script 13 with a distinct inner critic angle — e.g., why the inner critic is loudest at transitions or high-stakes moments, or the difference between the inner critic and healthy self-evaluation. Option B: Retain Script 13 (stronger on the practical technique) and replace Script 3 with a different topic entirely. Do not simply change the hook — the body content must be distinct.
**Severity:** Major

**Location:** Script 5, final paragraph — "not perfectly. Not immediately. But consistently, over time, the exhaustion diminishes."
**Problem:** The body of Script 5 builds genuine tension around why rest doesn't restore the chronically ruminative brain — this is a strong mechanism reveal. The close deflates rather than lands.
