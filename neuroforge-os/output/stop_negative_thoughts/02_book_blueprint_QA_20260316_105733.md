### ISSUES FOUND

**Issue 1:** Two citations flagged as unverified within the blueprint itself
**Location:** Chapter 7 source note ("Craske, M.G. et al. for related CBT worry protocols — citation unverified"); Chapter 10 source note (Szabo & Hopkinson 2007 — "citation unverified — Manuscript Agent must locate before citing")
**Problem:** The blueprint correctly flags these as unverified, which is responsible. However, passing this blueprint to the Manuscript Agent with unverified citations still listed creates a live risk: under time pressure or instruction ambiguity, the Manuscript Agent may cite them speculatively. Fabricated or misattributed citations are a Red Flag under NeuroForge standards. The gap needs to be resolved at the blueprint stage, not deferred.
**Required fix:** Verify both citations before passing to the Manuscript Agent. If Craske et al. cannot be identified with a specific study title, year, and journal, replace with a verified alternative (e.g., a specific Borkovec study, or a Worry Exposure paper from the CBT literature). If Szabo & Hopkinson 2007 cannot be confirmed, either verify the full journal details or replace with a verified alternative on news consumption and stress — Bernstein et al. (2017, *Nature Human Behaviour*) on news negativity bias is a confirmed candidate. Do not pass to Manuscript Agent with "unverified" still in the source notes.
**Severity:** Critical

---

**Issue 2:** Credibility overstatement on the Lieberman labelling research
**Location:** Chapter 8, second key point: "This is not metaphor or folk wisdom. It is one of the more robustly replicated findings in affective neuroscience"
**Problem:** This evaluative framing will almost certainly be reproduced verbatim or near-verbatim in the manuscript prose. The replication picture for affect labelling research is more mixed than "robustly replicated" implies — Lieberman's fMRI findings are well-cited but the field has not achieved consensus-level replication status. If a reviewer, journalist, or competitor checks this claim, the overstatement is findable and damages credibility. NeuroForge's brand standard is never overclaiming.
**Required fix:** Replace "one of the more robustly replicated findings in affective neuroscience" with a more defensible framing such as "one of the more consistently supported findings in affective neuroscience" or "supported by a body of neuroimaging research." Add a Manuscript Agent instruction to reflect this moderated framing rather than escalating the claim in prose.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 3:** Chapter 3 and Chapter 5 content overlap risk
**Location:** Chapter 3 (*The Thought Is Not the Truth — Learning to Defuse*) and Chapter 5 (*When Thoughts Feel Like Facts — The Problem of Cognitive Fusion*)
**Problem:** The blueprint distinguishes these correctly in intent — Chapter 3 is technique delivery, Chapter 5 is mechanism explanation — but the core concept is the same: cognitive fusion/defusion. A Manuscript Agent working chapter by chapter may duplicate material, particularly the fusion-versus-defusion distinction, the language shift examples ("I am worthless" → "I am having the thought that..."), and the explanation of why the thought feels true. If the reader encounters this material twice in adjacent chapters without clear signal that they are hearing something new, the repetition reads as padding and undermines the book's credibility and momentum.
**Required fix:** Add an explicit instruction to the Chapter 5 brief specifying: (a) what content the Manuscript Agent must NOT repeat from Chapter 3 (the defusion language examples, the fusion definition, the ACT introduction); (b) what Chapter 5 adds that Chapter 3 does not — specifically the metacognitive shift (attending to the process of thinking, not its content) and the cognitive distortions taxonomy. This distinction should also be made explicit in the Chapter 3 transition sentence, which currently ends on defusion without signalling that a deeper mechanistic layer is still to come in Chapter 5.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 4:** Word count ceiling ambiguity
**Location:** Section 1 (stated target: 25,000–30,000 words); per-chapter word count targets throughout Section 6
**Problem:** The per-chapter word count targets (Section 6) sum to approximately 28,700–33,500 words for chapter content alone. Adding the introduction (1,500–2,000 words, already included in the sum), front matter, back matter, appendices (Appendix C is specified as 2–3 pages; Appendix B as approximately 60–80 terms; Appendix D as annotated), and the conclusion will push the total to 32,000–38,000 words — meaningfully above the stated 25,000–30,000 target. A Manuscript Agent working to per-chapter targets will overshoot the book's stated length without realising it.
**Required fix:** Either (a) revise the overall target upward to 32,000–36,000 words to match the chapter architecture as designed, or (b) reduce the word count ceiling on the three longest chapter targets (Chapters 3, 4, 5 — each currently 2,400–3,000) by 200–400 words each, and specify that appendices should not exceed 3,000 words combined. Add a total word count ceiling to Section 1 that the Manuscript Agent is explicitly instructed not to exceed.
**Severity:** Major

**Issue 6:** Manuscript Agent guidance for the conclusion scene return is underspecified
**Location:** Section 8 (*Back Matter Plan*), conclusion description: "opens by returning to the scene from the introduction — the same person, the same situation, but with a different response"
**Problem:** The instruction tells the Manuscript Agent *what* to do but not *how* to do it with sufficient specificity to prevent a generic execution. "A different relationship with the arising of the thought" is the correct framing intellectually, but without more specific guidance on what that looks, sounds, and reads like — what the person *does* differently, not just *feels* differently — the Manuscript Agent is likely to default to a vague emotional resolution that undercuts twelve chapters of behavioural precision. The conclusion's final sentence ("the goal was never silence — the goal was safety") is strong and should be preserved exactly.
**Required fix:** Add two to three sentences to the conclusion brief specifying the behavioural content of the "different response": for example, "the person notices the thought arrive, names it ('there's the spiral starting'), does not fight it, and returns attention to what they were doing — not because the thought is gone, but because they are no longer required to resolve it before continuing." The Manuscript Agent needs a concrete behavioural beat, not just a tonal direction.
**Severity:** Minor

### PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure observed:** Citation management. The blueprint correctly identified two unverified citations and flagged them — but the flag is a warning, not a resolution. The blueprint passed to QA with live risks embedded in the source notes. This is a process gap in the Research Agent or Book Architect Agent, not a failure of intent.

**Producing agent:** Book Architect Agent (blueprint stage); the unverified citations originate from the Research Agent's brief.

**Recommended prompt change:** Add the following instruction to the Book Architect Agent prompt, under the source note section:

> *"Before including any citation in a blueprint source note, verify that you can state the full reference (author, title, journal/publisher, year, and — for journal articles — volume and page range). If you cannot verify the full reference, do not include the citation. Instead, write: [CITATION NEEDED — topic: X — Research Agent must locate a verified source for this claim before Manuscript Agent begins]. Do not use 'citation unverified — locate before citing' as a placeholder. A blueprint that passes QA should contain no unverified citations — only citations that are fully verified, or clearly marked gaps that must be filled before the chapter is written."*

This change converts the current warning pattern into a hard stop at the blueprint stage rather than a deferred risk at the manuscript stage.
