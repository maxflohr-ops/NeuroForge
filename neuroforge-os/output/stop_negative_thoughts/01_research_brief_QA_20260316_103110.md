### VERDICT

**CONDITIONAL PASS**

The brief is strong in structure, voice, and specificity, but three credibility concerns in the hooks section — one statistic with a ten-fold range and two unsourced claim-signals — must be resolved before any downstream content is produced using these hooks.

---

### ISSUES FOUND

**Issue 1:** Unverifiable statistic with implausible range
**Location:** Hook 4 — *"The average person has somewhere between 6,000 and 60,000 thoughts per day"*
**Problem:** A ten-fold range is not a finding — it is a failure of sourcing. This figure circulates widely online and is frequently cited without a traceable primary source. If this statistic appears in a published script or chapter under Dr. Nova Vale's name and a reader checks it, the credibility of the brand is damaged. The phrase "research consistently shows" compounds the problem by asserting consensus that does not exist for this specific claim.
**Required fix:** Either locate and confirm the primary source for this statistic and replace the range with the figure from that source, or remove the statistic entirely and rewrite the hook around the conceptual point (negativity bias, repetitive thought patterns) without citing a number that cannot be defended.
**Severity:** Critical

---

**Issue 2:** Claim-signals presented as implied citations
**Location:** Hook 5 — *"a well-documented psychological effect called the rebound effect"*; Hook 6 — *"Studies on emotional labelling show that simply naming what you're feeling...measurably reduces activity in the brain's threat response"*
**Problem:** Both hooks are written in a register that implies specific sourced evidence. Hook 5 is directionally accurate (the white bear suppression studies by Wegner are real and relevant), but "well-documented" as a standalone assertion, without a name or study attached, is exactly the kind of phrasing that gets challenged in comments sections and damages faculty credibility. Hook 6's phrasing ("studies show...measurably reduces") refers accurately to work by Matthew Lieberman on affect labelling, but that work is not cited, and the hook as written could mislead a content agent downstream into treating this as a verified, citable finding when it has not been confirmed for use.
**Required fix:** For Hook 5: either name the Wegner suppression research explicitly, or rewrite to "there's a documented effect — the harder you try to suppress a thought, the more reliably it returns" without the word "well-documented" as a credibility prop. For Hook 6: either add a source note (e.g., "Lieberman, 2007 — verify before use") flagged for the downstream agent, or rewrite the hook to remove the implied citation while preserving the mechanism: *"Naming what you're feeling — precisely, not generally — reduces its intensity. Not because it fixes anything. Because it changes what your brain does with it."*
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 3:** One redundant chapter angle
**Location:** Angle 2 (*"The difference between a thought and a fact"*) and Angle 9 (*"Emotional labelling as a neurological intervention"*) are distinct. However, Angle 2 and Angle 13 (*"Acceptance vs. resignation — why letting a thought be there isn't the same as agreeing with it"*) substantially overlap. Both are teaching the reader to detach from the content of a thought without endorsing it. The framing differs, but the cognitive work is nearly identical — a reader who has absorbed Angle 2 has largely received what Angle 13 teaches.
**Required fix:** Either consolidate these two angles into a single, more layered chapter angle, or differentiate them more sharply. One possible distinction: Angle 2 addresses the thought-as-fact problem (cognition), while Angle 13 addresses the fear-of-acceptance problem (resistance). If that distinction is made explicit in the angle description, both can remain.
**Severity:** Minor

---

**Issue 4:** Two desired outcomes use vague language inconsistent with the brief's own standard
**Location:** Desired Outcome 1 — *"feel genuinely unintimidated by it"*; Desired Outcome 7 — *"feel, over time, that the mind is a place they can be without bracing for something bad"*
**Problem:** The pain points section sets a high bar for specificity ("I replay a conversation from three days ago on a loop"). These two outcomes don't meet that bar. "Unintimidated" and "a place they can be without bracing" are felt states, not behavioural outcomes — which means they cannot be used as concrete chapter promises, exercise targets, or funnel copy without further translation. They are also closer to the kind of aspirational wellness language the brief otherwise avoids.
**Required fix:** Rewrite both to describe a behavioural or observable change rather than an internal feeling. For example, Outcome 1 could become: "To notice a negative thought arise, name what it is, and return to the task at hand within two minutes — without needing to resolve it first." Outcome 7 could become: "To end a day without having spent significant mental bandwidth on a thought that produced no decision and no action."
**Severity:** Minor

---

**Issue 5:** Internal quality checklist mark is inconsistent with brief content
**Location:** Section 10, checklist item — *"No fake citations or fabricated statistics included"* — marked as passed
**Problem:** As noted in Issues 1 and 2 above, Hook 4 contains an unsourced statistic with a ten-fold range, and Hooks 5 and 6 contain implied citation signals without sourcing. The checklist item should not have been marked as passed. This is a process integrity concern: if the self-check is passing items it should flag, the human editor may not know to look closely at these sections.
**Required fix:** Uncheck this item and add a note: *"Hook 4 statistic requires primary source verification. Hooks 5 and 6 contain unsourced claim-signals — flag for research verification before downstream use."* The checklist should reflect actual status, not aspirational status.
**Severity:** Major

### PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** The Research Agent correctly identified and described mechanisms (rebound effect, affect labelling) but did not distinguish between "this is a real and citable finding" and "this is directionally accurate but not yet sourced for brand use." As a result, hook copy was written in a register that implies citation without providing it — which passes a cursory internal check but would create credibility risk if used downstream without review.

**Producing agent:** Research Agent

**Recommended prompt addition:** Add the following instruction to the Research Agent's hooks section guidance:

*"When writing Stat or Fact hooks, you must do one of the following: (a) provide the primary source name and year alongside the hook, flagged as a note for QA review, or (b) rewrite the hook so it does not imply sourced evidence — using language like 'there is a pattern' or 'the experience is consistent' rather than 'research shows' or 'studies confirm.' Never write a hook that implies a specific citation that is not named. If you are unsure whether a finding is accurately attributed, write the hook without the citation and flag it: [SOURCE NEEDED — verify before use]."*

This single instruction would have prevented Issues 1 and 2 in this brief.
