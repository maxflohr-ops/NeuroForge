**SCRIPT 6**
**Hook:** There is a documented psychological effect called the rebound effect. The harder you try to push a thought out, the higher the frequency it returns.
**Faculty:** Dr. Nova Vale
**Topic:** The ironic process theory — why suppression amplifies the thought
**Estimated Duration:** 50 seconds

---

**In the 1980s, a psychologist named Daniel Wegner ran a simple experiment. He told participants: do not think about a white bear.**

They thought about almost nothing else.

What he found was that the act of monitoring your own mind for the presence of a thought — which is exactly what suppression requires — keeps that thought more active, not less. You need to hold the thought in mind to check that it isn't there. The checking is the thinking.

This is the ironic process. Your brain's attempt to suppress a thought creates the very condition that maintains it.

Most people dealing with intrusive thoughts have been fighting them for years. And the fighting is part of the loop. Not because they're doing it wrong — because suppression is structurally incapable of working.

What works instead is deliberate non-engagement. Not suppression. Not distraction. Not argument. You notice the thought. You name it. And you redirect your attention — not because the thought has been resolved, but because you are choosing not to give it the fuel it needs to persist.

You can't delete a thought. You can stop feeding it.

**There are two pathways your brain uses to process a potential threat.**

It also means this: the goal of intervention is not to reason your way out of the feeling. It's to give the nervous system a different signal once the alarm has sounded. Slow breathing works here — not because it changes the thought, but because it signals safety to the system that fired before your thinking mind arrived.

Different tool. Different target.

**SCRIPT 9**
**Hook:** There is a difference between productive worry and rumination. One of them is worth nothing at all.
**Faculty:** Dr. Nova Vale
**Topic:** How to distinguish useful thinking from recursive loops
**Estimated Duration:** 50 seconds

---

**Not all negative thinking is a problem. Some of it is your brain identifying something that needs action.**

Productive worry is future-oriented, specific, and terminates in a decision or an action. "I haven't responded to that email — I should do it today." That's the system working correctly.

Rumination is different. It circles the same material without moving toward resolution. It doesn't produce an action. It produces only more thinking. "Why did I say that. What must they think of me. The question that separates them is simple: is there anything I could actually do about this right now?

If yes — do it, or schedule it, and the thought can rest.

If no — there is no action available. Everything from this point is recycling, not processing. And recycling has a cost. It depletes you without producing anything.

This isn't about dismissing real problems. It's about recognising when the thinking has stopped serving the problem and started serving itself.

**SCRIPT 16**

**Estimated Duration:** 55 seconds

**Thought scheduling sounds strange until you try it. Then it sounds obvious.**
