### ISSUES FOUND

**Issue 1:** Unverified mechanistic claim — reintroduction timeline
**Location:** Chapter 8, Core Idea: *"Unstructured reintroduction — returning to previous inputs on the same terms as before — reliably produces a return to pre-reset threshold levels within one to two weeks."*
**Problem:** "One to two weeks" is a specific, quantified claim about the speed of threshold re-elevation following unstructured reintroduction. It is stated as established fact. There is no source attributed, and the manuscript agent will inherit this claim and write it into the chapter as authoritative. If this figure is not grounded in peer-reviewed research, it constitutes overclaiming — a credibility violation. The number may well be directionally correct, but "reliably" and "one to two weeks" together are a liability if challenged.
**Required fix:** The Research Agent must verify whether a specific timeframe for threshold re-elevation post-reset exists in the literature. If it does, the source must be logged in the chapter brief. If it does not, the claim must be reframed: replace "one to two weeks" with "within days to weeks, depending on input load" and remove "reliably." The directional claim survives; the false precision does not.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 2:** Unverified and over-specified mechanistic claim — sleep and dopamine restoration
**Location:** Chapter 11, Core Idea: *"There is a natural dopamine sensitivity restoration mechanism that operates during sleep — specifically during the deep sleep stages that are most vulnerable to the blue-light and arousal effects of nighttime screen use."*
**Location also:** Chapter 11, Key Points: *"The overnight dopamine restoration mechanism: which sleep stage is involved and what it requires to function."*
**Problem:** This claim does two things the blueprint needs to be able to support: (1) it asserts a specific sleep-stage mechanism for dopamine sensitivity restoration; (2) it promises the manuscript will name which sleep stage. The relationship between sleep and dopamine function is real and documented, but the specific formulation — "dopamine sensitivity restoration" occurring in a named deep sleep stage — is more precise than the general literature may support without careful sourcing. If the manuscript agent writes this chapter and names a specific sleep stage as the primary dopamine reset mechanism, and that claim cannot be sourced to peer-reviewed research, it is a fabrication risk regardless of plausibility. This is the most significant credibility issue in the blueprint because the entire chapter is built on this premise.
**Required fix:** Before the Chapter 11 manuscript brief is issued, the Research Agent must source the specific claim. The brief must arrive with: (a) the named sleep stage and a cited mechanism, or (b) a reframed premise — for example, "chronic sleep disruption is associated with reduced dopamine receptor sensitivity, with deep sleep stages playing a documented role in neurochemical restoration" — that stays within what the literature can support. The chapter promise can hold; the mechanism must be verifiable.
**Severity:** Critical

---

**Issue 3:** "Minimum viable detox" risks undermining the central argument
**Location:** Chapter 5, Key Points: *"The minimum viable detox: what the framework looks like for someone who cannot run a full aggressive reset — and whether a minimum viable version is sufficient to produce measurable recalibration."*
**Problem:** Chapter 3's entire purpose is to establish that partial resets fail because the threshold remains elevated as long as high-stimulus inputs are present. Chapter 5 then introduces a "minimum viable detox" and asks whether it "is sufficient to produce measurable recalibration." If the answer is yes, it directly contradicts Chapter 3's core argument. If the answer is no, it raises the question of why it is in the book at all. The inclusion of this point is not wrong in intent — accommodating readers who face real-world constraints is legitimate — but as currently framed it creates a logical tension that the manuscript agent will have to resolve mid-chapter without a clear steer.
**Required fix:** The blueprint must specify the answer in advance: either (a) "The minimum viable detox produces partial recalibration — enough to lower the threshold somewhat, but not enough to produce the full reset described in Chapter 4. It is included as a stepping-stone, not an equivalent alternative" — or (b) "The minimum viable detox is not recommended; it is included to name the failure mode explicitly so readers who cannot run a full reset understand what they are trading." The manuscript agent must not be left to resolve this question during drafting.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 4:** Chapter 7 exercise — "not meditates, not reflects, not journals" creates an instruction gap
**Location:** Chapter 7, Exercise: *"The reader sits — not meditates, not reflects, not journals — and records three things afterward."*
**Problem:** The negative instruction ("not meditates, not reflects") is clear in the blueprint, but without a positive instruction for what the reader is actually doing during the 20 minutes, the manuscript agent may write this into the chapter without resolving the instruction gap. "Sits" is not sufficient direction for a practice that runs for 20 minutes. A reader who has never sat with no agenda, no input, and no practice framework will not know what to do with their eyes, body, or attention, and the absence of instruction will either produce anxiety (which drives relapse) or get filled in by the reader with something that resembles meditation anyway.
**Required fix:** The blueprint should add a one-sentence positive instruction for the sit: what the reader does with their body, eyes, and attention during the 20 minutes. Example direction (not a rewrite, a steer for the manuscript agent): "Reader sits in a fixed position with eyes open or softly closed, no particular focus, no attempt to direct thought — the only instruction is to not reach for input." This does not need to be elaborated here, but it must be specified so the manuscript agent can write it precisely.
**Severity:** Minor

---

**Issue 5:** Voice drift — one instance of emotional framing in a Kai Ren document
**Location:** Chapter 3, Key Points: *"Why knowing this is not enough: the knowing-doing gap as an architecture problem, not an information problem."*
**Problem:** This point is correctly framed. The issue is the preceding point: *"The relapse pattern: how returning to high-stimulus input after brief abstinence feels intensified — and what that tells us about threshold vs. tolerance."* "Feels intensified" is the only subjective/affective framing in the entire blueprint. Every other formulation is behavioural or mechanistic. This is a minor inconsistency, but in a Kai Ren document it signals drift. "Feels intensified" should be expressed as what happens behaviourally or neurochemically, not as a feeling.
**Required fix:** Rephrase to: *"The relapse pattern: why returning to high-stimulus input after brief abstinence produces elevated engagement response — and what this indicates about threshold mechanics vs. tolerance."* One word change and a structural reframe.
**Severity:** Minor

**Issue 6:** Chapter 9 chapter promise is slightly under-specified relative to the rest of the blueprint
**Location:** Chapter 9, Chapter Promise: *"The reader understands why deep work still feels difficult even after a successful reset — and has a specific framework for restructuring task engagement so that meaningful work produces a genuine reward signal rather than a neutral or aversive one."*
**Problem:** Every other chapter promise in the blueprint ends with a behavioural or output-specific result: the reader "produces a complete ranked map," "has a specific tiered decision framework," "executes a structured staged reintroduction." Chapter 9's promise ends with "produces a genuine reward signal rather than a neutral or aversive one" — which is a mechanistic outcome, not a behavioural one, and is not measurable from the outside. It also cannot be confirmed by the reader during the exercise. This is a minor issue but creates an inconsistency in the blueprint's otherwise strong commitment to behavioural specificity.
**Required fix:** Reframe the chapter promise to end with the observable behavioural output: "...and has redesigned the structure of their three most important recurring work tasks so that each one has a defined start signal, a visible progress metric, and a completion event the reward system can register." This aligns with what the Task Architecture Template actually produces.
**Severity:** Minor

### PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** The blueprint's two most significant issues (Issues 1 and 2) share the same root cause: the Book Architect Agent is generating specific mechanistic claims — quantified timelines, named sleep stages, specific neurochemical processes — without flagging them as requiring source verification before the manuscript brief is issued. These are not fabrications; they are plausible inferences from the general science. But a blueprint that asserts "one to two weeks" or "the deep sleep stage responsible for dopamine sensitivity restoration" without a source is issuing a promissory note to the manuscript agent that the Research Agent may not be able to honour.

**Producing agent:** Book Architect Agent

**Recommended prompt change:** Add the following constraint to the Book Architect Agent prompt, in the chapter structure section:

*"If a Key Point includes a specific quantified claim (a timeframe, a percentage, a named mechanism, a named neurochemical process or sleep stage), flag it in the blueprint with [VERIFY] and a one-sentence description of what the Research Agent needs to confirm before the manuscript brief is issued. Do not present mechanistic specifics as established fact unless they are attributable to widely documented consensus. The manuscript agent will inherit every claim in this blueprint — flag anything that needs sourcing before that handoff."*

This single addition would have caught both Issues 1 and 2 at the blueprint stage and prevented them from propagating into the manuscript.
