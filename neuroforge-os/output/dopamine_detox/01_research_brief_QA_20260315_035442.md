## ISSUES FOUND

---

**Issue 1:** Unverified specific statistic
**Location:** Section 7, Hook 4 — *"The average person now switches tasks every 47 seconds. That number was 2.5 minutes a decade ago."*
**Problem:** This is a highly specific, comparative claim with a precise figure and a temporal comparison. Specific statistics like this are the most common vector for fabricated or misattributed citations in AI-produced content. If this is real, it needs a source attached to the brief so any downstream content that uses it can cite it correctly. If it cannot be sourced, it must be removed or softened to a directional claim without the specific figure. Publishing a precise statistic that turns out to be invented or misattributed is a credibility violation that is very difficult to recover from — particularly on a topic where the audience is already sceptical of pseudoscience claims (see Objection 3 in Section 6, which the brief itself anticipates).
**Required fix:** Research agent must provide the original source for this statistic (author, institution, publication, year) before this hook can be cleared for use. If no source can be verified, the hook must be rewritten to remove the specific figures. A directional version — "Your attention window has shrunk significantly in the last decade — and the mechanism behind it is well-documented" — is acceptable as a fallback, though less powerful.
**Severity:** Critical

---

**Issue 2:** Unattributed "studies" reference
**Location:** Section 7, Hook 6 — *"In studies on reward sensitivity, reduced dopamine baseline doesn't just make you less motivated — it makes ordinary tasks register as physically unpleasant."*
**Problem:** "Studies on reward sensitivity" is unfalsifiable as written. It names no researcher, no institution, no field of study, and no publication. This phrasing is a common AI-generated credibility signal that implies scientific backing without actually providing it. If this claim is grounded in real neuroscience, the brief should identify the specific research tradition or researcher it is drawing from so it can be accurately represented in downstream content. If it cannot be grounded, the hook needs to be reframed as a mechanistic explanation rather than a citation-implied finding.
**Required fix:** Identify the specific research this claim references (e.g., reward sensitivity literature, dopamine depletion studies, anhedonia research) or reframe the hook to remove the implied citation. Acceptable reframe: lead with the mechanism directly rather than anchoring it to unnamed studies. The claim about tasks registering as physically unpleasant is credible and usable — it just cannot be presented as study-backed without a real study behind it.
**Severity:** Major

## PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** The producing agent self-verified the brief's credibility by ticking the "no fake citations or fabricated statistics" checkbox before the brief had been externally reviewed. At the same time, the brief contains one specific comparative statistic (the 47-second task-switching figure) and one unnamed "studies" reference that have not been sourced. This is not necessarily fabrication — it may be accurately recalled data — but the agent cannot verify its own citations, and the self-certification creates false confidence.

**Producing agent:** Research Agent

**Recommended prompt change:** Add the following instruction to the Research Agent prompt, in the statistics and sourcing section:

*"If you include a specific statistic (any figure, percentage, or comparative data point), you must flag it inline with [NEEDS SOURCE VERIFICATION] immediately after the claim. Do not self-certify statistics as verified. Any reference to 'studies show' or 'research suggests' must either name the specific research tradition, researcher, or publication — or be rewritten to remove the implied citation. The QA agent will flag all unverified statistics regardless of plausibility."*

This change would eliminate the category of issue found here without requiring the Research Agent to have access to verified academic databases — it simply shifts the burden of verification to the correct stage of the pipeline (QA and human editor) rather than allowing the producing agent to inadvertently certify what it cannot confirm.
