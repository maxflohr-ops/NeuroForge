## ISSUES FOUND

---

**Issue 1:** Unqualified quantitative claim — peak discomfort window
**Location:** Script 7, paragraph 4: *"the peak discomfort window is hours 18 to 36. After 72 hours of reduced stimulus input, reported discomfort drops significantly for most people."*
**Problem:** These figures are stated as established data ("Here's what the data actually shows"). The 18–36 hour and 72-hour windows are not sourced from a specific study and appear to be approximations at best. Presenting them as data points — especially with the framing "here's what the data actually shows" — is a credibility violation. If a viewer or journalist asks for the source, there is none. This is the exact class of pseudo-precision that undermines the brand's credibility positioning. The dopamine detox literature is also contested; presenting specific hour-windows as settled science is overclaiming.
**Required fix:** Remove the specific hour figures entirely, or reframe them explicitly as approximate and experiential rather than data-derived. Replace "Here's what the data actually shows" with a framing that signals this is a reported pattern, not a measured finding. For example: "Most people who complete a full reset report the pull is at its worst in the first 36 hours — and noticeably softer by day three." No fabricated precision, no implied citation.
**Severity:** Critical

---

**Issue 2:** Unqualified quantitative claim — recalibration timeline
**Location:** Script 6, Carousel 1 (Slide 6), and multiple other instances across the batch: *"within 72 hours of reduced stimulus input, the shift begins"* / *"typically 72 hours minimum for early signal, seven days for meaningful reset"* (Script 16)
**Problem:** The 72-hour and 7-day figures recur across at least five assets and are stated as established thresholds rather than heuristics. Script 16 frames these as protocol parameters ("typically 72 hours minimum for early signal, seven days for meaningful reset") with clinical specificity that implies empirical grounding. The actual neuroscience on dopamine receptor recalibration timelines in humans is not this precise for general populations, and the specific figures are not traceable to published research. Repeated across the batch, this creates a cumulative credibility risk — the brand appears to be citing a study that does not exist. This is distinct from Issue 1 but shares the same failure mode: numbers stated as facts that are actually estimates.
**Required fix:** Across all instances, reframe these figures as working estimates based on reported experience, not measured outcomes. "Most people notice a shift within the first three days" is defensible. "Within 72 hours of reduced stimulus input, the shift begins" implies measurement. Remove the implied precision. A single consistent framing note should be applied to all instances in the batch before publication.
**Severity:** Critical

**Issue 3:** Word count exceeds 150-word limit for 60-second scripts
**Location:** Script 6 (estimated ~180 words in spoken body), Script 8 (~175 words), Script 18 (~185 words)
**Problem:** The system specification states body copy should be under 150 words for a 60-second script. Scripts 6, 8, and 18 are estimated at 50-second, 55-second, and 55-second durations respectively, but their spoken word counts exceed 150 words when the body text is counted. At a natural speaking pace of approximately 130–140 words per minute for Kai Ren's measured delivery style, these scripts will run over their stated duration. The visual direction will either be rushed or the copy will require on-set cuts — neither of which serves production.
**Required fix:** Each of the three scripts requires a word count reduction to bring spoken body copy to 150 words or below. The editor should identify the least load-bearing sentence in each script and remove it whole. Do not compress or condense — removal is cleaner. Script 6 candidate for removal: the final sentence of paragraph 3 ("You're asking your brain to engage with a signal that falls below the floor it's learned to expect.") — it restates the prior sentence. Script 8 candidate for removal: the final sentence of paragraph 2 ("This is not an accident.") — it adds no information. Script 18 candidate for removal: the second sentence of paragraph 3 ("Not a new high-intensity habit — that just substitutes one calibration problem for another.") — this idea is implicit in what follows.
**Severity:** Major

**Issue 4:** Caption hook does not match script content
**Location:** Script 2, Caption Hook: *"You sat down to work. Within 90 seconds you were somewhere else. You didn't decide to go."*
**Problem:** The hook line in the script body reads: *"You sit down to work. Within 90 seconds, you're on a different tab. You didn't decide to go there."* The caption hook changes "you're on a different tab" to "you were somewhere else" — shifting from the specific, visual behaviour (the tab switch) to a vague paraphrase. "Somewhere else" is generic. "A different tab" is the exact specificity that makes the hook work, because it names the precise moment. This is a minor inconsistency in this instance, but caption hooks should match the hook line precisely or be clearly more specific than the script hook — never less specific.
**Required fix:** Restore the caption hook to match the script hook exactly, or tighten it further. Acceptable version: *"You sat down to work. Within 90 seconds you were on a different tab. You didn't decide to go there."*
**Severity:** Minor

**Issue 5:** Motivational register drift — two instances
**Location:** Script 5, final paragraph: *"That person who could read for two hours and feel satisfied at the end of it — they're not gone. The settings are wrong."* / Script 15, paragraph 1: *"Habits are pressure that accumulates over time, and pressure reasserts itself."*
**Problem:** Both instances represent a slight drift from Kai Ren's clinical, systems-level voice into a warmer, more reassuring register. "They're not gone" is the kind of phrase that belongs to a more emotionally supportive persona — it's close to the motivational language the brand explicitly bans. It reads as encouragement rather than diagnosis. The pressure/reasserts metaphor in Script 15 is vague and slightly literary in a way that doesn't match Kai Ren's precision elsewhere. Neither is a ban-word violation, but both lower the voice consistency score.
**Required fix:** Script 5 final line: reframe the reassurance as a systems observation rather than emotional support. Suggested direction (not a rewrite): instead of "they're not gone," close on the mechanism — something that says the same thing but from the outside rather than the inside. Script 15: replace the pressure metaphor with a mechanism statement — the point is that without maintained architecture, the old input pattern returns because the environment hasn't changed. State that directly.
**Severity:** Minor

**Issue 6:** Repetition across mechanism-led scripts — recalibration concept used as universal answer
**Location:** Scripts 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10 — the term "recalibration" and the concept of "lowering the baseline" appears as the conclusion of every script in this section without differentiation
**Problem:** Each mechanism-led script explains a different facet of the dopamine problem but arrives at the same conclusion: reduce the baseline, recalibration occurs. By Script 10, a viewer who has watched the batch in sequence has heard the same answer five times. The issue isn't that the answer is wrong — it's that the mechanism scripts fail to differentiate their payoff. The originality score holds up for the batch as a whole because the hook-led and tool-led scripts are fresher, but the mechanism section reads as five versions of the same script.
**Required fix:** At minimum, Scripts 8, 9, and 10 need differentiated closing logic. Script 8's close should land on the structural point specific to variable reward (why "checking less" is insufficient — which it already does well, but then diffuses). Script 9 should close on the specific compounding mechanism of sleep-based sensitivity restoration. Script 10 already has the strongest differentiated close ("architecture, not moderation") and should remain as-is. The fix is sharpening the final turn of Scripts 8 and 9 so each closes on its specific mechanism, not on the general recalibration concept. This is a revision instruction, not a rewrite request.
**Severity:** Minor

**Issue 7:** Carousel 2, Slide 3 — specificity claim adjacent to Issue 1
**Location:** Carousel 2, Slide 3: *"Peak discomfort. The urge to return to high-stimulus inputs is at its highest. This is where most relapses happen — and also where the recalibration is doing its most significant work."*
**Problem:** This repeats the "peak discomfort" framing from Script 7 without the specific hour figures, which partially resolves the Issue 1 problem. However, the claim that "this is where the recalibration is doing its most significant work" is still presented as a factual statement about neurobiology without qualification. It may be true; it may be a simplification. As written it implies clinical knowledge of which phase of recalibration is most neurologically significant, which is not established. This is a lower-severity instance of the same credibility category as Issues 1 and 2.
**Required fix:** Reframe as an observational claim: "This is also, based on what we know about recalibration, the phase where the most significant neurological adjustment is occurring" — or more simply, qualify it as a working model rather than a measured fact.
**Severity:** Minor

## PASSED ELEMENTS

1. **Script 4's wanting/pleasure distinction is the strongest conceptual moment in the batch.** The framing — "dopamine is not the pleasure chemical, it's the wanting chemical" — is both accurate and genuinely under-used in short-form content at this level. It gives the viewer a cognitive tool they will actually remember and repeat. The follow-through ("you're not chasing pleasure, you're chasing the search for it") earns the hook.

2. **Script 14's knowing-doing gap reframe is well-executed.** Naming the gap as an architecture problem rather than a knowledge problem or a willpower problem is a precise and original angle that does real work for the brand's positioning. The line "the behaviour happens first, the rationalisation comes after" is one of the strongest text overlays in the batch.

3. **Visual direction is consistently actionable and on-voice throughout.** Unlike many script batches where visual direction defaults to "speaker talks to camera," every script here has a specific direction — stillness, pacing notes, text overlay trigger points, and in several cases minimal graphic inserts with duration specified. This is production-ready at the level of a brief, not a placeholder.

4. **The hook writing is consistently strong and format-native.** Across all 20 scripts, no hook begins with "In today's video," no hook is a repurposed blog title, and the majority name a specific behaviour or moment rather than a general topic. The specificity — "11 days," "within 90 seconds," "three hours of content you didn't enjoy" — is exactly the right register for short-form content of this type.

5. **Carousel 5 (Environmental Design) is the strongest carousel in the batch.** Each rule is stated as a specific instruction, not a principle. "Phone out of the bedroom — not on silent, not face-down, in a different room" is the right level of granularity. The contrast between architecture and intention is made concrete at each step. This carousel could stand alone as a lead magnet asset without modification.

## PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** The producing agent (Shorts Script Agent) has a consistent tendency to add quantitative specificity — hour windows, day counts, percentage-adjacent language — to claims about neurological processes that do not have that precision in the published literature. This appears across Scripts 7, 9, 16, and Carousel 2, and in the carousel copy. The failure mode is not fabricating citations (no named researchers or studies appear) but fabricating precision: presenting working estimates as measured data. This is a subtler credibility violation than a fake citation, but it compounds across a batch when the same numbers recur.

**Producing agent:** Shorts Script Agent

**Recommended prompt change:** Add the following constraint to the Shorts Script Agent prompt under its credibility rules:

*"If a script includes any number, timeframe, or quantitative threshold relating to neurological or psychological processes (e.g. '72 hours,' 'hours 18 to 36,' '7 days for meaningful reset'), it must be flagged for QA review. These figures may only appear in final copy if they are (a) directly traceable to a named published study already approved in the Source Library, or (b) explicitly framed as working estimates based on reported experience, not measured outcomes. Default framing for all timeline claims: 'most people report…' or 'the working estimate is…' — not 'the data shows' or 'within X hours.'"*

This change would prevent both Issue 1 and Issue 2 from recurring in future batches, and would catch the lower-severity instance in Carousel 2 Slide 3 before it reached QA.
