## ISSUES FOUND

---

**Issue 1:** Unverifiable precision in a mechanism claim
**Location:** Email 2, paragraph 7 — *"your phone has been engineered specifically to produce one every thirty seconds"*
**Problem:** "Every thirty seconds" is stated as a specific fact describing how phones deliver reward signals. This is not a cited figure — it is a rhetorical precision that sounds like data. If a reader challenges this, there is nothing to back it up. For a brand whose credibility depends on never overclaiming, a specific-sounding number deployed without a source is a liability. It works as rhetoric but fails as credibility. The copy is trying to sound empirical while being impressionistic.
**Required fix:** Remove the specific interval. Replace with a construction that preserves the point without implying a measured figure — e.g., *"your phone has been engineered to produce one at a frequency your reward system cannot habituate to"* or *"engineered to produce one faster than your brain can stop expecting the next one."* The mechanism argument does not require the number to land.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 2:** Section 5 is a self-assessment QA block, not reviewable content — and it should not be included in a submission to QA
**Location:** Section 5 — *"FUNNEL COPY QUALITY CHECK"* — entire section
**Problem:** The producing agent has included its own internal quality checklist, pre-ticked, as part of the submission. This creates a structural problem: a QA report cannot independently verify claims made by the agent about its own output. Several of the self-assessments in this section are accurate (the bullets are indeed behavioural; the email sequence does build before selling). But some are not straightforwardly true — notably the claim that *"all copy matches Kai Ren's assigned tone"* and the claim that there is *"no fake urgency."* The introductory price urgency claim (addressed in Issue 3 below) required independent review to catch. A self-ticked checklist that passes the urgency item without flagging the specific copy creates false confidence at the handoff. More broadly: if the producing agent's own quality check is included in submissions, it may lead human editors to skip re-checking items that the agent has already ticked. The self-assessment belongs in internal logs, not in the submission package.
**Required fix:** Remove Section 5 from all future funnel copy submissions before they reach QA. The producing agent's internal checklist is a production tool, not a deliverable. Flag this to the Funnel Agent's submission protocol.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 3:** The introductory price urgency claim is unverifiable and potentially false scarcity
**Location:** Thank You Page, Book Description block — *"This is an introductory price. It will go up."*
**Problem:** The self-assessment in Section 5 passes this as legitimate. On review, it is only legitimate if the price genuinely will increase and there is a documented plan to do so. If this is standard copy applied to all NeuroForge book launches regardless of whether pricing will change, it is false scarcity — a Red Flag category violation for funnel copy. The copy itself contains no mechanism to verify this. Unlike a countdown timer (which is visibly manufactured), this claim is stated as fact in declarative prose. A reader who buys at $17, returns in three months, and finds the price unchanged will register the brand as having lied to them.
**Required fix:** Either (a) confirm there is a documented plan to raise the price and add a specific date or threshold to the copy to make the claim accountable — e.g., *"$17 through [date] — then $27"* — or (b) remove the line entirely. Do not retain the line in its current form unless the pricing plan is confirmed and documented. Flag to Funnel Agent and editorial team for sign-off.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 4:** Google Ads headlines require character count verification
**Location:** Section 4, Google Ads — all three ads, all headline fields
**Problem:** Google Search Ads allow a maximum of 30 characters per headline. The following headlines have not been verified and appear to exceed or approach the limit in a way that requires confirmation before production:

- Ad 1, Headline 2: *"Free 7-Day Protocol + Diagnostic"* — 32 characters. **Exceeds limit.**
- Ad 2, Headline 2: *"It's a Threshold Problem, Not Willpower"* — 39 characters. **Exceeds limit.**
- Ad 3, Headline 2: *"Diagnostic + 7-Day Plan. Free."* — 30 characters. Borderline — verify.
- Ad 3, Headline 3: *"Know Your Stack. Fix the Threshold."* — 35 characters. **Exceeds limit.**

A Google Ad with headlines over 30 characters will be rejected or auto-truncated at upload, producing broken display copy. This is a production-blocking issue if it reaches the media buyer unchecked.
**Required fix:** All three ads must be character-counted against the 30-character headline limit before handoff. Any headline exceeding 30 characters must be revised. Description fields (90 characters max) should also be confirmed — several descriptions run close to the limit and should be verified.
**Severity:** Critical (production-blocking)

---

**Issue 5:** The thermostat analogy in Email 3 is the weakest moment in the sequence and represents a minor voice drift
**Location:** Email 3, paragraph 6 — *"If you've been running your thermostat at 85 degrees and you set it back to 68, the house doesn't immediately feel comfortable…"*
**Problem:** This is the one moment in the funnel where the copy reaches for a familiar self-help analogy rather than staying inside the mechanism. Kai Ren's voice throughout the rest of this sequence earns its authority by describing the process directly — not by reaching for an everyday object to explain it. The thermostat comparison is not wrong, but it is the kind of analogy that appears in any stress management blog post or productivity newsletter. It slightly softens the clinical precision that makes the rest of Email 3 the strongest piece in the sequence. It also creates a minor tonal inconsistency: the sentence that follows — *"The boredom and restlessness of day two is the neurological equivalent of that adjustment period"* — effectively says the same thing the analogy just said, making the analogy redundant as well as generic.
**Required fix:** Cut the thermostat analogy (the full paragraph, from *"Think about it this way"* through *"the system is adjusting"*) and move directly to *"The boredom and restlessness of day two is the neurological equivalent of that adjustment period."* The mechanism explanation that follows is sufficient — the analogy adds nothing and costs voice consistency.
**Severity:** Minor

## PASSED ELEMENTS

1. **The landing page bullet structure is the strongest in its category.** Every bullet follows the format [specific outcome] — [specific mechanism], and no bullet uses feeling-language. The fifth bullet — *"Execute the reintroduction checklist to bring inputs back without collapsing the reset"* — is particularly well-constructed because it names a failure mode ("collapsing the reset") that the reader has already experienced, rather than promising a positive outcome they haven't.

2. **The email sequence observes the trust-build rule with discipline.** Email 1 delivers genuine value and makes no offer. Email 2 deepens the diagnostic frame with no offer. The book appears in Email 3 only after the highest-value insight in the sequence has already been delivered. By the time Email 4 names specific chapters, the reader has received three consecutive emails that gave them something useful. This is the correct sequence and it is correctly executed.

3. **Email 2's description of the threshold decision moment is the strongest single piece of copy in the package.** *"The task hasn't failed yet. You haven't struggled yet. You've just started — and already the pull elsewhere is stronger than the pull to stay."* This names a precise, recognisable experience that most focus-improvement content misses entirely. It will stop the right reader cold. It earns the mechanism explanation that follows.

4. **The objection handlers on the thank you page are honest rather than dismissive.** The "I've tried self-help before" handler in particular — *"This isn't instruction — it's architecture. Every chapter produces a specific document"* — addresses the objection at the structural level rather than offering reassurance. It does not promise the reader this will be different because it's better; it explains the mechanism by which it is different. That is the correct handling for a sceptical, self-aware audience.

5. **The testimonial collection guidance is an unusually strong piece of production thinking.** Specifying that testimonials should describe a specific behaviour that changed, a measurable outcome, and the mechanism that made it work — and explicitly warning against "life-changing" and "amazing" language — is exactly the right brief for this audience. This guidance will materially improve the quality of social proof collected if it is followed.

## PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** The most significant structural issue — Section 5, the self-assessment QA block — was not a content failure but a submission protocol failure. The producing agent pre-assessed its own work against the checklist, included the ticked checklist in the deliverable, and reached a passing verdict on an item (the urgency claim) that required independent review to identify as problematic. This is a systemic issue: a self-assessment that travels with the content creates the conditions for checklist-laundering, where a human editor or downstream reviewer treats the ticked boxes as a substitute for their own evaluation.

**Producing agent:** Funnel Agent

**Recommended prompt change:** Add the following constraint to the Funnel Agent's output instructions: *"Do not include internal quality checks or self-assessments in your deliverable. Your output package contains only the copy sections. Any internal checklist is a production tool for your own use and must not appear in the submission."* Additionally, the Funnel Agent's checklist item on urgency and scarcity should be revised to include the following explicit check: *"If the copy includes a price increase claim, confirm with the account team that a price increase is documented before including the claim. Do not include 'price will go up' language speculatively."*
