**Track your focus before you try to fix it. You can't audit what you haven't measured.**

Most people begin a detox based on a feeling — a vague sense that their attention is broken. Feelings are an unreliable baseline. You can't track improvement against a feeling. And without a baseline, you won't know if what you're doing is working — which means you'll likely stop before the results are legible.

Before you change anything: run one week of focus tracking. No protocol, no changes, no discipline pushes. Just data. Record: how long before your first distraction in a work session. How many times you check your phone per hour. How long your longest uninterrupted focus block is each day.

Those three numbers give you a baseline. They tell you where the calibration is sitting right now. They become the thing you're trying to move.

When you recheck those numbers after a seven-day reset, the shift will be visible — and visible results are the only thing that makes a protocol sustainable.

Systems-oriented people who skip measurement are not operating from their own framework. They're operating from habit. Measure first.

**Slide 6:**
"Rule 5: Time-slot stimulation, don't just limit it. 'I use social media for 30 minutes at 6pm' is architecture. 'I'll try to use it less' is not."
