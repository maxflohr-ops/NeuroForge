### 1. CORE PROBLEM STATEMENT

Your brain has been recalibrated by high-stimulus environments — phones, feeds, notifications, instant entertainment — to the point where any task requiring sustained effort feels genuinely intolerable. The threshold for what counts as "rewarding enough to start" keeps rising, while your capacity for deep work keeps shrinking. A dopamine detox is the process of resetting that threshold so ordinary, meaningful work becomes accessible again.

---

### 2. AUDIENCE PAINS (8–10 specific pains)

1. I open my phone within 30 seconds of waking up and the day is already compromised before it starts.
2. I sit down to do real work and within 90 seconds I'm on a different tab — I didn't even decide to switch.
3. I've had the same important task on my to-do list for 11 days and I keep doing everything except that.
4. Doing nothing for 5 minutes feels physically uncomfortable — there's a pull I can't name and can't resist.
5. I watch 3 hours of content I didn't even enjoy and feel worse afterward, but I still do it the next night.
6. My attention span during conversations has shortened — I'm already thinking about my phone while someone is mid-sentence.
7. The things I used to love — reading, cooking, long walks — feel flat and boring now compared to the stimulation of a screen.
8. I know exactly what I should be doing to move my work forward, but the knowing and the doing have become completely disconnected.
9. Every time I try to quit social media or reduce screen time, I last about two days before the boredom becomes unbearable and I relapse.
10. I feel like I'm always consuming and never creating — and that gap is starting to bother me at an identity level.

---

### 3. DESIRED OUTCOMES (5–7 outcomes)

1. To be able to sit with a hard task and stay inside it for 60–90 minutes without external force or anxiety.
2. To find genuine satisfaction in low-stimulation activities again — reading, thinking, building — without needing a reward layered on top.
3. To stop feeling controlled by the pull of their phone and actually choose how their attention gets spent.
4. To rebuild the relationship between discomfort and productivity — to tolerate the friction of starting without needing to escape it.
5. To reduce the mental noise that fills every gap in the day, and recover some capacity for quiet, focused thought.
6. To feel the difference between genuine rest and passive numbing — and to actually rest when they rest.
7. To operate from intention rather than impulse — to have a plan for the day and follow it without constant derailment.

### 6. KEY OBJECTIONS AND RESISTANCE POINTS (5–7)

1. **Objection:** "I've tried reducing screen time before and it didn't stick — this probably won't either."
   **Counter:** This isn't a willpower intervention — it's an environmental architecture change; the goal is to make the default behaviour different, not the person.

2. **Objection:** "I need my phone and social media for work — I can't just cut it out."
   **Counter:** The framework is about stimulus load and intentionality, not total elimination; work use and compulsive use are structurally different and addressed separately.

3. **Objection:** "This sounds like pseudoscience — is dopamine detox even a real thing?"
   **Counter:** The name is informal but the mechanism is real: reducing high-frequency reward signals does restore sensitivity; the chapter on mechanism covers this directly.

4. **Objection:** "I'll just be bored and miserable — what's the point?"
   **Counter:** The boredom is temporary and is itself the recovery mechanism; the protocol maps the discomfort curve so you know exactly how long it lasts.

5. **Objection:** "I'm not addicted — I can stop whenever I want."
   **Counter:** The threshold calibration problem doesn't require addiction-level use; even moderate, daily high-stimulus consumption is sufficient to raise the baseline.

6. **Objection:** "I've read about this before — I know what to do, I just don't do it."
   **Counter:** The knowing-doing gap is an architecture problem, not a knowledge problem; the brief gives readers a system design, not more information.

7. **Objection:** "One detox won't fix a years-long pattern."
   **Counter:** Correct — which is why the final section covers maintenance and integration, not just reset; the detox is a reset, not the solution itself.
