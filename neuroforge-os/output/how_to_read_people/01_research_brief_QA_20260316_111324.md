### ISSUES FOUND

**Issue 1:** Unsourced numerical claim — microexpression timing
**Location:** Section 7, Hook Options, "Stat or fact hooks," hook 2 — *"Research on emotional microexpressions shows they last between 1/15th and 1/25th of a second — fast enough that most people miss them, but slow enough to train yourself to catch."*
**Problem:** This presents a specific numerical range as established research fact. The figures are associated with Paul Ekman's work but are stated here without attribution or qualification. In a research brief, unsourced numerical claims are likely to be reproduced by downstream agents as credible facts. If the figures are incorrect, misattributed, or contested, they will appear in published content under Luna Hart's name. The self-check in Section 10 marked "No fake citations or fabricated statistics included" — this tick should not have been applied while this claim was unresolved.
**Required fix:** Either (a) source this claim to a specific, verifiable publication and add the citation in a footnote or bracketed note for the writing agent, or (b) rephrase to remove the specific figures and replace with a non-numerical framing — e.g., *"Emotional microexpressions are gone before most people consciously register them — but training yourself to notice them is possible."* Do not pass the specific figures to the Manuscript Agent without a verified source.
**Severity:** Critical

---

**Issue 2:** Unsourced quantitative claim — deception detection performance
**Location:** Section 7, Hook Options, "Stat or fact hooks," hook 3 — *"Studies on deception consistently show that people perform only slightly better than chance when trying to detect lying — not because it's impossible, but because they're watching the wrong things."*
**Problem:** "Studies consistently show" is a citation signal — it implies a body of research supports this figure. The claim is directionally associated with real research (the work of Aldert Vrij and others), but the phrase "only slightly better than chance" presents a specific quantitative finding without attribution. The same fabrication risk applies as Issue 1: if reproduced in manuscript as a factual claim, it is either unverifiable or incorrect. The Section 10 self-check passed this incorrectly.
**Required fix:** Same resolution path as Issue 1 — either attach a verified source in a bracketed note, or rephrase to remove the quantitative framing. Suggested alternative: *"Most people are far worse at detecting deception than they believe — not because the signals aren't there, but because they've been taught to watch the wrong things."* This preserves the hook's logic without making a claim that requires a citation.
**Severity:** Critical

**Issue 5:** One desired outcome remains abstract
**Location:** Section 3, Desired Outcomes, outcome 6 — *"To become the person in the room who genuinely understands people, which deepens every relationship they're in and makes them easier to trust."*
**Problem:** "Deepens every relationship" and "makes them easier to trust" are results-level claims that are not behaviourally grounded. Every other desired outcome in this section specifies a concrete behavioural or perceptual change — this one describes a social identity and a feeling. It is not specific enough to anchor a chapter or a hook, and it risks being the kind of vague outcome the brief's own pain point section rightly rejects. It also leans slightly toward the motivational language the brand avoids.
**Required fix:** Rephrase to name a specific perceptual or behavioural change. Example: *"To consistently notice what someone needs from a conversation — and respond to that rather than their surface-level words — so interactions feel genuinely connecting rather than transactional."* The bar is: could this outcome anchor a chapter? If not, it needs to be more specific.
**Severity:** Minor

The producing agent should be noted as doing this section well.

2. **The identity hooks are the strongest in the batch.** Hook 15 (*"If you've ever left a conversation feeling uneasy and then spent the next three hours talking yourself out of what you felt — your instincts are not the problem"*) is the kind of sentence that makes a reader feel seen rather than addressed. It does not require the reader to already be interested in the topic — it reaches them before they know they need it. This is native short-form writing, not repurposed blog copy.

3. **Chapter angle 14 ("How to read people you know well — why familiarity creates blind spots") is the freshest angle in the batch.** Most people-reading content focuses on strangers or new relationships. Applying the framework to long-term relationships — and naming familiarity itself as the obstacle — inverts the expected framing in a way that generates genuine curiosity. This angle should be prioritised for development.

4. **The objection handling in Section 6 is unusually good.** Objection 5 (*"Body language and nonverbal cues are pseudoscience — I've heard they've been debunked"*) directly acknowledges a legitimate criticism rather than deflecting it, and the counter-argument concedes the valid part of the objection before reframing. This is the correct approach for a credibility-led brand and for Luna Hart's voice specifically — she does not oversell.

5. **Lead magnet bullet points have genuine standalone value.** The five bullets deliver specific, actionable promises — particularly *"the difference between someone who is warm and someone who is safe, and the one signal that tells them apart"* — which is a genuinely interesting distinction that creates curiosity without overpromising. The lead magnet passes the "would someone want this even if they never buy anything else" test.

### PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** The producing agent passed its own internal quality check (Section 10) while two unverified numerical claims were present in the document. This is a false negative in the self-verification step — the most structurally dangerous type of error in a research brief, because it reduces the probability that anyone downstream catches the problem before publication.

**Producing agent:** Research Agent

**Recommended prompt change:** Add the following instruction to the Research Agent's self-check step for stat/fact hooks specifically:

*"For every hook that contains a number, a percentage, a timeframe, or the phrase 'studies show' or 'research shows': pause and verify. Can you name the specific study, researcher, or publication this figure comes from? If no, do not present it as a factual claim. Either remove the figure and rephrase, or add a bracketed citation note flagging it as requiring verification before passing to the Manuscript Agent. A hook with an unverified statistic is not a factual hook — it is a liability. Do not mark the 'no fabricated statistics' checkbox as complete until every numerical claim in the hooks section has been reviewed against this standard."*

This single addition would have caught both Issues 1 and 2 before the brief reached QA.
