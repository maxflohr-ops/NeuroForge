## ISSUES FOUND

**Issue 1:** Chapter 13 Core Idea paragraph is tonally flat for a closing chapter
**Location:** Chapter 13, Core Idea paragraph — "The skills in this book are not one-time tools..."
**Problem:** Every other chapter core idea paragraph in this blueprint makes its case with some urgency or a genuine reframe. Chapter 13's equivalent describes the meta-point accurately — skills compound, a system is needed — but reads like a project management note rather than a closing idea that earns its place at the end of a book. A reader who arrived at this chapter in manuscript form would feel the momentum drop. Closing chapters need to earn their position; this one, as currently framed, merely occupies it.
**Required fix:** Rewrite the Core Idea paragraph for Chapter 13 to deliver a sharper framing of why *systematised practice* — not just enthusiasm after a good book — is the actual transformation mechanism. It should name the specific failure mode (the reader who gets a week of heightened perception and then returns to their defaults) with the same precision the rest of the blueprint applies to emotional patterns. The reframe should feel like a revelation, not an admin note.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 2:** Chapter 7 oxytocin source note risks overclaiming at manuscript stage
**Location:** Chapter 7, Key Points, bullet 4 — "Why emotionally intelligent, empathetic people are particularly susceptible to performed warmth... Source note: Manuscript Agent should reference research on the role of oxytocin and social bonding in interpersonal trust..."
**Problem:** The caution in the source note ("be careful not to overclaim; cite specifically and note limitations") is present but insufficiently directive. Oxytocin research is one of the most overclaimed areas in popular psychology — the gap between what the studies actually show (modest, context-dependent effects in controlled settings) and what popular writing claims is large. A Manuscript Agent following this direction without more specific guardrails may still produce a sentence that implies oxytocin mechanically explains why performed warmth fools empathetic people, which is not what the research supports and which would be a credibility liability.
**Required fix:** Add a specific constraint to the source note: the mechanism point in this bullet must not be framed as a neurochemical explanation. It should be framed behaviourally (empathetic people extend more good faith because of their dispositional orientation, not because of a hormonal response). If a neuroscience framing is retained, the Manuscript Agent must cite a specific peer-reviewed study, state the effect size in plain language, and include a caveat. Alternatively, replace this bullet's mechanistic framing with research on trust propensity and agreeableness, which has a more robust evidence base and is less likely to overclaim.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 3:** Chapter 11 predictive processing source note is too vague for manuscript use
**Location:** Chapter 11, Key Points, bullet 1 — "Source note: Manuscript Agent should reference predictive processing/predictive coding literature as it applies to social perception — Friston, K., relevant..."
**Problem:** "Friston, K., relevant" is not a usable citation direction. Karl Friston's work on predictive processing is technical, highly mathematical, and not directly about social perception or familiarity bias in the way this chapter needs. The Manuscript Agent following this note may either (a) cite Friston incorrectly — importing a theoretical framework from neuroscience into a social psychology context without the scaffolding to support it — or (b) produce a fabricated or approximate citation that gestures at the right area without being accurate. Either outcome is a credibility risk.
**Required fix:** Replace the Friston reference with a more accessible, directly applicable citation for the familiarity-and-perceptual-accuracy mechanism. Options: Stinson and Ickes (1992) on empathic accuracy in close relationships (already cited elsewhere in the blueprint — appropriate to anchor here too); or general research on the "relationship-specific mental models" literature in social cognition. If predictive processing is retained as a conceptual framing device (not a citation), the source note should clarify that it is being used as a lay explanatory frame, not a direct citation, and that the Manuscript Agent should not attribute the social familiarity point to Friston specifically.
**Severity:** Major

**Issue 5:** "Waiter rule" inclusion requires explicit framing guardrail
**Location:** Chapter 5, Key Points, bullet 5 — "how someone treats service staff, assistants, or anyone they perceive as having less status... Source note: 'waiter rule' concept — widely cited in leadership and character literature; Manuscript Agent should note this is observational/anecdotal rather than controlled experimental research and frame accordingly"
**Problem:** The source note correctly identifies this as anecdotal, but "frame accordingly" is ambiguous instruction. The Manuscript Agent needs to know what "accordingly" means in practice — is this bullet to be presented as conventional wisdom with an explicit caveat? As a widely observed heuristic with illustrative examples? Or dropped in favour of the Gottman repair research, which is empirically stronger and covers adjacent ground? Without explicit direction, the Manuscript Agent may present the "waiter rule" with more authority than it has earned, which is a credibility exposure point for a brand whose reputation depends on not overclaiming.
**Required fix:** Add a specific framing instruction: "Present this as a widely held observational heuristic, not a research-supported finding. Do not attribute it to a specific study. Introduce it with explicit acknowledgement that it is experiential rather than experimental — e.g., 'it is widely observed that...' or 'there is a long-standing claim in character assessment that...' — and weight it accordingly relative to the Gottman research in the same section."
**Severity:** Minor

## PASSED ELEMENTS

1. **The transformation arc is the strongest element in the document.** The before/after split in Section 5 is specific, behavioural, and non-generic. "Cannot distinguish between a genuine gut signal and their own anxiety, projection, or wishful thinking" is not a vague pain point — it is a precisely named experience that will produce an immediate recognition response in the target reader. The after states are equally specific and grounded. This is exactly the standard the QC checklist requires and it is met throughout.

2. **Chapter 9's core idea paragraph is the best-written section in the blueprint.** "Empathy is not just a defence against manipulation — in the hands of a skilled manipulator, it is the primary point of entry" is a genuinely counterintuitive claim, clearly stated, and the development of why (perspective-taking turned into self-doubt, commitment to fairness making it harder to act on partial information) is precisely the kind of layered thinking that makes Luna Hart's voice distinctive. This paragraph would work almost verbatim as a section opening in the manuscript.

3. **The source note practice throughout is markedly above average.** The blueprint doesn't just say "cite research here" — it names specific works, flags replication concerns (Ekman universality critique, noting Barrett's counter-research), distinguishes experimental from observational evidence, and gives the Manuscript Agent explicit direction on what not to do. This level of citation hygiene at blueprint stage significantly reduces the risk of fabricated or overclaimed research at manuscript stage.

4. **The chapter transition lines do genuine structural work.** Every transition closes the current chapter's question and opens the next one's without summarising or repeating. The Chapter 6 to 7 transition ("But there's a specific pattern that trips people up more than almost any other — because it mimics genuine warmth so closely that even perceptive readers miss it") creates genuine pull. The Chapter 10 to 11 transition ("there's one context in which all of these skills are most likely to fail — not because the skills don't apply, but because the reader stops using them") reframes the next chapter as a threat to something the reader now values, which is exactly the tension-maintenance technique that keeps readers moving through nonfiction.

5. **The recommended title rationale is specific and commercially grounded.** Rather than asserting that *How to Read People* is the right title, the blueprint provides a specific argument: highest-volume search term, unambiguous promise, subtitle carries the emotional register. The subtitle analysis is precise — "finally understand" acknowledges the reader's frustration, "what people are actually telling you" names the gap, "trust your gut" addresses the specific self-doubt the book is written for. This kind of explicit reasoning at blueprint stage means the title decision is auditable and revisable rather than arbitrary.

## PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** The blueprint's three most significant issues (Chapter 13 tonal flatness, oxytocin framing risk, Friston citation vagueness) share a common structure: they all occur at points where the Blueprint Agent made a correct structural decision but provided insufficient specificity in the execution guidance. In each case, the agent knew *that* something needed to be addressed (the closing chapter, the neuroscience framing, the predictive processing mechanism) but did not specify *how precisely* it needed to be handled, leaving the ambiguity to be resolved — or misresolved — by the downstream Manuscript Agent.

**Producing agent:** Book Architect Agent

**Recommended prompt addition:** Add the following instruction to the Book Architect Agent's closing-chapter and source-note protocols:

*For Chapter 13 / closing chapters:* "The Core Idea paragraph for the final chapter must name a specific failure mode the reader is at risk of — not just a general aspiration. It must identify what separates the reader who internalises the book's framework from the one who doesn't, and make that distinction feel urgent. Generic statements about skills compounding are insufficient."

*For source notes flagged as overclaim risks:* "Where a source note includes the instruction 'be careful not to overclaim,' it must also include: (1) a specific example of the overclaim to avoid, (2) an alternative framing that achieves the chapter's goal without the overclaim, and (3) where possible, a more robustly supported mechanism the Manuscript Agent can use instead. 'Be careful' without specifics transfers the credibility risk downstream rather than eliminating it."
