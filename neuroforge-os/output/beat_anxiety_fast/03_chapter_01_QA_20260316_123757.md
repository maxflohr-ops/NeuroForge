### ISSUES FOUND

**Issue 1:** Barlow attribution requires verification
**Location:** "The False Positive Is a Feature, Not a Bug" section — "David Barlow, in his extensive work on anxiety and its disorders, describes the mismatch between the evolved threat-response and the contemporary threat landscape in terms of this miscalibration."
**Problem:** This attributes a specific conceptual framing to David Barlow without citing a specific work. Barlow is a real and prominent researcher, but "his extensive work" is not a citation — it is a gesture toward a citation. If the editorial team cannot identify the specific text this refers to (likely *Anxiety and Its Disorders*, 2002), this attribution is unverifiable and could be a confabulation. Publishing an unverifiable attribution damages credibility if challenged.
**Required fix:** Either (a) verify this framing appears in a specific Barlow text and add the specific work title to the attribution, or (b) remove Barlow's name and retain the conceptual point, which stands without it. Do not leave the attribution in its current form.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 2:** LeDoux "low road / high road" framing — hedge is present but attribution scope needs confirmation
**Location:** "The Brain Has Two Speeds" section — the LeDoux paragraph and its follow-up hedge.
**Problem:** The hedge ("LeDoux has since refined aspects of this framework") is good editorial practice and demonstrates genuine familiarity with the field. However, the framing still attributes the "low road / high road" model specifically to LeDoux as "foundational work." LeDoux himself has moved away from this model in later work (*Anxious*, 2015; *The Deep History of Ourselves*, 2019), arguing it oversimplifies the neuroscience and that the amygdala's role in conscious fear experience was overstated. The current text gestures at this but does not quite capture the degree to which LeDoux disowned the popular version of his own model. This is a credibility risk — a reader familiar with LeDoux's more recent position could reasonably read this chapter as presenting superseded science with insufficient caveat.
**Required fix:** Strengthen the hedge in the follow-up paragraph. Something to the effect that LeDoux himself has cautioned against treating this as a literal anatomical map of fear, and that the chapter is using the framework as a functional description of a real phenomenon, not as a precise account of current neuroscientific consensus. The core point — that threat response precedes conscious awareness — is well-supported and should be retained. The LeDoux-as-authority framing needs more careful qualification.
**Severity:** Major

**Issue 3:** The Autonomic Nervous System section is the weakest passage in the chapter
**Location:** "The Autonomic Nervous System: The System You Will Actually Work With" — full section
**Problem:** This section reads differently from the rest of the chapter. It is accurate and necessary, but it delivers information in a more declarative, textbook style — "governs the body's involuntary functions," "operates through two branches that are broadly complementary" — without the observational precision and forward-pulling tension that characterises the stronger sections. The hedging phrase "a vague understanding" is the only moment in the chapter that slightly condescends to the reader. More importantly, this section does not have a counterintuitive core — it is setup for a point (entry via the body, not the cortex) that has already been made in the previous section. It currently reads as an expanded glossary that earns its place on function but not on engagement.
**Required fix:** This section does not need to be restructured, but it needs a single strong observational hook that matches the register of the rest of the chapter, and the phrase "a vague understanding" should be cut or reframed. The editor should also evaluate whether the ANS definitions can be condensed by 20–25% without losing the mechanistic grounding — the section currently runs longer than its contribution to the chapter's argument warrants.

### PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure observed:** Attribution imprecision — real researchers named, real frameworks referenced, but specific source texts absent. This is distinct from fabricated citation (a Red Flag), but it occupies dangerous adjacent territory. A reader who looks up "Barlow on anxiety and the evolved threat landscape" may find the concept is real but cannot verify the specific framing attributed to him here.

**Producing agent:** Manuscript Agent

**Recommended prompt addition:** Add the following instruction to the Manuscript Agent prompt:

*"When naming a specific researcher or academic in the text, you must either (a) name the specific published work being referenced in the sentence or a parenthetical, or (b) remove the name and retain only the concept. Attributing a concept to a named researcher without identifying a specific source is not acceptable. If you cannot identify a specific source, use general attribution: 'Research in this area suggests...' or 'As one influential account of fear processing describes it...'"*

This change would prevent both the Barlow and LeDoux issues in this chapter, and would eliminate the category of problem entirely from future manuscript output.
