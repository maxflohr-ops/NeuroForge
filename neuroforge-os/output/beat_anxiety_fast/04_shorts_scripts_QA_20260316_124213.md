### ISSUES FOUND

**Issue 1:** Unsupported empirical claim presented as research-confirmed
**Location:** Script 7 — "The ratio that research supports is roughly a four-count inhale and a six-to-eight-count exhale"
**Problem:** The script explicitly frames this ratio as research-backed ("that research supports") but names no study, researcher, or body of evidence. In a short-form format where there is no space for citations, this framing creates a precision signal the content cannot back up. If a viewer or journalist asks what research supports this specific ratio, there is no answer in the script. The hook also specifically calls out "most breathing instructions" as wrong, which raises the stakes on the claim. This is not a fabrication — the extended exhale mechanism is well-supported — but the specific 4:6–8 ratio claim requires either a softer framing ("a ratio that tends to work is...") or should be cut to the mechanism without the numbers.
**Required fix:** Change "The ratio that research supports" to "A ratio that tends to work well" or "A ratio commonly used in clinical practice" — language that is accurate without implying a specific sourced study. Alternatively, if the producing agent has a specific source for this ratio, name it in the brief so the human editor can verify and decide whether to include it.
**Severity:** Major

**Issue 2:** Research claim used as hook with no follow-through in body
**Location:** Script 10 — Hook: "That's not a metaphor — that's what the research shows." Body: no research is named, characterised, or described.
**Problem:** The hook explicitly positions the 20-minute claim as an empirical research finding. This is the hook's entire credibility lever — it's not just a reframe, it's "science says this specific thing." But the script body never names or describes the research. A careful viewer will notice the claim was asserted and then dropped. The "20 minutes" figure is also uncharacteristically precise without sourcing — it's the kind of number that looks invented if it can't be backed.
**Required fix:** Either (a) soften the hook to "Avoidance relieves anxiety short-term. Then it makes it worse — and here's the exact mechanism" — removing the research framing — or (b) have the producing agent identify the specific research being referenced so the human editor can verify the 20-minute figure and add appropriate attribution or softening language.
**Severity:** Major

**Issue 3:** Vague research reference — "research on cognitive reappraisal"
**Location:** Script 13 — "Research on what's called cognitive reappraisal shows that labelling activation as excitement rather than anxiety — even without fully believing it — measurably changes both physiological response and performance outcomes."
**Problem:** This is the closest the batch comes to a fabrication risk. The claim is specific ("measurably changes physiological response and performance outcomes"), the mechanism is named (cognitive reappraisal), and the framing implies a body of research. Alison Wood Brooks' work at Harvard does support this, but the script doesn't name it. If the research is real and known, it should be attributed. If the producing agent invented the specificity, this is a credibility violation. The claim is plausible — but plausible is not the same as verified.
**Required fix:** Have the producing agent identify the specific study or studies underpinning this claim. If verified (Brooks, 2014, or similar), the human editor can decide whether to name the researcher in the script ("Research from Harvard Business School shows...") or soften the framing. If the research cannot be identified, remove the word "measurably" and soften to "research suggests."
**Severity:** Major

**Issue 4:** Script 15 visual direction is incomplete — submission cut off
**Location:** Script 15 — Visual Direction ends mid-sentence: "Measured, close fr"
**Problem:** This script cannot go to production. The visual direction is missing, which means a videographer or editor working from this brief has no guidance for framing or delivery notes on the final script. This is a production error in the submission, not a content error.
**Required fix:** Return Script 15 to the producing agent for completion. The visual direction should follow the format of Scripts 11–14 in the same section: delivery notes, framing guidance, and any emphasis instructions.
**Severity:** Critical (production blocker)

**Issue 5:** Scripts 16–20, quote graphics, and carousel content are absent
**Location:** Submission-level — the document header states "20 Scripts + Quote Graphics + Carousels" but only Scripts 1–15 are present
**Problem:** This is an incomplete submission. Five scripts, all quote graphics, and all carousel content are unreviewed and presumably unproduced. This batch cannot be marked as complete or passed to design until the full deliverable is present.
**Required fix:** Return the brief to the producing agent to complete the full deliverable before the batch is marked as reviewed. QA cannot pass content that hasn't been submitted.
**Severity:** Critical (incomplete submission)

**Issue 6:** Action steps in several scripts remain vague for a format that requires behavioural specificity
**Location:** Scripts 4, 8, 11, 12, 15 — action steps defer entirely to the linked guide without giving the viewer a single completable micro-behaviour
**Problem:** The checklist requirement states "Action step is a specific behaviour, not a vague suggestion." The guide CTA is appropriate and consistent across the batch, but several scripts end with nothing more than "go to my guide" as the actionable component. Scripts 1, 2, 7, and 9 all give a specific on-platform action step before the CTA ("a slow, extended exhale," "three steps: body first, sensory anchor second, redirect third"). Scripts 4, 8, 11, 12, and 15 leave the viewer with only a reframe and a link. In a 45-second format, one specific micro-behaviour before the CTA is both achievable and necessary. The viewer should be able to do one thing before they reach the guide.
**Required fix:** For each affected script, add one specific micro-behaviour before the guide CTA. It does not need to be the full protocol — it should be the entry point. For Script 8, for example: "Start with cold water on your face and three extended exhales — then get the full sequence from the guide." This takes four seconds and gives the viewer something to act on immediately.
**Severity:** Minor

**Issue 7:** Slight tonal drift in Scripts 4 and 12 toward reassurance register
**Location:** Script 4 — "Stop measuring yourself against how other people appear to respond. You don't have access to what they were carrying when they walked in." Script 12 — "You're not broken. You're running a learned pattern. And learned patterns have an entry point."
**Problem:** These are not egregious departures, and both lines are well-written. However, Dr. Nova Vale's voice as established across the rest of the batch is clinical and explanatory — she diagnoses the mechanism, she does not console the viewer. "Stop measuring yourself" and "You're not broken" both move toward the reassurance register, which is more characteristic of a softer, more therapeutic persona. Neither line would fail a casual read, but a careful human editor should review them against the faculty character brief to determine whether they represent intentional range or drift.
**Required fix:** Flag for human editor to review against faculty character brief. If the brief supports occasional direct address with compassionate framing, these lines pass. If the brief defines Dr. Nova Vale as consistently clinical and mechanism-focused, these lines should be rewritten to stay in the explanatory register.
**Severity:** Minor

### PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** The mechanism-led scripts (6–10) share a recurring structure where the script body makes a specific empirical claim, frames it as research-supported, and then defers all specificity to a linked guide. This works as a content strategy but creates a credibility gap: the claim sounds precise, the support is absent, and the guide (which may or may not exist at the time of production) is carrying all the evidentiary weight.

**Producing agent:** Shorts Script Agent

**Recommended prompt change:** Add the following instruction to the Shorts Script Agent prompt under the Credibility section:

*"When a script references research, a study, or empirical data, one of the following must be true: (a) the research is named or attributed in the script body ('research from [field/institution] shows...'), (b) the framing is softened to reflect uncertainty ('tends to,' 'commonly found,' 'evidence suggests'), or (c) a research note is included in the production metadata identifying the specific source so the QA agent and human editor can verify it. Scripts may not use the phrase 'research shows' or 'studies show' without meeting one of these three conditions."*

**Secondary note:** The incomplete submission (Scripts 16–20, quote graphics, carousels missing) and the truncated Visual Direction in Script 15 suggest the Shorts Script Agent may be exiting the generation loop before completing the full deliverable. The producing prompt should include an explicit completion check: *"Before returning output, confirm that all requested deliverables are present and all scripts are complete to the end of the Visual Direction field."*
