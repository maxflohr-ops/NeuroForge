**SCRIPT 7**
**Hook:** Most people don't know there's a specific point in the exhale that directly triggers your parasympathetic system. Breathing deeply without hitting it does almost nothing.
**Faculty:** Dr. Nova Vale
**Topic:** The mechanics of extended exhale breathing
**Estimated Duration:** 50 seconds

---

**Most people don't know there's a specific point in the exhale that directly triggers your parasympathetic system.**

Breathing deeply — the generic advice — does very little for an activated nervous system on its own. The inhale actually activates your sympathetic system. It's the exhale that does the opposite.

More specifically: it's an extended exhale. One that's meaningfully longer than the inhale.

Here's the mechanism. Your heart rate speeds up slightly when you inhale and slows when you exhale. That's called respiratory sinus arrhythmia, and it's normal. When your exhale is longer than your inhale, you spend more of the breath cycle in the slowing phase. Over several cycles, this directly activates the vagus nerve and begins shifting your nervous system toward a calmer state.

The ratio that research supports is roughly a four-count inhale and a six-to-eight-count exhale. Not four and four. Not deep and slow without a ratio. The extension of the exhale specifically.

Most breathing instructions don't tell you this. They say breathe deeply, and leave you breathing harder into an already overactivated system.
