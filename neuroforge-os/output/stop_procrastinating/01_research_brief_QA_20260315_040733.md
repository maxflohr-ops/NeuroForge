### ISSUES FOUND

---

**Issue 1:** Unverified quantitative claim in hook
**Location:** Section 7, Stat/Fact Hooks, Hook 1 — *"The average person spends over two hours a day on tasks that actively help them avoid the work they planned to do."*
**Problem:** This is presented as a factual average with no source. The figure is specific enough that a reader or editor will ask where it comes from. If the number was generated rather than sourced, publishing it damages NeuroForge's credibility pillar. Even if a rough analogue exists in research, "over two hours" and "tasks that actively help them avoid" is a precise framing that requires a precise source.
**Required fix:** Either attach a verifiable source to this hook, reframe it as an observation rather than a statistic (*"Most people, if they tracked their day honestly, would find hours absorbed by tasks designed to feel like work while avoiding the one that matters"*), or cut this hook from the candidate set. Do not commission content using this hook until resolved.
**Severity:** Critical

---

**Issue 2:** Neuroscience claim requires qualification
**Location:** Section 7, Stat/Fact Hooks, Hook 2 — *"Your brain releases dopamine in anticipation of a reward — which means scrolling your phone before you start work has already paid your brain before you've done anything."*
**Problem:** The first clause is accurate — anticipatory dopamine release is well-established. The second clause (*"has already paid your brain"*) is an interpretive leap that conflates anticipatory dopamine signalling with satiation of the reward circuit. This is a meaningful distortion. The mechanism is more accurately that variable-ratio reinforcement schedules (as used in social feeds) trigger dopamine spikes that raise the effort threshold for less stimulating tasks — not that the brain has been "paid." The current framing could be cited back against NeuroForge by anyone with passing neuroscience knowledge, and it overstates what the science shows.
**Required fix:** Reframe the second clause to accurately represent the mechanism. Suggested direction: *"...which means your brain has already been primed for high-stimulation reward before you've opened a single work file — making the task in front of you feel comparatively unrewarding."* This preserves the insight without misrepresenting the neuroscience.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 3:** Research-backed claim with no sourcing infrastructure
**Location:** Section 7, Stat/Fact Hooks, Hook 3 — *"Studies on task switching show that every time you defer a task, the mental representation of that task grows heavier — not the task itself, the thought of it."*
**Problem:** *"Studies on task switching"* is a vague attribution that signals a sourced claim without providing one. Task switching research (e.g., Monsell, 2003; or the Zeigarnik effect literature) does not straightforwardly map onto the specific claim about deferred tasks accumulating cognitive weight. The Zeigarnik effect is the closest analogue, but it addresses uncompleted tasks staying active in working memory — not that deferral progressively increases their perceived weight. The hook is plausible and directionally aligned with cognitive load research, but as written it implies a specific study that may not exist.
**Required fix:** Either name the actual research being drawn on and verify the claim maps accurately to that research, or remove the *"studies show"* framing and restate it as a mechanistic observation grounded in what the brief already establishes (*"Every time you defer a task, the mental representation of it grows heavier — not the task, the thought of it. That weight is real, and it compounds."*). This version is equally strong and makes no false sourcing claim.
**Severity:** Major

---

**Issue 4:** Section 7 hook candidate contains minor voice drift
**Location:** Section 7, Identity Hooks, Hook 2 — *"You're not lazy. You're not broken. You have a brain that's learned the wrong lesson about where effort gets rewarded."*
**Problem:** The reassurance structure (*"You're not lazy. You're not broken."*) edges toward the therapeutic-validating register that is inconsistent with Kai Ren's established voice. Kai Ren's persona is precise and mechanistic — it does not comfort the reader, it explains the system to them. This hook reads more like a therapist-adjacent voice than a systems thinker. It is not a severe drift, but if used as a script or ad hook it will slightly misrepresent the faculty's tone.
**Required fix:** Either cut this hook from the candidate set or reframe it to lead with the mechanism rather than the reassurance. Suggested direction: *"Laziness is a moral judgment. What's actually happening is a brain that's learned the wrong lesson about where effort gets rewarded — and that's fixable."*
**Severity:** Minor

**Issue 5:** One lead magnet bullet is under-specified relative to the rest
**Location:** Section 8, Lead Magnet Bullets, Bullet 5 — *"The one environmental change that produces an immediate reduction in task-switching behaviour."*
**Problem:** Every other bullet in this section names a specific mechanism, tool, or timeframe. Bullet 5 withholds the what entirely — which works as mystery in a sales context, but in a research brief the commissioning purpose is to specify what the content must deliver. A manuscript agent or content creator working from this brief does not know what environmental change to build content around. This creates a downstream gap.
**Required fix:** In the brief (not necessarily in the public-facing magnet copy), name the specific environmental change being referenced so that any agent or editor commissioning content from this brief knows what to develop. A parenthetical note in the brief is sufficient: e.g., *(specifically: phone removal from workspace or single-app fullscreen mode — confirm which approach Kai Ren's system uses before commissioning).*
**Severity:** Minor

### PROMPT IMPROVEMENT NOTE

**Pattern of failure:** The Research Brief Agent is generating plausible-sounding quantitative and research-backed hooks without attaching sourcing requirements to them. The agent's internal quality check approves hooks that contain specific figures (*"over two hours a day"*) and research references (*"studies on task switching show"*) without flagging these as requiring verification. This is the most common single failure mode in this brief.

**Producing agent:** Research Brief Agent

**Recommended prompt change:** Add the following rule to the Research Brief Agent's hook generation instructions:

*"Any hook that contains a number, a statistic, a percentage, or the phrase 'research shows', 'studies show', or any variation thereof must be flagged with a [NEEDS SOURCE] tag inline. Do not present these hooks as finalised. The QA agent will reject any hook with an unverified quantitative claim. If you cannot attach a verifiable source, reframe the hook as a mechanistic observation rather than a data point. A strong observation hook is more credible than a weak stat hook."*

This single addition would have prevented Issues 1, 2, and 3 in this review.
