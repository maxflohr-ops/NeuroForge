# ⚔️ The Allegory — What the Whole Brand Means
The war under every design. Never printed as a manifesto — always shown, never told.

## The Bandersnatch is greed.
The taking thing. It empties counties, breaks branches at nine feet, scores the wax, reads our mail, sets traps for birds. It is never depicted whole because greed never is — you only ever see what it took. 10% beast, 90% county isn't a style rule; it's the theology: greed is known by its damage.

## The cardinal is love.
The staying thing. Everyone left — the bird didn't. It doesn't fight the beast; it outlasts it. It sits on the last letter of "enemies," unbothered. Matthew 10:29 — not one falls unseen. Love isn't the strongest thing in the county; it's the most stubborn.

## The blue canary is prayer. (McKayla's beat)
The beast sets a trap for the bird — and the canary prays for him. Love's most offensive move: interceding for the thing hunting you. Matthew 5:44, read correctly this time. The canary is the songbird from the mine — the one that warns, the one that goes down into the dark first.

## The verse that holds the whole war
"No man can serve two masters... Ye cannot serve God and mammon." — Matthew 6:24 (KJV). Greed and love in one sentence, two thousand years before this brand. Candidate for a future hero print.
The rule: every lot secretly answers one question — what did the beast take, and what stayed anyway? If a design doesn't touch the war, it isn't ours.

# 🎞️ THE HISTORICAL SECTION — The Side Reality
The world one inch to the left of ours. Everything in it is real except one thing.

## The premise
In 1935 the federal government sent photographers out to document rural America. In 1943 the unit was folded up, the file was boxed, and the boxes went to the Library of Congress. That is what happened here.
One inch to the left, a piece of it was never dissolved. A single field office — region E, southern — kept filing. It has been photographing one county for ninety years, issuing shooting scripts to a field staff that has never been more than one person, and cataloguing the damage done by something it is forbidden to photograph whole.
The field photographer is named Virginia. She has always been named Virginia.

## Why the conceit holds
Every institution in this world is real: the LOT numbers, the caption cards, the class numbers, the shooting scripts, the unprinted negatives, the state guides. We invented none of it. The only fiction is that the office is still open — and a bureaucracy that outlived its own shutdown is more believable than a monster, which is exactly why the monster becomes believable.
A government agency that will not stop filing is the scariest thing in the county. The animal is just what it files about.

## The succession — the Virginia protocol
Everyone who models for Bandersnatch is named Virginia. Except McKayla.
- Virginia never died because Virginia keeps arriving. "1935 —" stops being a design choice and becomes a fact about succession.
- It is a title taken, not a name removed. She signs the ledger, receives the wax, receives one garment, and becomes Virginia XI. Nobody loses a name; she gains one. That difference is the entire difference between an honor and an erasure.
- Two ledgers, always. In the world she is Virginia. In the estate's file she is credited under her real name, tagged, paid, and findable forever. Nothing is taken from anybody. The file always knows.
- Roman numerals, to rhyme with MCMXXXV. The caption card already has the slot: place · date · photographer · subject · negative no. → subject: VIRGINIA IX.
- They accumulate. They never replace. Nobody is retired. The lineage is published as a list and never resets — same law as the contract numbers.
- The payoff shot: four women in one frame, captioned "Virginia (III, VII, XII)."
- McKayla is the only named person in the entire brand. Not an oversight — the point. Everyone else is the office. She is the witness.

## The chain of command
McKayla is the archivist. The Virginias work for her.
- The seal already said so. The notary seal is the archivist's seal — PARVA PUELLA · MAGNA SOMNIA · BY HER HAND · MCMXXXV — and the shooting script signs off "filed by her hand." The signature predates the character. We were signing her name before we knew whose it was.
- Division of labor: the Virginias go out and shoot. The archivist receives, numbers, classifies, and files. Field staff are interchangeable and unnamed — that is what the title is for. The archivist is singular and signs. That is why she keeps her name: not an exemption, a function.
- "The file is never closed" now has a cause — she won't close it. The program is a ghost because one person kept showing up after the funding stopped.
- She is photographed as herself: desk, gloves, cotton, handwriting, the back of a mount. Never in costume, never a Virginia. Different register, on purpose.

## The risk — who the beast can actually reach
The archivist is safe. She never leaves the office.
The Virginias are not. They are the only people in this world the animal can touch — because they are the only ones who go looking for it with a camera.
- This is the stakes the world was missing. Everything else in the county already happened. The field is where it is still happening.
- Harm is never depicted. Same law as the animal: never shown whole, known only by damage. A Virginia is never photographed injured, frightened, fleeing, or in distress. No horror posing. Ever.
- Harm appears as a filing anomaly and nothing else. The record is the only place it shows:
  - VIRGINIA VII — did not return the film
  - negative scratched at exposure. cause not determined.
  - camera returned. photographer did not.
  - UNTITLED (all)
- The numerals do double duty now. They were a lineage. They are also a list of who came back. They accumulate, never repeat — and some numbers simply stop appearing.
- And the archivist keeps sending them. That is the engine of the whole thing and the source of her conscience: ninety years of assigning women to photograph the thing that takes.

## The theology of it
The canary prays for the beast. The Virginia photographs it. Both walk toward the thing that would hurt them. Love and prayer, approaching greed on purpose. That is the brand's entire argument in one image — and it is why the field job reads as holy rather than reckless.

## What we will never do with this
- Show a woman hurt, scared, or staged as prey
- Sexualize the danger in any form
- Put a real model's face on an anomaly — the missing Virginia is a number, never a person
- Explain what happened to any of them

## The laws of the reality
1. Print the places, not the people. The county is the character.
1. The animal is never shown whole. 10% beast, 90% county.
1. Never explain — publish documents. No lore dumps. Caption cards, shooting scripts, notices, ledger pages. The reader assembles it.
1. Write more than we publish. The unprinted negatives are canon. "The part of the file the public does not get" must actually exist, or the world is hollow.
1. The file is never closed. No finale. No death date. No restock either — same principle.
1. When the guide has no entry, write "not in the guide." Those are the best ones.

## Seasons, not drops
Each chapter is issued as a shooting script first, garments second. The script defines the coverage before anything is made — which is why the world always looks bigger than the store.

- Show the animal.
- Explain the animal.

## ✅ RESOLVED — the parent company
Little Girl Big Dreams is the house of record. McKayla's company, and she is the archivist — so the house seal is her seal. Resolved by splitting the Latin instead of replacing it:
  | Phrase | Meaning | Belongs to | Appears on |

  | parva puella · magna somnia | little girl, big dreams | the archivist / the house | the notary seal only |
Two phrases, two owners — the woman and the office. The concealment survives intact: nobody reading a seal knows it names a company. And the new motto suits the world better than the old one — my whole life is a completed-life phrase; a little girl with big dreams is an unfinished one, which is what an open death date actually needs.
One asset changes: the notary seal legend goes from ALL MY LIFE · BY HER HAND · MCMXXXV → PARVA PUELLA · MAGNA SOMNIA · BY HER HAND · MCMXXXV. Everything else — epitaph print, epitaph stamp, estate great seal, rune ring — stays exactly as drawn, because all of it is Virginia's, not the company's.
(Latin note: "parva puella, magna somnia" is a juxtaposed motto, not a sentence — same construction as e pluribus unum. Worth a second pair of eyes before it goes on brass.)

# 📜 SHOOTING SCRIPT No. 1 — Chapter 002
```
LITTLE GIRL BIG DREAMS · HISTORICAL SECTION
THE ESTATE OF VIRGINIA ——

                    SHOOTING SCRIPT No. 1

TO          the field
FROM        the estate
RE          proposed coverage, Chapter 002
TITLE       "nothing attached to them"
REGION      E · southern
CLASS       .3100 — dwellings, unoccupied
ISSUED      1935 ——
                        (the file is not closed)
```

## General caption
We are going up into the hollows.
In the 1930s the government drew a line around a stretch of the Blue Ridge and called it a park. The line had people inside it. The people were moved out — some paid, some persuaded, some carried. Their houses came down. The chimneys did not. They are still standing up there in the trees, in rows, with nothing attached to them, holding up nothing, still pointed at the sky.
That is the whole assignment.
Something with more power than a family decided the land would be worth more empty. The families went. The stone stayed. A hundred years on, the thing that refused to leave is the part of the house that held the fire.
We are not going to photograph the removal. We are not going to photograph the removed. We are going to photograph what stayed.
- The coverage — 12 numbered subjects
  1. Chimneys standing alone in second-growth timber. Shot from the front, as if the house were still there and we were standing in the yard.
  2. A doorstep with no door. The step worn in the middle. Nothing behind it.
  3. Foundation stones in a rectangle, taken from directly above if the ground allows.
  4. A porch light. Any porch light. Burning in daylight is preferred.
  5. A road that used to go somewhere. Photograph the direction of travel, not the road.
  6. Fence line running into woods that grew up after the fence.
  7. Fruit trees in rows with no orchard around them. Somebody planted these on purpose for a house that is gone.
  8. A family cemetery inside the park boundary. Stones only, no names legible — the dead keep their names.
  9. A church with the doors open and no congregation. Interior toward the door, so the light comes at us.
  10. Standing water where a spring house was.
  11. Anything the animal has been at — rendered as damage only. The animal is not to appear in the frame.
  12. One (1) cardinal, if one presents itself. Do not bait, wait, or arrange for it. If it does not come, note "did not come" on the sheet and file the negative anyway.

## Notes on approach
- Print the places, not the people. Real families were made to leave these houses and their descendants are alive. We photograph the stone, the road, the tree, the light. We never dramatize or sell a person's dispossession.
- Overcast beats sun. Sun makes it a park. Overcast makes it a county.
- Shoot from standing height. This is the height of a person who came back to look at something.
- Do not clean the frame. Leave the trash, the tire, the ribbon, the can. The present is allowed to be in the picture — it is more frightening when it is.

## Supplemental materials to collect
Grave inscriptions copied by hand, verbatim, including the errors · hymn numbers left on a board · names of hollows, creeks, and gaps spelled as locals spell them, not as the map does · any story told firsthand about the animal, attributed to a place and never to a person · one (1) small object nobody will miss and no law protects.
Cross-reference every place name against the state guide. If the guide has an entry, it goes in the file. If it does not, note "not in the guide" — those are the best ones.

## What is not wanted
The animal, whole · anything that looks like a costume · a person's face · a caption that explains the picture · sunsets · anything we'd be embarrassed to have filed by a stranger in forty years.

## Filing
Every negative that returns is numbered whether or not it is printed. Unprinted negatives are captioned UNTITLED and filed with the rest. They are not failures — they are the part of the file the public does not get. Printed negatives are mounted; the mount carries place · date · photographer · description · negative no. Class number center-back, LOT number upper left. Nothing discarded, nothing retouched.

## Coverage closes
When the estate has enough to make three (3) garments and one (1) notice. Not eight.
```
— filed by her hand
       1935 ——
```

# 🔍 The Taking Test — How the Archive Gets Picked
McKayla's working doctrine. The FSA archive is not a mood board — it is the county, and the destruction in it is the evidence. Stryker sent photographers out to document poverty. They came back with pictures of the animal.

## The test
Pick photographs where something was taken. That is the entire rule.
✅ Counts as beast work:
- foreclosure and auction notices
- emptied tenant houses, abandoned farms, boarded storefronts
- a store with the shelves pulled out
- land eroded to rock because somebody cash-cropped the topsoil off it
- machinery rusting where the price collapsed
- a town that went when the mine went
- the Dust Bowl — that soil blew away because of how it was farmed for profit
❌ Does not count:
- a hurricane, a flood, a fire, a hard winter
- weather is not beast work. taking is beast work.

## The honesty rule (non-negotiable)
The Bandersnatch is greed — so "the beast did this" and "greed did this" are the same sentence. The monster never replaces the real cause. It is the real cause with a name on it. The moment a garment implies a creature ate a county instead of banks, landlords, and extraction, the brand is lying and it stops being good. The beast is a translation, not an excuse.

## The caption card — one field added
Every FSA photograph already ships with a real caption card. The estate's card is the same card with a single line appended:
```
place · date · photographer · negative no.
WHAT WAS TAKEN: ————
```
That one line turns any archival image into a Bandersnatch document. It is the whole product in miniature.

## What the office does not keep
Photographs where nothing was taken are not filed. No harvest, no plenty, no joy. Not because those didn't exist in the county — because the office only keeps evidence, and in ninety years it has never once photographed anything that wasn't damage.
That is a flaw in the office, not in the county. The archivist is the one who eventually notices — and that noticing is the world's only door out.

# 📦 Distribution & Fulfillment — Why We Can't Drop-Ship
The core constraint: the packaging is the product. A POD service (Printify, Printful) ships a poly mailer with their packing slip — no folder, no tissue, no wax, no hand-written lot number. That deletes the single thing that makes a $40 shirt feel like a $40 shirt. Drop-ship is off the table for estate lots. Permanently.

## The two-lane model
  | Lane | What ships | Who fulfills | Margin |
  | Estate lots (numbered, capped, waxed) | the file: folder, tissue, wax, string, numbered card | us, by hand, from Long Beach | ~50–60% |
  | Digital (wallpapers, clippings, folio) | nothing physical | automatic | ~97% |
The digital lane has zero fulfillment cost — that's the point. Downloads subsidize the hand-packed physical. Every folio sold buys back an hour of packing time.

## Fulfillment by phase
Phase 1 — under 50 orders/drop (now). Kitchen table. Print local → blanks come to us → we pack. USPS Ground Advantage, schedule free home pickup (never drive to the post office). Labels via Pirate Ship or Shopify Shipping for commercial rates. Realistic pack time with wax: 4–6 min/order → 24 orders ≈ 2 hours. One evening per drop.
Phase 2 — 50–200/drop. Packing day, not packing hours. Pay a helper for one shift per drop. Move overflow to a 5×10 storage unit. Speed hack: pre-make wax seals on the string/tag in batches during downtime instead of melting wax per order — cuts pack time roughly in half without cheapening it.
Phase 3 — 200+/drop. Standard 3PLs will not do wax. Options: (a) boutique kitting 3PL, $3–5/order pick-pack plus receiving; (b) keep it in-house as a brand feature. Online Ceramics self-fulfilled far longer than anyone expects — the hand is the moat.

## Shipping policy (recommended)
- Flat $6 US. Covers Ground Advantage on a 1 lb parcel in most zones; slight loss on far zones is acceptable.
- No free-shipping threshold at launch. Free shipping is a discount, and the estate does not negotiate. Charge the six dollars and say nothing about it.
- US only to start. Customs forms + wax + international loss rates = pain we don't need. Canada later.
- Print-to-order timing already protects cash: collect → print → ship. Zero inventory risk. Keep it.

# 📚 Research & Benchmarks — The Outside Numbers
Researched July 2026. Real industry data, not assumptions. This section corrects two things we had wrong.

## ⚠️ Correction 1: the 24-cap sits on the worst part of the print cost curve
Screen printing is a setup-heavy process — burning screens, mixing ink, registering the press takes the same labor whether you run 24 shirts or 240. Published 2026 pricing, all-in (blank + print):
  | Quantity | Per shirt, 1 color |
  | 12–24 | $15–$22 |
  | 25–49 | $10–$16 |
  | 50–99 | $8–$12 |
  | 100–249 | $6–$10 |
Setup runs $30–$40 per color per print location, charged once per order. Most shops won't take orders under 24–72 pieces at all. At exactly 24 units we're paying near the top of the curve — our earlier $13–19 COGS estimate was optimistic; the honest number at a 24-run is closer to $18–$25 landed once packaging, processing, and shipping are added. Against $40 retail that's a thin 40–55%.
Three fixes, in order of preference:
1. Raise the cap to 48 on hero lots. Jumping 24 → 48 cuts per-shirt cost roughly 35% and keeps scarcity intact. "Edition of 48" is still scarce.
1. Use DTF for lots under 24. DTF has no screen setup and is cheaper below the 24–48 crossover. Quality on white-ink-over-black is now strong. Screen print above 24, DTF below — that's the operating rule.
1. Ask Chadd whether screens are kept on file. Many shops store them indefinitely, making reorders zero setup. If ours does, chapter reissues and "the estate reopened one lot" become economically free.

## ⚠️ Correction 2: this is a traffic problem, not a product problem
Apparel converts at 2–3%; the Shopify-wide average is 1.4%, with the top 20% above 3.2%. Mobile is ~84% of traffic and converts lower than desktop.
So the math nobody had run: selling all 192 units (8 lots × 24) needs roughly 10,000–14,000 store sessions inside the drop window. That is a large number for a brand with no audience yet.
Honest expectation: chapter 001 will not sell out. That's fine — but plan around it. Recommendation: cut chapter 001 to 3 lots. 72 units needs ~3,600 sessions, which one strong TikTok can deliver. Sell 3 lots out completely rather than 8 lots partially — sellouts create the lore; partial sales create dead stock and wasted screen setups.

## ✅ Validated: the guestbook / download strategy is the right engine
Traffic-source conversion is wildly uneven:
- Email traffic: 4.0–5.3%
- Organic search: 2.7–3.0%
- Paid social: 0.7–1.2% (the worst)
- Returning customers convert 4.5–6.0% vs first-timers at 1.0–2.0%
And within email, automated flows are ~5% of sends but ~41% of email revenue, with roughly 18× the revenue per recipient of one-off campaigns. Flow placed-order rate averages 2.11% (top 10%: 4.3%) against 0.16% for campaigns.
What this means for us: the relic-pack email gate isn't a nice extra — it's the highest-converting acquisition channel available, and it costs nothing to fulfill. Build the welcome flow before spending a dollar on ads. Sequence: sign the guestbook → wallpaper delivered → lore email → chapter announcement. Paid social converting at 1% is the argument for organic clipping over ad spend at this stage.

## ⚖️ Trademark research — two findings that matter
1. A decorative front print does not establish trademark use. The USPTO requires the mark to function as a source identifier — on labels, hangtags, or packaging, not as art across the chest. Our estate file (folder, stamps, numbered card, wax) is exactly the right specimen. Put BANDERSNATCH on the neck label and the hangtag — it's both brand-correct and the thing that makes the filing work.
1. Class 25 is the most crowded class in the system — ~38,000 applications a year, roughly 15% of all US trademark filings. Searches surfaced no live BANDERSNATCH apparel registration (a 1989 "Bandersnatch Brew Pub" mark went abandoned in 1990), but an active entity named Bandersnatch Group, Inc. exists and has filed class-25 goods under other marks. Not disqualifying — but it's the exact kind of thing a knockout search must clear. Send both findings to Clare before any brass die or blank order.

## 🎯 Benchmark brand: Online Ceramics
The closest business analog, and the model confirms our instincts: limited drops with no guaranteed restocks, no paid ads and no countdown timers — organic posts and cultural momentum only, strange inserts in the packaging, and collabs that expand the universe without breaking tone. Their scarcity reads as a small art collective rather than a factory. That is the exact register we're aiming at — and they got there without buying traffic.
